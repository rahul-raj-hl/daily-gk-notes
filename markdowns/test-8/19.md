# 📌 Karla Caves & Buddhist Rock-Cut Architecture

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Location**: Located in Karli near **Lonavala, Maharashtra** (along the ancient trade route connecting the Deccan plateau to West Coast ports).
- **Architectural Highlight**: Contains the **largest and best-preserved Chaityagriha** (prayer hall) in India, dating from the **2nd century BCE to 5th century CE**.
- **Sect Association**: Associated primarily with the **Mahasamghika** sect of Hinayana Buddhism.
- **Key Features**: Teakwood roof ribs intact after 2,000 years, grand entrance pillars topped with lion capitals, and detailed carvings of *Mithuna* (coupled) figures.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused Concepts

| Structural Term | Definition / Purpose | Distinctive Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chaityagriha** | Congregational prayer hall containing a Stupa at the apsidal end. | Long barrel-vaulted roof with pillars dividing central nave and aisles. |
| **Vihara** | Living quarters/residence for Buddhist monks. | Central hall surrounded by small square cell chambers. |
| **Stupa** | Hemispherical mound containing relics of the Buddha or senior monks. | Object of circumambulation (*Pradakshina*) inside a Chaitya. |

- [ ] Ajanta Caves

- [ ] Elephanta Caves

> **Explanation**: The **Barabar Caves** in Bihar (Jehanabad district) are the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India, carved during the Mauryan era (3rd Century BCE) primarily for the Ajivika sect.

### PYQ 5

In the context of ancient Indian rock-cut architecture, the term 'Mithuna' depicted in the relief sculptures of Karla Caves refers to:

- [ ] Divine celestials playing musical instruments
