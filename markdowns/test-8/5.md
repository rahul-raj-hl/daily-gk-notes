# 📌 1991 Economic Reforms (LPG Policy)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Context of Crisis (1991)**: India faced a severe **Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis**. Foreign exchange reserves plunged to barely **2 weeks of imports**, while peak annual inflation soared to **16.7%** (August 1991).
- **Rao-Manmohan Model**: Adopted by the P. V. Narasimha Rao government with Dr. Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister under IMF/World Bank structural adjustment conditions.
- **Three Pillars**:
  1. **Liberalisation**: Removing bureaucratic controls, licensing restrictions, and trade barriers.
  2. **Privatisation**: Reducing the role of the public sector (disinvestment) and encouraging private enterprise.
  3. **Globalisation**: Integrating the domestic economy with the world economy via capital inflows and trade policy.

---

## 📖 LPG Reforms Matrix

### 1. Classification of Reform Measures

| Reform Pillar | Macroeconomic / Structural Focus | Key Specific Measures Taken |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liberalisation** | **Industrial & Tax Reforms** | Abolition of Industrial Licensing (except 6 hazardous/strategic industries); relaxation of MRTP Act limit; devaluation of the Indian Rupee (July 1991). |
| **Privatisation** | **Public Sector Reforms** | Dereservation of industries reserved for PSU (reduced from 17 to 2: Atomic Energy & Railway operations); **Disinvestment**; Navratna/Maharatna status. |
| **Globalisation** | **Trade & Foreign Investment** | Automatic approval for FDI up to 51% in high-priority sectors; removal of Quantitative Restrictions (QRs) on imports; Partial convertibility of Rupee on Current Account (LERMS). |

---

### 2. Reform Framework (IMF / World Bank Guidance)

| Category | Objective | Primary Focus Areas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Macroeconomic Stabilisation** | Short-term demand management | Controlling high inflation, BoP deficit correction, currency devaluation. |
| **Structural Adjustment** | Long-term supply-side reforms | Trade reforms, financial sector reforms, tax reforms, privatization, dereservation. |

---

## 🧠 Exam Booster

| Strategic Point | Fact / Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **New Industrial Policy Date** | Announced on **July 24, 1991** by Minister of State for Industry, P. J. Kurien. |
| **Current Account Convertibility** | Full convertibility on the **Current Account** was established in **August 1994** (Article VIII of IMF). Capital Account remains partially convertible. |
| **MRTP Act Replacement** | Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices (MRTP) Act 1969 was replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002** (Competition Commission of India). |
| **Safety Net Creation** | **National Renewal Fund (NRF)** was set up in 1992 to provide safety nets/retraining for workers affected by industrial restructuring. |

---

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Concept | Key Distinguishing Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Current Account Convertibility** | **Fully Convertible** in India since 1994 (for trade in goods, services, and remittances). |
| **Capital Account Convertibility** | **Partially Convertible** in India (controls exist on asset movement, foreign property purchase, and foreign loans). |
| **Disinvestment vs Privatisation** | Disinvestment is selling minority government stake ($\le 50\%$); Privatisation transfers majority ownership/control ($>50\%$) to private hands. |
