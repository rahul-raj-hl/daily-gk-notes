# 📌 Mohiniyattam & Historical References in Texts

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Vyavaharamala Text**: The classical dance form **Mohiniyattam** is explicitly mentioned in the 1709 text *Vyavaharamala* written by **Mazhamagalam Narayanan Namputiri**.
- **State of Origin**: Native to **Kerala**.
- **Literary References**: Also referenced in the poem *Ghoshayatra* by poet **Kunjan Nambiar**.
- **Meaning**: Named after **Mohini**, the feminine enchantress avatar of Lord Vishnu.
- **Style Features**: Performed solo by female dancers characterized by graceful, swaying body movements and white-and-gold (*Kasavu*) attire.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Mohiniyattam & Literary Sources

*Historical texts mentioning Kerala's classical dance.*

- **Vyavaharamala (1709 AD)**: Composed by **Mazhamagalam Narayanan Namputiri**; contains one of the earliest direct written references to Mohiniyattam.
- **Ghoshayatra**: A famous poem by 18th-century Malayalam poet **Kunjan Nambiar** that documents Mohiniyattam performances.
- **Royal Patronage**: Revived and structured extensively in the 19th century by **Maharaja Swathi Thirunal** of Travancore.
- **20th-Century Revival**: Revivalist effort led by nationalist poet **Vallathol Narayana Menon**, who founded the **Kerala Kalamandalam** in 1930.

---

### 2. Overview of Other Classical Dance Forms in Options

*Important details on Sattriya, Manipuri, and Odissi.*

- **Sattriya (Assam)**:
  - Introduced in the 15th century AD by the Vaishnavite saint **Srimanta Sankardev**.
  - Originates from the *Sattras* (monasteries) and includes one-act plays called **Ankiya Nat** performed in **Bhaona**.
- **Manipuri (Manipur)**:
  - Centered on the **Madhura Raas** of Radha and Krishna.
  - Characterized by soft, fluid, and serene body movements without sharp jerks.
- **Odissi (Odisha)**:
  - Originated in the temples of Odisha, traditionally performed by **Maharis** (temple dancers).
  - Key posture includes the **Tribhanga** (three-bend stance) and *Chowk*.
