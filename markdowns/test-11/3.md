# 📌 Amendment of the Indian Constitution (Article 368)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Article & Part**: The amendment procedure is contained in **Article 368** under **Part XX** of the Constitution.
- **Source**: Borrowed from the **Constitution of South Africa**.
- **State Consent**: Consent of states is **NOT mandatory for all amendments**, only for those affecting the federal structure (requires ratification by at least half of the state legislatures).
- **No Joint Sitting**: There is **no provision for a joint sitting** of both Houses in case of a deadlock over a Constitutional Amendment Bill.
- **Prior Permission**: Does **NOT require prior permission** of the President to be introduced in Parliament.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Statement Analysis

- **Statement 1: The Amendment procedure has been provided in Article 368 of the Constitution of India.**
  - *Correct.* Article 368 in Part XX deals specifically with the power of Parliament to amend the Constitution and procedure therefor.
- **Statement 2: The consent of the States is mandatory for all Amendments to the Constitution of India.**
  - *Incorrect.* State consent is required **only** for provisions related to the **federal structure** (ratification by simple majority of at least 50% of states). Most provisions can be amended by Parliament alone.

---

### 2. Three Types of Amendments in India

*Methods used to amend different parts of the Constitution.*

- **By Simple Majority of Parliament**:
  - Out of the scope of Article 368.
  - Examples: Admission/formation of new states (Art. 2, 3), creation/abolition of legislative councils in states (Art. 169), official language usage, citizenship rules.
- **By Special Majority of Parliament (Under Article 368)**:
  - Requires a majority of the total membership of each House AND a $2/3\text{rd}$ majority of members present and voting.
  - Examples: Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP).
- **By Special Majority + State Ratification (Under Article 368)**:
  - Requires Special Majority in Parliament + ratification by **at least 50% of State Legislatures** by simple majority.
  - Examples: Election of President, representation of states in Parliament, Supreme Court & High Courts powers, distribution of legislative powers (7th Schedule), Article 368 itself.
