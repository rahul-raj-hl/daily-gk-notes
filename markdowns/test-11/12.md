# 📌 Fundamental Duties & 86th Constitutional Amendment Act

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Eleventh Fundamental Duty**: Added by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002**.
- **Duty Provision**: Mandates parents or guardians to provide educational opportunities to their child or ward aged **6 to 14 years** under **Article 51A(k)**.
- **Original Inclusion**: Fundamental Duties were added to Part IVA (Article 51A) by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**.
- **Initial Count**: Initially, there were **10 Fundamental Duties**; the 86th Amendment brought the total to **11**.
- **Right to Education (RTE)**: The 86th Amendment also introduced **Article 21A** (Fundamental Right to Education) and modified **Article 45** (DPSP).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002

*A landmark amendment revolutionizing education in three distinct parts of the Constitution.*

- **Fundamental Rights (Article 21A)**: Made free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 years a Fundamental Right.
- **Directive Principles of State Policy (Article 45)**: Substituted Article 45 to direct the State to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete 6 years of age.
- **Fundamental Duties (Article 51A(k))**: Added the **11th Fundamental Duty** making it the duty of every citizen who is a parent/guardian to provide education opportunities to their child between 6 and 14 years.

---

### 2. Overview of Fundamental Duties in India

*Key Constitutional aspects and features.*

- **Part & Article**: **Part IVA**, **Article 51A**.
- **Borrowed From**: **USSR** (now Russia) Constitution.
- **Committee Recommendation**: Recommended by the **Swaran Singh Committee** (1976).
- **Nature**: **Non-justiciable** in nature (citizens cannot be legally compelled or prosecuted directly in court solely for non-performance of duties, though Parliament can enact specific laws to enforce them).

| **Fundamental Duty (FD)** | **Article 51A(k)** $\rightarrow$ Parent's duty to educate child (6-14 yrs) |
