# 📌 Terracotta Art & Post-Mauryan Sunga Art

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Peak Patronage**: Terracotta art executed during the pre-Mauryan and Mauryan periods was redefined at its best under the **Sunga Dynasty**.
- **Mould-Made Plaques**: The Sunga era introduced **mould-made terracotta plaques** in cities across the Gangetic Valley.
- **Major Center**: **Chandraketugarh** in West Bengal emerged as one of the most prominent hubs for Sunga terracotta art.
- **Time Period**: Sunga terracotta art is dated primarily from the **beginning of the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century BCE**.
- **Founder**: **Pushyamitra Sunga** founded the Sunga Dynasty after overthrowing Brihadratha (the last Mauryan king) in 185 BCE.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Features of Sunga Terracotta Art

*Unique elements of post-Mauryan clay art.*

- **Production Technique**: Shifted from hand-modeled figurines to mass-produced **single-mould terracotta plaques**.
- **Key Themes**: Depicted secular life, Yakshas, Yakshinis, royal processions, erotic couples (*Mithuna*), and nature motifs reflecting popular social preferences.
- **Major Locations**:
  - **Chandraketugarh** (West Bengal)
  - **Tamluk** (West Bengal)
  - **Mathura**, **Kausambi**, **Ahichchatra** (Uttar Pradesh)
  - **Patna**, **Vaisali**, **Buxar** (Bihar)

---

### 2. Comparison of Art Traditions Across Dynasties

*Distinct artistic milestones across major ancient Indian dynasties.*

- **Mauryan Art**:
  - Famous for monumental stone architecture, polished monolithic pillars (e.g., Sarnath Lion Capital), and rock-cut caves (Barabar Caves).
- **Sunga Art**:
  - Known for stone gateway carvings (Toranas at Sanchi Stupa) and refined **mould-made terracotta plaques**.
- **Kushan Art**:
  - Famous for stone sculpture schools: **Gandhara School** (Greco-Roman influence) and **Mathura School** (indigenous style).
- **Gupta Art**:
  - Known as the *Golden Age of Indian Art*; created sacred Hindu and Jain temple imagery using stone, metal, and terracotta plaques (e.g., Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh).
