# 📌 Food Fortification

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- Food fortification is the addition of essential vitamins and minerals to food.
- It helps prevent micronutrient deficiencies and improves nutritional quality.
- It is a cost-effective public health intervention.
- In India, food fortification is promoted by FSSAI.

---

## 🔑 Key Points

### 🥛 What is Food Fortification?

*One-liner:* Addition of essential micronutrients to commonly consumed foods.

- Improves nutritional value.
- Prevents deficiency diseases.
- Helps combat hidden hunger.
- Does not change the taste or appearance of food.

---

## 🧪 Common Nutrients

| Nutrient | Benefit |
|----------|----------|
| Iron | Prevents anaemia |
| Iodine | Prevents goitre |
| Vitamin A | Healthy vision & immunity |
| Vitamin D | Strong bones |
| Zinc | Growth & immunity |
| Folic Acid | Prevents neural tube defects |

---

## 🍚 Commonly Fortified Foods

- Rice
- Wheat Flour (Atta)
- Milk
- Edible Oil
- Salt

---

## 🎯 Why is Fortification Needed?

- Hidden hunger (micronutrient deficiency)
- Nutrient loss during food processing
- Poor dietary intake
- Supports public nutrition programmes

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Process | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Fortification | Adds nutrients |
| Supplementation | Nutrients given separately (tablet/syrup) |
| Enrichment | Restores nutrients lost during processing |
