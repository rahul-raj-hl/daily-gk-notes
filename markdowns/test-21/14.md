# 📌 Ohm's Law: Voltage, Current, and Resistance Calculations

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Formula (Ohm's Law)**: States that at a constant temperature, the current ($I$) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference ($V$) applied across its ends:
  $$V = I \times R \implies R = \frac{V}{I}$$
- **Step-by-Step Calculation**:
  - Given Potential Difference ($V$) = $12\text{ V}$
  - Given Electric Current ($I$) = $2\text{ mA} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}$
  - Resistance ($R$) = $\frac{12}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{12000}{2} = \mathbf{6000\ \mathbf{\Omega}}\ (6\text{ k}\Omega)$
- **SI Unit of Resistance**: **Ohm ($\Omega$)** ($1\ \Omega = 1\text{ V / A}$).
- **Key Determinants of Resistance**: $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$, where resistance depends directly on the length ($l$) of the conductor, inversely on its area of cross-section ($A$), the nature of the material (resistivity $\rho$), and temperature.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Mathematical Breakdown of Ohm's Law

| Variable | Symbol | Given / Standard Value | Standard SI Unit | Unit Conversion Equivalent |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Potential Difference (Voltage)** | $V$ | $12\text{ V}$ | Volt ($\text{V}$) | $1\text{ V} = 1\text{ Joule / Coulomb}$ |
| **Electric Current** | $I$ | $2\text{ mA}$ | Ampere ($\text{A}$) | $1\text{ mA} = 10^{-3}\text{ A} = 0.002\text{ A}$ |
| **Electric Resistance** | $R$ | **$6000\ \Omega$** | Ohm ($\Omega$) | $1\text{ k}\Omega = 1000\ \Omega = 10^3\ \Omega$ |

$$R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{12\text{ V}}{2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}} = 6 \times 10^3\ \Omega = 6000\ \Omega$$

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Parameter | Correct Relationship | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unit Conversion ($\text{mA}$ to $\text{A}$)** | $2\text{ mA} = 0.002\text{ A}$ | Dividing $12$ by $2$ directly to get $6\ \Omega$ (forgetting the 'milli' prefix) |
| **Resistance vs. Resistivity** | • **Resistance ($R$)**: Depends on length & cross-sectional area<br>• **Resistivity ($\rho$)**: Independent of dimensions (material & temperature only) | Assuming resistivity changes when a wire is stretched or cut |
| **Ideal Measuring Devices** | • Ideal Ammeter $\rightarrow$ **Zero Resistance** ($R = 0$)<br>• Ideal Voltmeter $\rightarrow$ **Infinite Resistance** ($R = \infty$) | Swapping the resistance characteristics of ammeters and voltmeters |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "V OVER I EQUALS R, WATCH OUT FOR THE MILLI STAR!"**
> - **$V / I = R$** $\rightarrow$ Voltage divided by Current gives Resistance
> - **Milli Star** $\rightarrow$ Always convert **$\text{mA}$ to $10^{-3}\text{ A}$** before calculating to avoid the $6\ \Omega$ trap

- What is the SI unit of electrical resistivity? $\rightarrow$ **Ohm-metre ($\Omega\cdot\text{m}$)**

- If the length of a uniform conducting wire is doubled, what happens to its resistance? $\rightarrow$ **It gets doubled ($R \propto l$)**
- If the area of cross-section of a wire is doubled, its resistance becomes: $\rightarrow$ **Halved ($R \propto \frac{1}{A}$)**

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

A $12\text{ V}$ battery is connected across an unknown resistor. If a current of $2\text{ mA}$ is flowing through the circuit, the value of the resistance is:

- [ ] $600\ \Omega$
- [ ] $6\ \Omega$
- [x] $6000\ \Omega$
- [ ] $60\ \Omega$

> Applying Ohm's law, $R = \frac{V}{I}$. Substituting $V = 12\text{ V}$ and $I = 2\text{ mA} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}$ gives $R = \frac{12}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = 6000\ \Omega$.

---

### PYQ 2

Which of the following represents the correct mathematical expression for Ohm's Law?

- [ ] $V = I^2 R$
- [x] $V = I R$
- [ ] $I = V R$
- [ ] $R = V I$

> Ohm's Law states that the potential difference across a conductor is directly proportional to the current flowing through it, represented by the linear relation $V = IR$.

---

### PYQ 3

What is the SI unit of electrical resistivity of a metallic conductor?

- [ ] $\Omega / \text{m}$
- [ ] $\Omega / \text{m}^2$
- [x] $\Omega\cdot\text{m}$
- [ ] $\text{Siemens}$

> From the formula $R = \rho \frac{l}{A} \implies \rho = \frac{R \cdot A}{l}$, the unit is $\frac{\Omega \cdot \text{m}^2}{\text{m}} = \Omega\cdot\text{m}$ (Ohm-metre).

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

If a current of $2.5\text{ mA}$ flows through a component when connected to a $5\text{ V}$ power source, what is the component's electrical resistance?

- [ ] $2\ \Omega$
- [ ] $20\ \Omega$
- [ ] $200\ \Omega$
- [x] $2000\ \Omega$

> Using $R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{5\text{ V}}{2.5 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}} = 2000\ \Omega$ ($2\text{ k}\Omega$).

---

### Q2

An ideal voltmeter should have an internal resistance of:

- [ ] Zero
- [ ] $1\ \Omega$
- [ ] $100\ \Omega$
- [x] Infinity

> An ideal voltmeter must draw zero current from the branch it is measuring in parallel; therefore, its internal resistance must theoretically be infinite.

---

### Q3

If a cylindrical conducting wire of resistance $R$ is stretched to double its original length without altering its volume, its new resistance becomes:

- [ ] $2R$
- [x] $4R$
- [ ] $\frac{R}{2}$
- [ ] $\frac{R}{4}$

> When stretched to double its length ($l' = 2l$), its cross-sectional area is halved ($A' = A/2$) to keep volume constant. Thus, $R' = \rho \frac{2l}{A/2} = 4\left(\rho \frac{l}{A}\right) = 4R$.

---

### Q4

Which of the following devices is an example of a non-ohmic conductor (does NOT obey Ohm's Law)?

- [ ] Copper wire
- [ ] Nichrome resistor
- [x] Semiconductor Diode
- [ ] Aluminum strip

> Semiconductor devices like p-n junction diodes, transistors, and electrolytes have non-linear $V$-$I$ characteristics and do not obey Ohm's Law.
