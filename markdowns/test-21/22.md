# 📌 Origin of Indian Monsoon: Thermal Role of the Tibetan Plateau

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Tibetan Thermal Engine Concept**: Pioneering meteorologists **Dr. P. Koteswaram (India)** and **Hermann Flohn (Germany)** established the **thermal impact of the high Tibetan Plateau** on the origin and maintenance of the Indian Summer Monsoon.
- **The Mechanism**: During northern summer, the elevated Tibetan Plateau (average elevation $> 4,500\text{ m}$) absorbs intense solar insolation, acting as an **elevated heat engine (heat source)** relative to the surrounding mid-troposphere.
- **Atmospheric Circulation**: This intense sensible and latent heating creates a rising convective column of warm air over Tibet, forming an upper-tropospheric high-pressure anticyclone (**Tibetan Anticyclone**).
- **Upper-Air Jet Link**: The clockwise outflow from this high-pressure cell diverges southwards and accelerates over peninsular India as the **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** at $\approx 150\text{ hPa}$ ($\approx 12 - 14\text{ km}$ altitude).
- **Surface Convergence**: The descending branch of this circulation intensifies high pressure over the south-western Indian Ocean (near Mascarene Islands), which powerfully drives the **Southwest Monsoon winds** across the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal toward the Indian mainland.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Major Theories on the Origin of the Indian Monsoon

| Theory / Model | Proposed By | Core Driving Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Thermal Theory (Classical)** | **Sir Edmond Halley (1686)** | Differential seasonal heating and cooling between the vast Asian landmass and the Indian Ocean, behaving as a gigantic land-sea breeze. |
| **Dynamic Concept (ITCZ Shift)** | **Hermann Flohn (1951)** | Seasonal northward migration of planetary wind belts; the **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)** shifts over the Gangetic Plain (*Monsoon Trough*). |
| **Jet Stream Theory** | **M. T. Yin (1949)** | Abrupt northward shifting of the **Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream (SWJ)** to the north of the Himalayas triggers the sudden "burst" of the monsoon over India. |
| **Tibetan Thermal Engine & TEJ** | **P. Koteswaram & H. Flohn (1958)** | The elevated **Tibetan Plateau heats the upper troposphere**, establishing the **Tibetan Anticyclone** and the **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** over South Asia. |
| **Somali / Findlater Jet** | **J. Findlater (1969)** | Low-level cross-equatorial south-westerly jet off the coast of East Africa (Somalia) funneling moisture into the Indian subcontinent. |

### 2. The Dual Role of the Tibetan Plateau in Indian Climatology

| Role Category | Physical Function | Impact on Indian Weather & Climate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Mechanical / Ographic Barrier** | Acts as a high-altitude topographical wall (~$4.5\text{ km}$ high). | • Blocks freezing winter winds originating from Siberia/Central Asia.<br>• Forces the Subtropical Westerly Jet to split into northern and southern branches during winter. |
| **2. Thermal Engine (Summer)** | Acts as an elevated direct heat source for the mid-troposphere. | • Warms the air $2^\circ\text{C} - 3^\circ\text{C}$ higher than adjacent regions at the same altitude.<br>• Creates an upper-level divergent outflow that powers the Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ). |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Atmospheric Feature | Altitude / Atmospheric Layer | Direction & Season | Impact on Indian Monsoon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subtropical Westerly Jet (SWJ)** | Upper Troposphere ($\approx 9 - 12\text{ km}$) | **West to East** (Winter & Pre-monsoon) | Must shift **north of the Himalayas** for the Southwest Monsoon to arrive. |
| **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** | High Upper Troposphere ($\approx 13 - 15\text{ km}$) | **East to West** (Summer only) | Its formation and strength **directly correlate with a strong monsoon**. |
| **Somali Jet (Findlater Jet)** | Low-level Troposphere ($\approx 1.5\text{ km}$) | **Southwest to Northeast** (Summer) | Transports massive maritime moisture from the Indian Ocean to India's west coast. |
