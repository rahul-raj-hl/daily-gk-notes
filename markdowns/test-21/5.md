# 📌 Spherical Mirrors: Image Formation, Characteristics & Ray Optics

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Convex Mirror Characteristics**: A **convex mirror (diverging mirror)** *always* forms a **virtual, erect, and diminished image** behind the mirror, irrespective of where the object is placed in front of it.
- **Object at Infinity in a Convex Mirror**: When an object is at infinity, the image is formed **behind the mirror at the focus ($F$)** and is **virtual, erect, and highly diminished (point-sized)**.
- **Concave Mirror Exception**: A **concave mirror (converging mirror)** forms real and inverted images for most object positions. It forms a **virtual and erect image** in *only one specific case*: when the object is placed **between the Pole ($P$) and Focus ($F$)**, and this image is always **magnified/enlarged**, *never diminished*.
- **Definition of Virtual Image**: An image formed when reflected rays only *appear* to diverge from behind the mirror surface; it **cannot be obtained on a screen**.
- **Common Applications**:
  - **Convex Mirrors**: Used as **rear-view mirrors in vehicles** and **blind-spot safety mirrors** because they offer a much wider field of view and consistently form erect, diminished images.
  - **Concave Mirrors**: Used as **shaving/makeup mirrors**, **dentist mirrors**, and **solar concentrators/headlights** because they produce an enlarged, erect image close up or focus parallel rays at the focal point.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Image Formation by a Convex Mirror

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Relative Size |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **At Infinity** | **At the focus ($F$)**, behind the mirror | **Virtual and Erect** | **Highly diminished (point-sized)** |
| **Between Infinity and Pole ($P$)** | **Between $P$ and $F$**, behind the mirror | **Virtual and Erect** | **Diminished** |

---

### 2. Image Formation by a Concave Mirror (All Positions)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Relative Size | Sign of Magnification ($m$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **At Infinity** | **At Focus ($F$)** | Real and Inverted | Highly diminished (point-sized) | $m < 0$, $|m| \ll 1$ |
| **Beyond $C$** | Between $F$ and $C$ | Real and Inverted | Diminished | $m < 0$, $|m| < 1$ |
| **At Center of Curvature ($C$)** | **At $C$** | Real and Inverted | **Same size** as object | $m = -1$ |
| **Between $C$ and $F$** | Beyond $C$ | Real and Inverted | Enlarged (Magnified) | $m < 0$, $|m| > 1$ |
| **At Focus ($F$)** | **At Infinity** | Real and Inverted | Highly enlarged | $m < 0$, $|m| \gg 1$ |
| **Between Pole ($P$) & Focus ($F$)** | **Behind the mirror** | **Virtual and Erect** | **Magnified (Enlarged)** | $m > 0$, $|m| > 1$ |

---

### 3. Key Optical Formulas & Sign Conventions (Cartesian System)

| Parameter / Formula | Mathematical Expression | Key Sign Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mirror Formula** | $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$$ | • Object distance ($u$) is **always negative ($-$)**.<br>• Focal length ($f$) is **negative ($-$) for Concave** and **positive ($+$) for Convex**.<br>• Virtual image distance ($v$) is **positive ($+$)**; Real image ($v$) is **negative ($-$)**. |
| **Linear Magnification ($m$)** | $$m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h_i}{h_o}$$ | • $m > 0 \rightarrow$ Virtual & Erect.<br>• $m < 0 \rightarrow$ Real & Inverted.<br>• $|m| < 1 \rightarrow$ Diminished; $|m| > 1 \rightarrow$ Magnified. |
| **Radius of Curvature ($R$)** | $$R = 2f$$ | Radius of curvature is twice the focal length. |

| **Always Virtual, Erect & Diminished** | **Convex Mirror** | Universal behavior for all real object distances |

| **Focal Length Sign** | **Concave = Negative ($-$)** \| **Convex = Positive ($+$)** | Crucial for numerical problems |
| **Vehicle Rear-View Mirror** | **Convex Mirror** | Gives a wider field of view |
| **Headlights / Torches Reflector** | **Concave Mirror** (Bulb placed at focus) | Produces a powerful parallel beam |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Optical Condition | Mirror Type Responsible | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Virtual and Diminished** | **Convex Mirror** (Always) | Choosing concave mirror by mistake |
| **Virtual and Magnified** | **Concave Mirror** (Object between $P$ & $F$) | Assuming concave mirrors can never form virtual images |
| **Real and Diminished** | **Concave Mirror** (Object beyond $C$ or at $\infty$) | Assuming convex mirrors form real images |
| **Same size, Virtual and Erect** | **Plane Mirror** ($m = +1$) | Confusing plane mirror with convex mirror |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

If the linear magnification produced by a spherical mirror is $m = +0.4$, which type of mirror is it and what is the nature of the image?

- [ ] Concave mirror, real and magnified image
- [x] Convex mirror, virtual and diminished image
- [ ] Concave mirror, virtual and magnified image
- [ ] Plane mirror, virtual and same-sized image

> A positive magnification ($m > 0$) signifies a virtual and erect image, while $|m| = 0.4 < 1$ indicates that the image is diminished. Only a convex mirror can produce a virtual and diminished image ($m < +1$).

---

### Q2

At what position in front of a concave mirror should an object be placed so that the size of the real image formed is equal to the size of the object?

- [ ] At the Focus ($F$)
- [ ] Between $F$ and $C$
- [x] At the Center of Curvature ($C$)
- [ ] Beyond $C$

> Placing an object at the center of curvature ($C$) of a concave mirror produces a real, inverted image at $C$ with a magnification of $m = -1$ (same size).

---

### Q3

Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding a virtual image formed by a mirror?

- [ ] It cannot be captured on a screen
- [ ] It is formed where reflected rays appear to diverge from a point behind the mirror
- [x] It is always formed in front of the reflecting surface on the same side as the object
- [ ] It is always erect with respect to the object

> Virtual images in mirrors are formed *behind* the reflecting surface where reflected rays appear to meet; real images are formed in front of the mirror on the same side as the object.

### Q4

Dentists use small handheld mirrors to view enlarged images of patients' teeth. What type of mirror is used and where is the tooth positioned relative to the mirror?

- [ ] Convex mirror, tooth at infinity
- [ ] Plane mirror, tooth at center of curvature
- [x] Concave mirror, tooth placed between the pole and principal focus
- [ ] Concave mirror, tooth placed beyond the center of curvature

> Dentists use concave mirrors and hold them close so that the tooth lies between the pole ($P$) and focus ($F$), producing an erect, magnified virtual image.
