# 📌 Directive Principles of State Policy: "Novel Feature" & Key Opinions

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **"Novel Feature" Statement**: **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (Chairman of the Drafting Committee) described the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) as a **"novel feature"** of the Indian Constitution.
- **Constitutional Location**: Enshrined in **Part IV (Articles 36 to 51)** of the Constitution of India.
- **Source of Borrowing**: Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution of 1937** (which had originally derived them from the Spanish Constitution).
- **Core Objective**: To establish **Socio-Economic Democracy** and realize a **"Welfare State"**, in contrast to Fundamental Rights (Part III) which primarily secure *Political Democracy*.
- **Nature of Enforceability**: Under **Article 37**, DPSPs are **non-justiciable** (not enforceable by any court of law), yet declared **"fundamental in the governance of the country"**, making it the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws.
- **Historical Precursor**: Resembles the **"Instrument of Instructions"** issued to the Governor-General and Governors under the **Government of India Act, 1935**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Famous Constitutional Quotes on DPSP

| Constitutional Scholar / Leader | Famous Quotation / Description of DPSP | Core Perspective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** | **"A novel feature of the Indian Constitution"** | Emphasized their role in providing socio-economic democracy. |
| **K. T. Shah** | **"A cheque on a bank, payable only when the resources of the bank permit"** | Criticized their non-justiciable / non-binding legal character. |
| **Granville Austin** | DPSP and Fundamental Rights constitute the **"Conscience of the Constitution"** | Highlighted their moral and philosophical foundation. |
| **Sir Ivor Jennings** | **"Pious aspirations"** and **"Fabian Socialism without the socialism"** | Criticized the lack of immediate legal teeth and enforceability. |
| **K. C. Wheare** | **"A manifesto of aims and aspirations"** | Viewed DPSP as political and moral benchmarks for governments. |
| **L. M. Singhvi** | **"Life-giving provisions of the Constitution"** | Highlighted their role in social revolution and welfare governance. |

### 2. Broad Classification of Directive Principles (Part IV)

| Ideological Category | Core Philosophical Objective | Representative Articles |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Socialistic Principles** | Direct the state toward eliminating socioeconomic inequalities and ensuring equitable wealth distribution. | • **Art 38**: Promote social order & minimize inequalities<br>• **Art 39**: Adequate livelihood & equal distribution of wealth<br>• **Art 39A**: Equal justice and free legal aid<br>• **Art 41**: Right to work, education & public assistance<br>• **Art 42**: Humane work conditions & maternity relief<br>• **Art 43/43A**: Living wage & workers' participation in management |
| **Gandhian Principles** | Based on Mahatma Gandhi's vision of rural reconstruction, swadeshi, and village autonomy. | • **Art 40**: Organization of Village Panchayats<br>• **Art 43**: Promotion of cottage industries<br>• **Art 43B**: Promotion of Cooperative Societies (*97th CAA*)<br>• **Art 46**: Educational/economic interests of SCs, STs & OBCs<br>• **Art 47**: Prohibition of intoxicating drinks/drugs<br>• **Art 48**: Prohibition of slaughter of cows, calves & milch cattle |
| **Liberal-Intellectual Principles** | Embody the ideology of modern liberalism, global peace, and administrative rule of law. | • **Art 44**: Uniform Civil Code (UCC)<br>• **Art 45**: Early childhood care and education ($< 6$ years)<br>• **Art 48A**: Protection of environment, forests & wildlife<br>• **Art 49**: Protection of monuments & historical places<br>• **Art 50**: Separation of Judiciary from the Executive<br>• **Art 51**: Promotion of international peace and security |
