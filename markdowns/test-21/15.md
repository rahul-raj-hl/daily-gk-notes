# 📌 Fundamental Rights: Constitutional Protection of Third Gender Rights (Articles 14 & 21)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Governing Constitutional Articles**: The non-discrimination and fundamental rights of persons belonging to the third gender / transgender community are anchored primarily in **Article 14** (Right to Equality) and **Article 21** (Right to Life, Dignity, Personal Liberty, and Privacy).
- **Article 14 Application**: Transgender persons fall squarely within the constitutional expression **"person"** under Article 14, guaranteeing them **equality before the law and equal protection of the laws**, and prohibiting arbitrary discrimination by the State.
- **Article 21 Application**: Guarantees the right to live with **human dignity, self-determination of gender identity, personal autonomy, and privacy**, affirming that life is not merely physical existence.
- **NALSA Judgment (2014)**: In *National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) v. Union of India*, the Supreme Court legally recognized transgender individuals as the **"Third Gender"** and affirmed their right to self-identify their gender.
- **Navtej Singh Johar Judgment (2018)**: A five-judge Constitution Bench decriminalized consensual same-sex relations by reading down **Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)**, upholding **constitutional morality** over societal/majoritarian morality.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Constitutional Foundations Protecting Third Gender / LGBTQ+ Rights

| Constitutional Provision | Core Guarantee | Application to Third Gender Rights |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Article 14** | **Equality before law & Equal protection of the laws** | • Prohibits arbitrary State action.<br>• The term *"person"* applies universally regardless of gender binary (male/female).<br>• Guarantees equal civil, citizenship, and socio-economic rights. |
| **Article 15(1) & 15(2)** | **Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of sex, race, caste, etc.** | • The word **"sex"** includes **gender identity and sexual orientation**.<br>• Bars discrimination in public employment, public spaces, and institutions. |
| **Article 19(1)(a)** | **Freedom of Speech and Expression** | • Protects the freedom of **gender expression** through dress, speech, mannerisms, and chosen identity. |
| **Article 21** | **Protection of Life and Personal Liberty** | • Encompasses the right to **live with human dignity**.<br>• Encompasses the **Right to Privacy** (*Puttaswamy Case, 2017*).<br>• Encompasses bodily and psychological **decisional autonomy**. |

---

### 2. Landmark Supreme Court Judgments Matrix

| Landmark Case | Year | Key Ruling & Constitutional Principle Established |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **NALSA v. Union of India** | **2014** | • Legally recognized transgender persons as the **"Third Gender"**.<br>• Upheld the fundamental right to self-determine one's gender identity under **Articles 14, 19(1)(a), and 21**.<br>• Directed Centre/States to treat them as socially and educationally backward classes. |
| **K. S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India** | **2017** | • Unanimously declared the **Right to Privacy** an intrinsic part of **Article 21**.<br>• Affirmed that privacy includes individual autonomy over identity and intimate choices. |
| **Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India** | **2018** | • Read down **Section 377 IPC**, decriminalizing consensual homosexual acts among adults.<br>• Established the supremacy of **Constitutional Morality** over popular/public morality. |
| **Supriyo v. Union of India** | **2023** | • Acknowledged queer couples' right to cohabit without discrimination; held that the right to marry is not an unqualified fundamental right and falls within legislative competence. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Constitutional Right / Case | Exact Scope | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NALSA Case (2014) vs. Navtej Johar Case (2018)** | • **NALSA**: Third gender legal identity under Arts 14 & 21<br>• **Navtej Johar**: Decriminalization of Sec 377 IPC | Confusing third gender identity recognition with Section 377 decriminalization |
| **Article 14 vs. Article 15** | • **Art 14**: Universal equality for all *"persons"*<br>• **Art 15**: Specific non-discrimination for *"citizens"* | Forgetting Article 14 applies to all "persons" (including non-binary individuals) |
| **Constitutional Morality vs. Public Morality** | Courts must enforce **Constitutional Morality**, not popular majority sentiments | Assuming laws are judged by traditional public morality |
