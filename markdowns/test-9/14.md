# 📌 British Land Revenue Systems: Ryotwari, Zamindari & Mahalwari

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Statement A is True**: The **Ryotwari System** established a direct link between the British state and the individual peasant (*ryot*), eliminating traditional intermediaries.
- **Statement B is False**: The **village headman (*Lambardar*)** was used for revenue collection in the **Mahalwari System**, *not* the Ryotwari System.
- **Pioneers of Ryotwari**: Introduced on a trial basis by **Captain Alexander Read** in Baramahal (1792) and extended across Madras Presidency by **Sir Thomas Munro** (1820).

## 📖 Comparison of British Land Revenue Systems

| Feature | Zamindari (Permanent Settlement) | Ryotwari System | Mahalwari System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Introduced By** | Lord Cornwallis (1793) | Thomas Munro & Alexander Read (1820) | Holt Mackenzie (1822) & William Bentinck (1833) |
| **Primary Regions** | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Varanasi | Madras, Bombay, parts of Assam & Coorg | NWFP, Punjab, Awadh, Central Provinces |
| **Land Coverage** | ~19% of British India | **~51% of British India** (Largest share) | ~30% of British India |
| **Intermediary** | Zamindar (Landlord) | **None** (Direct settlement with the *Ryot*) | Village Headman (*Lambardar*) / Village Community |
| **Revenue Nature** | Fixed permanently | Revised periodically (every 20–30 years) | Revised periodically |
| **Ownership Rights** | Given to Zamindars | Given to Individual Cultivators (*Ryots*) | Held collectively by the Village (*Mahal*) |

## 🧠 Exam Booster

| System | Key Term / Unique Characteristic | High-Yield Point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sunset Law (1794)** | Associated with **Permanent Settlement** | If a Zamindar failed to pay revenue by sunset of a specified date, their estate was auctioned. |
| **51% Coverage** | **Ryotwari System** | Covered the largest total area among all three revenue systems in colonial India. |
| **Mahal** | Associated with **Mahalwari System** | Replaced individual assessment with collective village/unit (*Mahal*) assessment. |
| **High Revenue Rates** | **Ryotwari Assessment** | Revenue rates were exorbitantly high (up to 50%–60% of produce), leading to widespread peasant debt and moneylender exploitation. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused Concepts

| Concept | Correct Detail | Common Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Village Headman Role** | **Mahalwari System** used the headman (*Lambardar*) to collect tax. | Believing Ryotwari used village headmen; Ryotwari was a *direct* individual-taxpayer model. |
| **Territorial Reach** | **Ryotwari** = South & West India (Madras/Bombay); **Zamindari** = East India (Bengal/Bihar); **Mahalwari** = North & NW India (Punjab/UP). | Swapping regions between Ryotwari and Mahalwari in matching questions. |
| **Permanence of Tax** | Only the **Zamindari System (1793)** had a fixed/permanent revenue rate; Ryotwari and Mahalwari rates were revised periodically. | Assuming tax rates were permanent across all three colonial systems. |

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

Who among the following introduced the Ryotwari System in the Madras Presidency?

- [ ] Lord Cornwallis
- [x] Sir Thomas Munro
- [ ] Warren Hastings
- [ ] Lord Dalhousie

> **Explanation**: Sir Thomas Munro, along with Captain Alexander Read, formulated and extended the Ryotwari System across the Madras Presidency starting around 1820.

---

### PYQ 2

Under which of the following land revenue systems was the revenue assessed collectively on the entire village (*Mahal*) through the village headman?

- [ ] Permanent Settlement
- [ ] Ryotwari System
- [x] Mahalwari System
- [ ] Dahsala System

> **Explanation**: The Mahalwari System, drafted by Holt Mackenzie in 1822, collected revenue from a village unit (*Mahal*) through the village headman (*Lambardar*).

---

### PYQ 3

Which land revenue system covered the largest area of British India (~51%)?

- [ ] Permanent Settlement
- [x] Ryotwari System
- [ ] Mahalwari System
- [ ] Malguzari System

> **Explanation**: The Ryotwari system covered roughly 51% of British territory, including Madras, Bombay, parts of Assam, and Coorg.

---

### PYQ 4

The 'Sunset Law' was associated with which of the following colonial revenue systems?

- [x] Permanent Settlement
- [ ] Ryotwari System
- [ ] Mahalwari System
- [ ] Malwar System

> **Explanation**: Under the Permanent Settlement (1793), if zamindars failed to pay their dues before sunset on the specified date, their land rights were confiscated and auctioned.
