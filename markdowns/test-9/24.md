# 📌 Foreign Travelers in Ancient India: Fa-Hien & Hiuen Tsang

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Primary Purpose**: Chinese pilgrim-scholars visited India to visit sacred Buddhist pilgrimage sites, study at universities like **Nalanda**, and collect authentic Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts to translate back in China.
- **Fa-Hien (Faxian)**: Visited during the 5th century CE under **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** of the **Gupta Dynasty**. Authored ***Fo-Kwo-Ki***.
- **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)**: Visited during the 7th century CE under **Harshavardhan** of the **Vardhana/Pushyabhuti Dynasty**. Authored ***Si-Yu-Ki***. Known as the **"Prince of Pilgrims"**.

---

## 📖 Key Comparison: Fa-Hien vs. Hiuen Tsang

| Attribute | **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** | **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Period / Century** | 5th Century CE (399–414 CE) | 7th Century CE (629–645 CE) |
| **Ruling Indian Monarch** | **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** | **Harshavardhan (Harsha)** |
| **Ruling Dynasty** | Gupta Dynasty | Pushyabhuti / Vardhana Dynasty |
| **Travel Account / Book** | ***Fo-Kwo-Ki*** (*Record of Buddhist Kingdoms*) | ***Si-Yu-Ki*** (*Great Tang Records on the Western Regions*) |
| **Primary Base in India** | Spent 3 years in **Pataliputra** | Studied at **Nalanda University** under **Shilabhadra** |
| **Title / Epithet** | First major Chinese Buddhist pilgrim | *"Prince of Pilgrims"* / *"Master of Law"* |

---

## 📖 Detailed Analysis

### 1. Fa-Hien (Faxian)
* **Contemporary Ruler**: Arrived during the Golden Age of the **Gupta Empire** under **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)**.
* **Key Observations**:
  * Noted that the Gupta administration was benevolent, mild, and non-intrusive (no death penalty).
  * Observed high public safety (people lived peacefully without locking their doors) and widespread vegetarianism.
  * Focused primarily on Buddhist places like **Lumbini, Sarnath, Kapilavastu, and Pataliputra**.

### 2. Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)
* **Contemporary Ruler**: Visited India during the reign of **Harshavardhan**.
* **Key Observations**:
  * Attended the grand religious assemblies at **Kannauj** and **Prayag (Kumbh Mela)** organized by Harsha.
  * Documented social structure, law and order, regional administration, and economic life.
  * Studied Yogacara philosophy at **Nalanda University** for several years under Abbot Shilabhadra.

## ⚠️ Common Trap Questions in Exams

1. **Ruler Swap Trap**:
   * *Trap*: "Hiuen Tsang visited Chandragupta II" $\rightarrow$ **Incorrect**.
   * *Correct*: **H**iuen Tsang $\rightarrow$ **H**arsha | **F**a-Hien $\rightarrow$ **G**upta (**C**handragupta II).
2. **Travelogue Swap Trap**:
   * *Trap*: "Fa-Hien authored *Si-Yu-Ki*" $\rightarrow$ **Incorrect**.
   * *Correct*: **F**a-Hien $\rightarrow$ ***Fo-Kwo-Ki*** | **H**iuen Tsang $\rightarrow$ ***Si-Yu-Ki***.

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

During the reign of which of the following rulers did the Chinese traveler Hiuen Tsang visit India?

- [ ] Chandragupta I
- [ ] Chandragupta II
- [x] Harshavardhan
- [ ] Ashoka

> **Explanation**: Hiuen Tsang visited India in the 7th century CE during the reign of King Harshavardhan of the Pushyabhuti dynasty.

---

### PYQ 2

The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Fa-Hien came to India during the rule of which Gupta Emperor?

- [ ] Samudragupta
- [x] Chandragupta II
- [ ] Kumaragupta I
- [ ] Skandagupta

> **Explanation**: Fa-Hien traveled to India between 399–414 CE, which coincided with the reign of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya).

---

### PYQ 3

Which of the following books was written by the Chinese traveler Hiuen Tsang?

- [ ] Fo-Kwo-Ki
- [x] Si-Yu-Ki
- [ ] Indika
- [ ] Kitab-ul-Hind

> **Explanation**: *Si-Yu-Ki* (*Great Tang Records on the Western Regions*) was authored by Hiuen Tsang. *Fo-Kwo-Ki* was written by Fa-Hien, *Indika* by Megasthenes, and *Kitab-ul-Hind* by Al-Biruni.

---

### PYQ 4

Moroccan traveler Ibn Battuta visited India in the 14th century during the reign of:

- [ ] Alauddin Khalji
- [x] Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- [ ] Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq
- [ ] Firuz Shah Tughlaq

> **Explanation**: Ibn Battuta arrived in India in 1333 CE during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq, who appointed him as the Qazi (judge) of Delhi.

- [ ] 2 only
