# 📌 Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Significance**: Reflected an attempt to **widen political participation** by enlarging legislative councils while simultaneously **institutionalizing differentiated (communal) representation**.
- **Popular Nomenclature**: Commonly known as the **Morley-Minto Reforms**, named after **John Morley** (Secretary of State for India) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy of India).
- **Communal Electorates**: Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** for the first time in British India, earning Lord Minto the title *"Father of Communal Electorate"*.
- **Council Expansion**: Substantially increased the size of both the Imperial Central Legislative Council (from 16 to 60 members) and Provincial Legislative Councils.
- **Official vs. Non-Official Majority**: Retained an **official majority** in the Central Legislative Council, but allowed a **non-official majority** in Provincial Legislative Councils.
- **Indian Entry into Executive Councils**: Allowed Indians to join the Executive Councils of the Viceroy and Governors for the first time (**Satyendra Prasad Sinha** became the first Indian member as a Law Member).
- **Deliberative Powers**: Empowered legislative council members to ask supplementary questions, move resolutions on the budget, and discuss public interest matters, though without establishing a responsible government.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Provisions & Structural Impact of the 1909 Act

| Feature / Provision | Specific Constitutional Mechanism | Political & Administrative Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Separate Electorates** | Muslim members were to be elected exclusively by Muslim voters based on communal reservation. | Institutionalized the colonial policy of *"Divide and Rule"* and legally embedded communalism into Indian politics. |
| **Expansion of Councils** | Central Council enlarged from **16 to 60** members; Provincial Councils (Bengal, Madras, Bombay, UP) enlarged to up to 50 members. | Widened the scope of indirect election and Indian presence in deliberative forums. |
| **Council Majorities** | • **Centre**: Official majority retained.<br>• **Provinces**: Non-official majority permitted. | Maintained ultimate colonial executive dominance at the Centre while granting nominal provincial representation. |
| **Deliberative Rights** | Members could ask **supplementary questions** and vote on separate budget items (except non-votable heads). | Advanced parliamentary procedure beyond the limited scope of the Indian Councils Act, 1892. |
| **Executive Council Inclusion** | Indian appointment permitted in Viceroy’s and Governors' Executive Councils. | **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** appointed as the first Indian Law Member in the Viceroy's Executive Council. |

---

### 2. Evolution of Representative Councils (1861 vs. 1892 vs. 1909 vs. 1919)

| Constitutional Act | Expansion of Councils | Financial / Deliberative Powers | Electoral System & Communal Principle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1861** | Non-official members added to Viceroy's council (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao). | Very limited; purely advisory with no budget discussion. | Direct nomination by Viceroy; no election mechanism. |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1892** | Council size increased; indirect principle introduced via recommendations of local bodies. | Members could discuss the annual financial statement (budget) and ask questions with 6 days' notice. | Indirect recommendation system; no supplementary questions permitted. |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1909** | Central Council strength increased to 60; non-official majority in provinces. | Members could ask **supplementary questions** and move resolutions on the budget. | **Separate communal electorates introduced for Muslims**. |
| **Government of India Act, 1919** | **Bicameral legislature** at Centre; **Dyarchy** introduced in provinces. | Substantial voting control on budget; direct elections expanded. | Communal electorates extended to **Sikhs, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians, and Europeans**. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Constitutional Reform | Core Focus / Distinction | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)** | • **Separate electorates for Muslims**<br>• Enlarged legislative councils | Confusing 1909 with Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919) |
| **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919)**| • **Dyarchy in Provinces**<br>• Bicameralism at Centre<br>• Extended communal electorates to Sikhs/Christians | Attributing Dyarchy or Bicameralism to the 1909 Act |
| **Right to Ask Questions** | • **1892 Act**: Right to ask primary questions on budget<br>• **1909 Act**: Right to ask **supplementary questions** | Mixing up the financial powers granted in 1892 vs 1909 |
| **Executive vs Legislative Council** | SP Sinha joined the **Viceroy's Executive Council** (not just legislative) | Assuming Indians were only members of legislative bodies |

> - **Morley Talks** $\rightarrow$ John Morley (Secretary of State) expanded council discussions & supplementary questions
> - **Minto Separates** $\rightarrow$ Lord Minto introduced **Separate Electorates for Muslims**
> - **Sinha Joins** $\rightarrow$ **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** became the 1st Indian Law Member in the Executive Council
