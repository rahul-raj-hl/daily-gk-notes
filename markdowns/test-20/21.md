# 📌 New Economic Policy (NEP) 1991: Privatisation & Disinvestment

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Objective of Privatisation**: **Transferring ownership, management, or equity of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) to the private sector** to inject capital, modern technology, managerial expertise, and enhance operational efficiency.
- **Context of 1991 Reforms**: Announced in **July 1991** under Prime Minister **P. V. Narasimha Rao** and Finance Minister **Dr. Manmohan Singh** to overcome a severe Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis and high fiscal deficit.
- **Core Triad (LPG)**:
  - **Liberalisation**: Abolishing Industrial Licensing (*License-Permit-Quota Raj*), de-reserving industries, and easing market entry barriers.
  - **Privatisation**: Selling government equity in PSUs (**disinvestment**) and withdrawing state monopoly.
  - **Globalisation**: Integrating the domestic economy with the global market via tariff reductions and easing Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) caps.
- **De-reservation of Industries**: Reduced the number of industries exclusively reserved for the public sector from **17 to 8 in 1991**, and subsequently down to just **2** (**Atomic Energy** and **Railway Operations**).
- **Managerial Autonomy**: Granting status tiers (**Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna**) to high-performing PSUs to facilitate commercial freedom without routine bureaucratic interference.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Three Pillars of the New Economic Policy (NEP) 1991

| Reform Pillar | Primary Economic Objective | Key Reform Measures Undertaken |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liberalisation** | Eliminate unnecessary administrative controls and promote market competition. | • Abolished industrial licensing for most sectors.<br>• Deregulated interest rates and financial markets.<br>• Abolished the MRTP (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices) asset limit. |
| **Privatisation** | Transform loss-making/inefficient state monopolies into commercially viable enterprises. | • **Disinvestment**: Offloading government equity in CPSEs.<br>• Strategic sales and transfer of management control.<br>• Granting Navratna / Miniratna autonomy to profit-making PSUs. |
| **Globalisation** | Integrate India's production and trade systems with international trade flows. | • Substantial reduction in peak customs import tariffs.<br>• Devaluation of the Rupee (1991) and moving to market-determined exchange rates (LERMS).<br>• Automatic route approvals for Foreign Direct Investment (FDI). |

---

### 2. Routes & Mechanisms of Privatisation in India

| Privatisation Route | Operational Description | Impact on State Ownership & Control |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minority Disinvestment** | Government sells a fraction of its equity (e.g., $5\% - 25\%$) through Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) or Offer for Sale (OFS). | Government retains majority control ($\ge 51\%$) and management of the PSU. |
| **Majority / Strategic Disinvestment** | Government offloads $50\%$ or more of its equity along with the **transfer of management control** to a private strategic partner. | Effective transfer of ownership and day-to-day governance to the private sector (e.g., Air India to Tata Group). |
| **Asset Monetisation** | Leasing public infrastructure assets (roads, railways, power lines) to private operators for a fixed tenure. | Government retains ultimate asset ownership while generating upfront revenue (e.g., National Monetisation Pipeline). |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Economic Concept | Operational Meaning | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Privatisation vs. Disinvestment** | • **Disinvestment**: Selling government shares (can be partial or total)<br>• **Privatisation**: Complete transfer of majority ownership ($\ge 51\%$) and management | Assuming every disinvestment leads to complete privatization (minority stake sales keep govt control) |
| **Liberalisation vs. Privatisation** | • **Liberalisation**: Removing rules/licenses for all players<br>• **Privatisation**: Selling state-owned assets to private buyers | Confusing the removal of license barriers with the transfer of state enterprise equity |
| **DIPAM vs. NITI Aayog** | • **DIPAM**: Executes disinvestment transactions<br>• **NITI Aayog**: Identifies CPSEs for strategic divestment | Swapping execution role with advisory/identification mandate |

> **Mnemonic: "MANMOHAN CUTS RAJ: LIBERALISE RULES, PRIVATISE PSUs, GLOBALISE TRADE"**
> - **Liberalise Rules** $\rightarrow$ Abolish industrial licenses and quotas
> - **Privatise PSUs** $\rightarrow$ Sell state equity and transfer management to improve efficiency
> - **Globalise Trade** $\rightarrow$ Slash import duties and open doors to foreign FDI
