# 📌 Sources & Foundations of the Indian Constitution

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Government of India Act, 1935**: Regarded as the **most important colonial influence and structural backbone** of the administrative framework of the Constitution of India.
- **Parliamentary System & Collective Responsibility**: The Westminster system in India operates on the cornerstone principle of **collective responsibility** of the Council of Ministers to the popular house:
  - **Union Level**: **Article 75(3)** (Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**).
  - **State Level**: **Article 164(2)** (Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the **State Legislative Assembly**).
- **Genesis of Fundamental Rights**: The formal demand for enforceable Fundamental Rights was articulated in the **Nehru Report (1928)** and reiterated in the **Karachi Resolution (1931)** of the Indian National Congress.
- **Part III of the Constitution**: Enshrines **Fundamental Rights (Articles 12 to 35)**, inspired by the US Bill of Rights but **adapted to Indian socio-economic conditions** rather than adopted mechanically.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Major Structural & Philosophical Sources of the Indian Constitution

| Source / Influence | Major Features Borrowed & Adapted | Impact on Constitutional Architecture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Government of India Act, 1935** | • Federal Scheme & Provincial Autonomy<br>• Office of Governor<br>• Judiciary structure (Federal Court)<br>• Public Service Commissions (UPSC/SPSC)<br>• Emergency Provisions & Administrative Details | Forms over **half (nearly 250 provisions)** of the textual and administrative structure of the Constitution. |
| **British Constitution (UK)** | • Parliamentary form of government<br>• Principle of **Collective Responsibility (Arts 75 & 164)**<br>• Rule of Law & Legislative procedure<br>• Single Citizenship & Cabinet system<br>• Prerogative Writs & Bicameralism | Establishes the executive's accountability to the elected legislature. |
| **US Constitution (USA)** | • **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts 12–35)**<br>• Independence of Judiciary & Judicial Review<br>• Impeachment of President & Removal of Judges<br>• Post of Vice-President | Forms the cornerstone of civil liberties and constitutional checks. |
| **Nehru Report (1928) & INC Resolutions** | • Explicit charter of 19 Fundamental Rights<br>• Universal Adult Suffrage<br>• Equal rights for women<br>• Secular state apparatus | Provided the indigenous ideological foundation for Part III and adult franchise. |

---

### 2. The Core Principle of Collective Responsibility

| Level of Government | Constitutional Article | Nature of Responsibility | Constitutional Effect |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Union Government** | **Article 75(3)** | Council of Ministers $\rightarrow$ **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) | The entire ministry falls if a **No-Confidence Motion** is passed in the Lok Sabha. |
| **State Government** | **Article 164(2)** | Council of Ministers $\rightarrow$ **Legislative Assembly** (*Vidhan Sabha*) | The Council of Ministers resigns if it loses majority support on the floor of the State Assembly. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Constitutional Feature | Constitutional Source / Article | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Collective Responsibility (Art 75)** | Responsible to **Lok Sabha only** | Mistakenly selecting "Parliament" (which includes Rajya Sabha & President) |
| **Individual Responsibility (Art 75(2))**| Ministers hold office during **Pleasure of the President** | Confusing collective accountability with individual tenure |
| **Fundamental Rights vs. DPSP Sources**| • **FR (Part III)**: US Constitution<br>• **DPSP (Part IV)**: Irish Constitution | Mixing up the borrowing origins of Part III and Part IV |
| **Emergency Origin** | • **Framework**: GoI Act 1935<br>• **Suspension of FR during Emergency**: Weimar (Germany) | Swapping the general emergency structure with the suspension of rights |

- [ ] The British Parliamentary Conventions
- [ ] The Irish Directive Principles
- [x] The US Bill of Rights
- [ ] The Weimar Constitution

> Part III was inspired by the US Bill of Rights, but the Constituent Assembly tailored it by introducing reasonable restrictions to balance individual liberty with social order.

- [ ] Office of Governor
