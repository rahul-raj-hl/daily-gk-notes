# 📌 Baisakhi (Vaisakhi): Sikh New Year, Khalsa Panth & Harvest Festival

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Significance**: Baisakhi marks the **beginning of the Sikh New Year** and commemorates the historic **formation of the Khalsa Panth** by the 10th Sikh Guru, **Guru Gobind Singh**, in **1699**.
- **Seasonal & Agricultural Context**: Celebrated primarily in Northern India (especially **Punjab and Haryana**) as a joyous **spring harvest festival** marking the ripening and harvesting of **Rabi crops** (predominantly wheat).
- **Date of Observance**: Usually falls on **13th or 14th April** every year, coinciding with the first day of the solar month of *Vaisakh* according to the solar cycle / Nanakshahi calendar.
- **Cultural Festivities**: Marked by community feasting (*Langar*), visits to Gurdwaras, organizing colorful processions (*Nagar Kirtan*), and high-energy traditional folk dances—**Bhangra** (by men) and **Giddha** (by women).
- **Historical Resonance**: Also solemnly remembered in modern Indian history as the fateful day of the **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre** (13 April 1919) in Amritsar.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Historical Foundation of the Khalsa Panth (1699)

| Dimension | Historical & Religious Milestone |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Year & Venue** | **13 April 1699** at **Anandpur Sahib** (Punjab). |
| **Initiating Guru** | **Guru Gobind Singh Ji** (10th Sikh Guru). |
| **The Panj Pyare** | The "Five Beloved Ones" who stepped forward ready to sacrifice their lives and received the initiation of *Khande Di Pahul* (Amrit Sanchar). |
| **The Five K's (Kakars)** | Mandated for all baptized Khalsa Sikhs: <br>1. **Kesh** (Uncut hair)<br>2. **Kangha** (Wooden comb)<br>3. **Kara** (Iron/steel bracelet)<br>4. **Kachera** (Cotton undergarment)<br>5. **Kirpan** (Curved ceremonial dagger) |
| **Identity Suffixes** | Conferred the universal title **Singh** ("Lion") for men and **Kaur** ("Princess") for women to dismantle caste hierarchies. |

| **Bisu** | **Karnataka (Tulu Nadu)** | Traditional agrarian solar New Year. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Festival | Primary State / Religion | Nature of Harvest / Occasion | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Baisakhi** | **Punjab / Sikhism** | **Spring / Rabi harvest** & Khalsa founding | Confusing with winter harvest festivals (Lohri/Makar Sankranti) |
| **Lohri** | **Punjab (January 13)** | **Winter harvest** (Sugarcane/Mustard/Bonfires) | Mixing up the timing of Lohri (winter) and Baisakhi (spring) |
| **Mahavir Jayanti** | **Jainism** | Celebrates the birth of Lord Mahavira (Chaitra) | Confusing Baisakhi with Jain lunar festivals |
| **Vishu vs. Onam** | • **Vishu**: April (Solar New Year)<br>• **Onam**: Aug–Sept (Harvest/Mahabali) | Confusing Kerala's New Year (Vishu) with its mega harvest festival (Onam) |
