# 📌 Government of India Act, 1935: Autonomy & Imperial Safeguards

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Significance**: Represented an attempt to **combine limited provincial autonomy with continued imperial safeguards** and executive vetoes.
- **Abolition of Provincial Dyarchy**: Abolished dyarchy in the provinces and replaced it with **Provincial Autonomy**, allowing elected Indian ministers to administer provincial departments.
- **Proposed All-India Federation**: Provided for an **All-India Federation** comprising British Indian Provinces and Princely States (which never came into operation as princely states refused to join).
- **Dyarchy at the Centre**: Proposed dyarchy at the federal executive level by dividing federal subjects into **Reserved** (Defense, External Affairs, Ecclesiastical, Tribal) and **Transferred** spheres.
- **Executive Discretion & Veto**: Retained overriding **discretionary powers and special responsibilities** with the Governor-General and Provincial Governors, allowing them to dismiss ministries and override legislation.
- **Constituent & Parliamentary Sovereignty**: No constituent authority was given to Indians; supreme power to amend or frame constitutional structures remained with the **British Parliament**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Pillars of the Government of India Act, 1935

| Constitutional Feature | Operational Mechanism | Historical & Political Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Provincial Autonomy** | Replaced dyarchy with responsible ministerial government in 11 provinces. | Ministers were accountable to provincial legislatures, but Governors retained veto powers. |
| **All-India Federation** | Proposed federation of British provinces and willing Princely States. | **Never materialized** because accession of princely states was optional and they failed to join. |
| **Three Legislative Lists** | Divided legislative powers into: <br>• **Federal List** (59 items)<br>• **Provincial List** (54 items)<br>• **Concurrent List** (36 items) | **Residuary powers** were vested in the discretion of the **Governor-General (Viceroy)**. |
| **Federal Court** | Established a Federal Court (set up in **1937**) to resolve inter-provincial disputes. | Precursor to the Supreme Court of India; final appeals still went to the Privy Council in London. |
| **Bicameralism in Provinces** | Introduced bicameral legislatures in **6 out of 11 provinces** (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP). | Expanded regional legislative councils and assemblies. |

### 2. Evaluation of Options & Constitutional Realities

| Analytical Statement | Validity | Underlying Reason |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Combined Provincial Autonomy with Imperial Safeguards** | **Correct** | Granted autonomy to elected ministries while safeguarding British colonial supremacy through gubernatorial overrides. |
| **Complete Parliamentary Sovereignty in India** | **Incorrect** | Sovereign power remained vested entirely in the **British Parliament and Crown**. |
| **Abolished Executive Discretion in Governance** | **Incorrect** | Expanded Governor-General and Governors' discretionary powers (e.g., Section 93 governor rule). |
| **Transferred Constituent Authority to Indians** | **Incorrect** | Indians had no power to amend the Act; full constituent control was kept in London. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Feature / Concept | Government of India Act, 1919 | Government of India Act, 1935 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dyarchy** | Introduced in **Provinces** | Abolished in Provinces; Proposed at **Centre** |
| **Bicameralism** | Introduced at the **Centre** | Introduced in **6 Provinces** (and retained at Centre) |
| **Provincial Autonomy** | Absent (Divided into Transferred & Reserved) | **Introduced** (Responsible provincial ministries) |
| **Residuary Powers** | Kept with the Central Government | Vested specifically in **Governor-General's discretion** |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "1935 FREES PROVINCES, BINDS WITH SAFEGUARDS, MOVES DYARCHY TO THE CENTRE"**
> - **Frees Provinces** $\rightarrow$ Provincial Autonomy introduced (Dyarchy abolished in provinces)
> - **Binds with Safeguards** $\rightarrow$ Imperial veto and discretionary powers retained
> - **Dyarchy to Centre** $\rightarrow$ Dyarchy shifted to the Federal level

### PYQ 1

Which of the following statements best explains the constitutional significance of the Government of India Act, 1935?

- [ ] It established complete parliamentary sovereignty in India.
- [x] It represented an attempt to combine limited provincial autonomy with continued imperial safeguards.
- [ ] It abolished executive discretion in governance.
- [ ] It transferred full constituent authority to elected Indian representatives.

> The 1935 Act granted autonomy to elected provincial governments while retaining extensive discretionary powers and veto safeguards with the Governor-General and Governors to maintain British control.

---

### PYQ 2

Under the Government of India Act of 1935, the 'Residuary Powers' of legislation were vested in which authority?

- [ ] Federal Legislature
- [ ] Provincial Legislatures
- [x] Governor-General
- [ ] Secretary of State for India

> Unlike modern India's Constitution where residuary powers reside with the Union Parliament, under the 1935 Act, the Governor-General held discretionary authority over residuary subjects.

---

### PYQ 3

In how many British Indian provinces was a bicameral legislature introduced under the Government of India Act, 1935?

- [ ] 4
- [x] 6
- [ ] 8
- [ ] 11

> Out of eleven provinces, bicameralism was introduced in six: Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, and the United Provinces (UP).
