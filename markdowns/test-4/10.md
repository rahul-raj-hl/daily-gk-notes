# 📌 Types of Unemployment & Structural Unemployment

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Structural Unemployment**: Arises due to a **mismatch between the jobs available in the market and the skills/locations of available workers** (caused by technological advancement, automation, or structural shifts).
- **Disguised Unemployment**: Occurs when **more workers are engaged in a job than actually needed**; the marginal productivity of extra labor is zero (common in Indian agriculture).
- **Cyclical Unemployment**: Caused by **business cycle fluctuations and economic downturns/recessions** due to deficient aggregate demand.
- **Seasonal Unemployment**: Occurs when people find work only during specific seasons of the year (e.g., farming seasons, ice-cream factories, tourism).
- **Frictional Unemployment**: Temporary unemployment occurring while a worker is **searching for a new job or transitioning between jobs**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Understanding Structural Unemployment

*Underlying reasons, characteristics, and solutions.*

- **Core Cause**: Occurs due to long-term fundamental changes in the structure of an economy:
  - **Technological Changes & Automation**: E.g., manual typists replaced by computers and word processors.
  - **Industrial Transition**: E.g., coal miners losing jobs as the energy sector transitions to renewables.
  - **Geographical Mismatch**: Job openings exist in one region, while the unemployed labor force resides in another.
- **Nature**: It is typically **long-term and chronic** because workers cannot immediately take up open jobs without undergoing re-skilling or geographic relocation.
- **Remedies**:
  - Government and industry investment in vocational training and skill development programs.
  - Providing relocation allowances and improving labor market mobility.

---

### 2. Comparative Matrix of Unemployment Types

*High-yield distinctions tested frequently across exams.*

- **Disguised Unemployment**:
  - Visible employment, but hidden underemployment.
  - Removing surplus workers causes **zero reduction in total output** ($\text{Marginal Productivity} = 0$).
- **Frictional Unemployment**:
  - Voluntary, short-term search period.
  - Exists even in a healthy, full-employment economy.
- **Cyclical (Keynesian) Unemployment**:
  - Involuntary; directly correlated with economic slowdowns, recessions, or depression phases.
  - Addressed through expansionary fiscal and monetary stimulus.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Unemployment Pair | Core Differentiating Factor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Structural Unemployment** | Driven by **permanent structural/technological changes & skill gap** |
| **Cyclical Unemployment** | Driven by **temporary macroeconomic cycles & demand slowdown** |

---

| Unemployment Pair | Characteristic Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Disguised Unemployment** | Person appears employed, but **Marginal Productivity = 0** |
| **Frictional Unemployment** | Person is actively searching between leaving and joining jobs |

> - **SKILL GAP** $\rightarrow$ **M**ismatch between available jobs and worker capabilities
