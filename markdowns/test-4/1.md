# 📌 Factors Responsible for Cyclone Formation

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Non-Factor in Formation**: **Rainfall** is an **outcome/result** of a cyclone, NOT a causal factor responsible for its formation.
- **Key Formation Factors**: High Sea Surface Temperature ($> 27^\circ\text{C}$), high humidity/moisture in the air, favorable wind speed/direction, low vertical wind shear, and presence of Coriolis force.
- **Etymology**: The word *Cyclone* is derived from the Greek word *Cyclos*, meaning the *"coils of a snake"*.
- **Term Coined By**: Coined by **Henry Piddington** (often written as Paddington) observing tropical revolving storms in the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea.
- **Core Structure**: Cyclones are characterized by **rapid inward air circulation around a low-pressure center**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Cause vs. Effect in Cyclone Formation

*Distinguishing initiating parameters from resultant weather phenomena.*

- **Causal / Driving Factors**:
  - **Sea Surface Temperature (SST)**: Warm ocean waters ($\ge 27^\circ\text{C}$) provide the continuous heat and thermal energy.
  - **High Humidity & Moisture**: Warm, moist air rising from the ocean surface releases latent heat of condensation, powering the storm.
  - **Wind Speed & Low Vertical Wind Shear**: Ensures uniform vertical structure without tearing the storm apart.
  - **Coriolis Force**: Essential to create the cyclonic rotational spin (deflection to the left in Southern Hemisphere and right in Northern Hemisphere). Absent at the equator ($0^\circ-5^\circ$ latitude).
- **Resultant / Outcome Phenomenon**:
  - **Torrential Rainfall**: Condensation of moisture inside cumulonimbus clouds produces heavy rain and flooding; rainfall does not trigger the cyclone.
  - **Storm Surge & High Waves**: Ocean water pushed inland due to high-speed cyclonic winds.

---

### 2. Atmospheric Anatomy of a Tropical Cyclone

*Key structural regions of a mature cyclone.*

- **Eye**:
  - Calm, cloudless center with descending air and lowest atmospheric pressure.
- **Eye Wall**:
  - Region surrounding the eye with the most violent winds, thickest clouds, and maximum rainfall.
- **Spiral Rainbands**:
  - Outer feeder bands spiraling into the eye wall, bringing persistent showers and gusts.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Why do tropical cyclones NOT form near the Equator ($0^\circ$ to $5^\circ$ latitude)?

- [ ] Low ocean temperatures
- [x] Absence of sufficient Coriolis Force
- [ ] Excessively high wind shear
- [ ] Lack of moisture

> Coriolis force is zero at the equator, which prevents the development of rotational vortex necessary for cyclonic spin.

---

### Q2

What is the central calm area of a mature tropical cyclone called?

- [x] Eye
- [ ] Eye wall
- [ ] Crest
- [ ] Ridge

> The 'Eye' is the roughly circular area of comparatively light winds and fair weather found at the center of a severe tropical cyclone.

---

### Q3

In the Northern Hemisphere, winds blow in which direction around a low-pressure cyclonic center?

- [ ] Clockwise
- [x] Anticlockwise (Counterclockwise)
- [ ] Straight vertical
- [ ] West to East linearly

> Due to the Coriolis effect, cyclonic winds rotate anticlockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
