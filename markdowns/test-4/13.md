# 📌 Commercialisation of Agriculture under British Rule & Indigo Cultivation

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Promoted Export Cash Crop**: **Indigo** (along with cotton, opium, tea, and jute) was heavily promoted and forced upon Indian farmers to feed the British textile and colonial export markets.
- **Food Crops vs. Cash Crops**: Staple subsistence food crops like **Maize**, **Barley**, and **Pulses** were grown for local domestic consumption and were discouraged or replaced by cash crops.
- **Exploitative Cultivation Systems**: Practiced mainly through the **Nij System** (direct cultivation by planters) and **Ryoti System** (coercive advance contracts to peasants).
- **Indigo Revolt (Nil Bidroho, 1859–60)**: A major peasant uprising centered in Bengal (Nadia district) against European planters, leading to the appointment of the **Indigo Commission of 1860**.
- **Literary Highlight**: Dinabandhu Mitra authored the famous play ***Nil Darpan* (1860)** to expose the atrocities of the indigo planters.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Commercialisation of Agriculture

*Transformation of Indian subsistence agriculture to raw-material export.*

- **Underlying Driver**: Driven by the raw material demands of the **Industrial Revolution in Britain** and lucrative trade monopolies.
- **Major Cash Crops Encouraged for Export**:
  - **Indigo**: Natural blue dye required for British textile factories.
  - **Raw Cotton**: Fed the textile mills of Manchester and Lancashire.
  - **Opium**: Exported illegally/monopolistically to China to balance the trade deficit in tea (leading to Opium Wars).
  - **Tea & Coffee**: Plantation agriculture developed in Assam, Darjeeling, and Nilgiris.
  - **Jute**: Cultivated in Bengal to supply Dundee mills with bagging materials.
- **Consequences**: Disrupted traditional cropping patterns, exhausted fertile topsoil, and caused frequent artificial food shortages and severe famines.

---

### 2. The Indigo Exploitation & Rebellion (1859–60)

*The struggle of the Bengal ryots against forced dye cultivation.*

- **Cultivation Systems**:
  - **Nij Cultivation**: Planters produced indigo on lands directly controlled by them using hired labor.
  - **Ryoti System**: Planters forced the ryots (cultivators) to sign agreements (*satta*) and accept advance cash loans (*dadon*), compelling them to grow indigo on at least $25\%$ of their land.
- **The Indigo Rebellion (Nil Bidroho)**:
  - Began in **1859** in Govindpur and Chaugacha villages (Nadia district, Bengal) under the leadership of **Digambar Biswas** and **Bishnu Biswas**.
  - Peasants refused to take advances, refused to plant indigo, and resisted planters' lathiyals (armed retainers).
- **Outcome & Indigo Commission (1860)**:
  - The British government instituted the **Indigo Commission (1860)**.
  - The Commission ruled that indigo cultivation was unremunerative for the ryot and declared that peasants could not be forced to grow it against their will.
