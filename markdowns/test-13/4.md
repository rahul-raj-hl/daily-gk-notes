# 📌 Ibn Battuta & Foreign Travelers in Medieval India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Native Country**: Ibn Battuta was a famous Muslim traveler from **Morocco** (Tangier, North Africa).
- **Arrival in India**: Arrived in India in **1333 A.D.** during the rule of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (Delhi Sultanate).
- **Royal Position**: Appointed as the **Qazi** (Islamic judge) of Delhi by Muhammad bin Tughlaq for 8 years.
- **Famous Travel Book**: Author of the book **'Rihla'** (written in Arabic language), describing social and economic life in 14th-century India.
- **Mission to China**: Sent as an official ambassador (envoy) of Muhammad bin Tughlaq to China in 1341 A.D.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Ibn Battuta's Visit & Work

*Details of his arrival, duties, and writings.*

- **Origin**: Born in Tangier, Morocco (North Africa).
- **Year of Visit**: Entered India through the high mountain passes (Hindu Kush) in **1333 A.D.**
- **Ruler's Court**: Visited the court of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (Tughlaq Dynasty).
- **Key Writings**: Wrote **'Rihla'** (The Journey) in **Arabic**, which gives details about the Indian postal system, betel leaf (*paan*), coconut, and urban life.
- **Role in Delhi**: Appointed as the grand judge (Qazi of Delhi) and later sent as a diplomatic messenger to China.

---

### 2. Major Foreign Travelers in Ancient & Medieval India

*Other frequently tested foreign visitors in SSC exams.*

- **Megasthenes** (Greece): Visited Chandragupta Maurya's court; wrote **Indika**.
- **Fa-Hien / Faxian** (China): Visited during Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya); studied Buddhist manuscripts.
- **Hiuen Tsang / Xuanzang** (China): Visited during **Harshavardhana**; known as "Prince of Pilgrims"; wrote **Si-Yu-Ki**.
- **Al-Biruni** (Uzbekistan / Persia): Came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**; wrote **Kitab-ul-Hind** (Tahqeeq-i-Hind).
- **Marco Polo** (Italy): Visited South India (Pandya Dynasty) around 1292 A.D.
- **Nicolo de Conti** (Italy): Visited the Vijayanagara Empire during the reign of **Deva Raya I** (1420 A.D.).
- **Abdul Razzaq** (Persia): Visited Vijayanagara court during **Deva Raya II** (1443 A.D.).
