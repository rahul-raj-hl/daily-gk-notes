# 📌 Gudi Padwa & Regional New Year Festivals of India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Gudi Padwa**.
- **What is Gudi Padwa?**: The traditional **New Year** festival celebrated by the people of **Maharashtra, Goa (Konkan region)**, and parts of neighboring states.
- **When is it Celebrated?**: On the first day (*Pratipada*) of the bright half (*Shukla Paksha*) of the Hindu lunar month of **Chaitra** (March–April).
- **The 'Gudi' Flag**: A victory flag made of a bamboo staff wrapped in a bright green or yellow silk cloth, topped with neem leaves, mango twigs, flower garlands, and an **inverted copper or silver pot (*Kalash / Tambya*)**.
- **Symbolism**: Signifies the **triumph of good over evil**, Lord Rama's victory over Ravana and his return to Ayodhya, the coronation of King Shalivahana, and the arrival of the spring harvest (*Rabi season*).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Customs of Gudi Padwa

- **The Gudi Hoisting**: Placed at the entrance or outside windows/terraces of households to ward off evil and invite prosperity.
- **Traditional Food & Prasad**:
  - A special prasad made of **neem leaves, jaggery, tamarind, and coriander seeds** is consumed, symbolizing that life is a balanced mix of bitter, sweet, sour, and tangy experiences.
  - Festive dishes like **Shrikhand-Puri** and **Puran Poli** are prepared.
- **Padwa Meaning**: The term *Padwa* originates from the Sanskrit word *Pratipada* (the first day of a lunar fortnight).

---

### 2. Festivals in the Other Options

- **Navratri**:
  - A nine-night festival dedicated to the worship of Goddess Durga and her nine forms (*Navadurga*).
  - Celebrated twice a year: in spring (*Chaitra Navratri*) and autumn (*Sharad Navratri*), known for Garba/Dandiya dances in Gujarat and across India.
- **Janmashtami (Gokulashtami)**:
  - Celebrates the birth of **Lord Krishna** on the 8th day (*Ashtami*) of the dark fortnight (*Krishna Paksha*) in the Hindu month of **Bhadrapada**.
  - Famous for the **Dahi Handi** festival in Maharashtra.
- **Durga Puja**:
  - Major 10-day festival especially celebrated in **West Bengal, Odisha, Tripura, and Assam**, honoring Goddess Durga's victory over the demon Mahishasura.
  - Inscribed on **UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** (2021).
