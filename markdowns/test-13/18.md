# 📌 Third Five-Year Plan (1961–1966) & Economic Planning in India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Third** (Third Five-Year Plan, 1961–1966).
- **Target vs. Actual Growth**: Targeted a growth rate of **5.6%**, but achieved only **2.8%** (making it the most unsuccessful Five-Year Plan).
- **Primary Causes of Failure**:
  - **Sino-Indian War (1962)**: Chinese aggression forced massive diversion of funds to defense.
  - **Indo-Pakistani War (1965)**: Further strained national finances and foreign exchange reserves.
  - **Severe Droughts (1965 & 1966)**: Caused an acute food crisis and famine-like conditions across several states.
- **Immediate Consequence**: The government declared a **Plan Holiday (1966–1969)**, during which three successive **Annual Plans** were implemented instead of a regular Five-Year Plan.
- **Also Known As**: **Gadgil Yojana** (named after D. R. Gadgil, then Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Third Five-Year Plan (1961–1966)

*Key objectives, major initiatives, and external shocks:*

- **Core Objectives**:
  - Aimed to establish a self-reliant and self-generating economy (*"Take-off stage"*).
  - Emphasized both agriculture (food grains) and heavy basic industries.
- **Major Establishments during the 3rd Plan**:
  - **Bokaro Steel Plant** (set up in 1964 with Soviet assistance in Jharkhand).
  - **Food Corporation of India (FCI)** established in 1965 to ensure food grain procurement.
  - **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP / Agricultural Prices Commission)** set up in 1965 to determine Minimum Support Prices (MSP).
  - **Industrial Development Bank of India (IDBI)** and **Unit Trust of India (UTI)** founded in 1964.
- **The 1966 Devaluation**: Due to depleted foreign reserves and soaring inflation, the Indian Rupee was devalued by nearly 36.5% in June 1966.

---

### 2. Overview of Plans in the Options

- **Third Plan (1961–1966)**:
  - Slogan/Goal: Self-reliance and food self-sufficiency.
  - Severely impacted by two wars and back-to-back droughts.
- **Fourth Plan (1969–1974)**:
  - Slogan/Goal: *"Growth with stability and progressive achievement of self-reliance"*.
  - Key events: **Nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks (1969)**, **Indo-Pak War / Bangladesh Liberation War (1971)**, launch of the **Green Revolution**, and **Pokhran-I nuclear test (Operation Smiling Buddha, 1974)**.
- **Seventh Plan (1985–1990)**:
  - Slogan/Goal: *"Food, Work, and Productivity"*.
  - One of the most successful plans (Target: 5.0%, Achieved: 6.01%).
  - Launch of Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (JRY, 1989) and speedpost services.
- **Eighth Plan (1992–1997)**:
  - Slogan/Goal: Human resource development and economic modernization.
  - Formulated on the **Rao-Manmohan Model** following the landmark **1991 LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation) reforms**; achieved 6.8% growth against a 5.6% target.

| **2nd Plan** | **1956–1961** | PC Mahalanobis Model | Heavy Industry & Capital Goods | Steel Plants (Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela) |

| **Rolling Plan** | **1978–1980** | Gunnar Myrdal concept | Flexible yearly targets | Introduced by Morarji Desai Government |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Term / Period | Exact Years | Reason / Main Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plan Holiday** | **1966–1969** (3 Annual Plans) | Declared due to failure of 3rd Plan (wars, drought, devaluation) |
| **Rolling Plan** | **1978–1980** | Introduced by the Janata Party after ending the 5th Plan early |
| **Annual Plans (Gap)**| **1990–1992** | Caused by political instability and the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis |
