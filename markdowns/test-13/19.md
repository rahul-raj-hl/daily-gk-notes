# 📌 Office of the Governor & Constitutional Provisions

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **A is correct and B is incorrect**.
- **Statement A is CORRECT**: The **Governor** is the constitutional/nominal **head of the state** (Article 153 & 154), while the Chief Minister is the real executive head (*de facto*).
- **Statement B is INCORRECT**: The Governor is **NOT appointed by the state government**; the Governor is **appointed by the President of India** by warrant under his hand and seal (Article 155).
- **Dual Role of the Governor**:
  1. **Constitutional Head** of the State Executive.
  2. **Representative / Agent of the Central Government** in the state.
- **Tenure**: Holds office during the **pleasure of the President** (Article 156).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Constitutional Articles Related to the Governor

*Core articles under Part VI of the Indian Constitution:*

- **Article 153**: Provides that there shall be a Governor for each state (*The 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 enabled the appointment of the same person as Governor for two or more states*).
- **Article 154**: Executive power of the state is vested in the Governor and is exercised either directly or through officers subordinate to him.
- **Article 155**: The Governor of a state is **appointed by the President** by warrant under his hand and seal.
- **Article 156**: The Governor holds office during the **pleasure of the President** (normal term is 5 years, but can be removed anytime without specified constitutional grounds).
- **Article 157 & 158**: Qualifications for appointment:
  - Must be a **citizen of India**.
  - Must have completed **35 years of age**.
  - Must not hold any office of profit and should not be a member of Parliament or State Legislature.
- **Article 161**: Power of the Governor to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment.
- **Article 213**: Power of the Governor to **promulgate Ordinances** when the State Legislature is not in session.

---

### 2. Comparison: Head of State vs. Head of Government

*Understanding nominal versus real executive power:*

- **At the Union (Centre) Level**:
  - **Head of State / Nominal (*De Jure*) Head**: **President of India**
  - **Head of Government / Real (*De Facto*) Head**: **Prime Minister**
- **At the State Level**:
  - **Head of the State / Nominal (*De Jure*) Head**: **Governor**
  - **Head of the State Government / Real (*De Facto*) Head**: **Chief Minister**

- [ ] President of India
- [ ] Chief Minister of the State
- [x] Chief Justice of the concerned High Court
- [ ] Speaker of the Legislative Assembly

> Under Article 159, the oath of office to the Governor is administered by the Chief Justice of the High Court exercising jurisdiction over the state.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

What is the minimum age prescribed under Article 157 for a person to be eligible for appointment as a Governor?

- [ ] 25 years
- [ ] 30 years
- [x] 35 years
- [ ] 40 years

> According to Article 157, a person must have completed 35 years of age to be appointed as Governor.

---

### Q2

Under which Article of the Indian Constitution is the Governor empowered to promulgate an Ordinance during the recess of the State Legislature?

- [ ] Article 123
- [ ] Article 200
- [x] Article 213
- [ ] Article 356

> Article 213 grants ordinance-making powers to the Governor (while Article 123 grants it to the President at the Centre).

---

### Q3

The Governor holds office during the pleasure of:

- [ ] Prime Minister
- [x] President of India
- [ ] Parliament
- [ ] State Legislative Assembly

> Under Article 156(1), the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President and can be removed at any time.

- [x] Sarkaria Commission (1983)
