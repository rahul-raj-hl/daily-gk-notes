# 📌 Sources of the Indian Constitution

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Joint Sitting of Parliament**: The provision for the joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament (Article 108) is borrowed from **Australia**.
- **Major Sources**: The Constitution of India heavily borrowed features from various countries including the UK, US, Ireland, Canada, Australia, USSR, and France.
- **Government of India Act 1935**: Served as the structural backbone and largest overall source for the Indian Constitution.
- **Article 108**: Deals with the joint sitting summoned by the President to resolve deadlocks on ordinary bills.
- **Presiding Officer**: A joint sitting is presided over by the **Lok Sabha Speaker** (or Deputy Speaker in their absence).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Features Borrowed from Key Constitutions

*Detailed breakdown of borrowed provisions frequently asked in SSC exams.*

- **Australia**:
  - **Joint Sitting of the two Houses of Parliament** (Article 108).
  - **Concurrent List** (List-III under Schedule 7).
  - **Freedom of Trade, Commerce, and Intercourse**.
- **United Kingdom (UK)**:
  - Parliamentary form of government, Rule of Law, and Legislative procedure.
  - Single Citizenship, Cabinet system, Prerogative writs, Parliamentary privileges, and Bicameralism.
- **United States (US)**:
  - Fundamental Rights, Preamble, and Independence of Judiciary.
  - Judicial Review, Impeachment of President, and Removal of Supreme Court & High Court judges.
- **Ireland (Irish)**:
  - Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP).
  - Method of election of the President.
  - Nomination of 12 members to Rajya Sabha by the President.
- **Canada**:
  - Federation with a strong Centre (Centrifugal federalism).
  - Vesting of Residuary Powers in the Centre.
  - Appointment of State Governors by the Centre and Advisory Jurisdiction of Supreme Court.
- **USSR (Russia)**:
  - Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) and Ideals of Social, Economic, and Political Justice in the Preamble.
- **France**:
  - Concept of Republic and Ideals of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity in the Preamble.

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

The feature of 'Joint Sitting of two houses of Parliament' in the Indian Constitution is borrowed from the constitution of ________.

- [ ] Canada
- [ ] United States of America
- [x] Australia
- [ ] France

> The provision for the joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament to resolve deadlocks is taken from the Australian Constitution.

---

### PYQ 2

The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' (DPSP) in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from which country?

- [ ] USA
- [x] Ireland
- [ ] UK
- [ ] Canada

> DPSP was borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which originally took it from Spain).

---

### PYQ 3

From which country's constitution was the concept of 'Fundamental Duties' incorporated into the Indian Constitution?

- [ ] USA
- [ ] UK
- [x] USSR (now Russia)
- [ ] Australia

> Fundamental Duties were added via the 42nd Amendment Act (1976), inspired by the USSR Constitution.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Which Article of the Indian Constitution provides for a Joint Sitting of both Houses of Parliament?

- [ ] Article 105
- [x] Article 108
- [ ] Article 110
- [ ] Article 112

> Article 108 deals with the Joint Sitting of both Houses summoned by the President.

---

### Q2

Who presides over a Joint Sitting of the two Houses of Parliament?

- [ ] President of India
- [ ] Vice-President of India
- [x] Speaker of Lok Sabha
- [ ] Prime Minister of India

> The Speaker of Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament.

---

### Q3

The feature of 'Residuary Powers' resting with the Centre in the Indian Constitution is borrowed from:

- [ ] USA
- [ ] Australia
- [x] Canada
- [ ] Ireland

> A strong federation with residuary powers vested in the Centre was borrowed from Canada.

---

### Q4

The ideals of 'Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity' written in the Preamble were borrowed from the revolution of which country?

- [ ] Russian Revolution
- [ ] American Revolution
- [x] French Revolution
- [ ] Industrial Revolution

> Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity were slogan ideals of the French Revolution (1789–1799) borrowed from France.
