# 📌 Earthquake Measurement Scales & Scientific Instruments

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Richter scale**.
- **Richter Scale**: A **logarithmic scale** developed in **1935** by **Charles F. Richter** (along with Beno Gutenberg) that measures the **magnitude (energy released)** of an earthquake.
- **Logarithmic Nature**: Each whole-number increase on the scale represents a **10-fold increase in wave amplitude** and approximately **31.6 times more energy released**.
- **Seismograph / Seismometer**: The physical instrument that detects and records earthquake shockwaves, producing a trace called a **seismogram**.
- **Mercalli Scale**: Measures the **intensity / visible damage** caused by an earthquake on a linear scale from **I to XII** (Roman numerals).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Earthquake Measurement Scales: Magnitude vs. Intensity

*Difference between energy release and visible ground damage:*

- **Richter Scale (Magnitude)**:
  - **What it Measures**: Quantitative measure of total energy released at the focus/hypocenter.
  - **Scale Type**: Base-10 logarithmic scale (practically ranges from 0 to 10, though open-ended).
  - **Values**: Quakes under 2.0 are micro-earthquakes (not felt by humans); quakes above 7.0 are major with widespread destruction.
  - **Historical Peak**: Largest recorded earthquake was the **1960 Valdivia Earthquake in Chile** (magnitude 9.5).
- **Mercalli Scale (Intensity)**:
  - **What it Measures**: Qualitative assessment of ground shaking, damage to buildings, and human impact at a specific surface location.
  - **Scale Range**: **1 to 12 (I to XII)** in Roman numerals.
  - **Variation**: Magnitude has a single value for an earthquake, whereas Mercalli intensity decreases with distance from the epicenter.

---

### 2. Instruments in the Options

- **Polygraph**: Commonly known as a **lie detector**; records involuntary physiological changes (heart rate, blood pressure, skin conductivity, respiratory rate) to assess truthfulness.
- **Barometer**: Invented by **Evangelista Torricelli** to measure **atmospheric pressure**; sudden drops indicate approaching storms/cyclones.
- **Thermometer**: Measures **temperature** (thermal state of a body/environment) using expansion of mercury, alcohol, or digital sensors.
- **Richter Scale / Seismograph**: Measures earthquake magnitude and records seismic waves.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Parameter | Richter Scale | Mercalli Scale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Measures** | **Magnitude** (Total energy released) | **Intensity** (Visible destruction and shaking) |
| **Data Type** | Quantitative & Mathematical | Qualitative & Observational |
| **Scale Numbers** | Decimals & Whole numbers (e.g., 6.4, 7.8) | Roman Numerals (**I to XII**) |
| **Location Dependency**| **Same single value** everywhere | **Changes with distance** from epicenter |

> - **R-M-E** $\rightarrow$ **R**ichter measures **M**agnitude (**E**nergy)
> - **M-I-D** $\rightarrow$ **M**ercalli measures **I**ntensity (**D**amage)
