# 📌 Father of Communal Electorate in India (Lord Minto)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Lord Minto** (Lord Minto II).
- **Father of Communal Electorate**: **Lord Minto**, who served as Viceroy of India from **1905 to 1910**, is known as the *"Father of Communal Electorate"* because he legalized communal representation in Indian politics.
- **Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)**: Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims**, where Muslim representatives could only be voted for by Muslim voters in designated seats.
- **Historical Impact**: Sowed the institutional seeds of communalism and political division between Hindus and Muslims, which later played a major role in the partition of India in 1947.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)

*Background, key provisions, and consequences:*

- **The Designers**:
  - **John Morley**: The Secretary of State for India (based in London).
  - **Lord Minto**: The Viceroy and Governor-General of India (in Calcutta/Delhi).
- **Introduction of Separate Electorates**:
  - In October 1906, a delegation of Muslim elites led by the **Aga Khan** met Lord Minto at Shimla (**Shimla Deputation**), demanding separate representation for Muslims based on their political importance rather than mere numbers.
  - Minto accepted their demands, and the 1909 Act formally introduced **separate communal electorates** for Muslims.
- **Expansion of Legislative Councils**:
  - Increased the number of members in the Central Legislative Council from 16 to 60.
  - Allowed members to ask supplementary questions and move resolutions on the budget.
- **First Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council**:
  - **Satyendra Prasanna Sinha (SP Sinha)** became the first Indian appointed to the Viceroy's Executive Council (appointed as the Law Member).

---

### 2. Analysis of the Other Figures in the Options

- **Lord Morley**:
  - Served as the **Secretary of State for India** during the 1909 reforms. While he co-authored the reforms, the direct policy push and acceptance of the communal electorate in India was led by Lord Minto.
- **Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)**:
  - Viceroy during the enactment of the **Government of India Act 1919** (**Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**).
  - Introduced **Dyarchy** (dual governance) in the provinces and expanded separate electorates to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans.
- **Lord Metcalfe (1835–1836)**:
  - Acting Governor-General of India famously known as the **"Liberator of the Indian Press"** for repealing the restrictive 1823 Press Regulations through the Press Act of 1835.
