# 📌 Preamble to the Constitution of India & Famous Opinions

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **N. A. Palkhivala**.
- **The Description**: **Nanabhoy "Nani" Palkhivala**, an eminent jurist and constitutional expert, famously described the Preamble as the **"Identity Card of the Constitution"**.
- **Foundational Root**: The Preamble is based on the historic **'Objectives Resolution'** drafted and moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** in the Constituent Assembly on **13 December 1946** (unanimously adopted on **22 January 1947**).
- **Amendability**: Amended only **once** so far by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, which added three new terms: **"Socialist"**, **"Secular"**, and **"Integrity"**.

### 2. Landmark Supreme Court Cases on the Preamble

*Evolution of the Preamble's legal status as an integral part of the Constitution:*

- **1. Berubari Union Case (1960)**:
  - The Supreme Court held that the Preamble is a key to open the mind of the makers, but it is **NOT a part of the Constitution**.
- **2. Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**:
  - The Supreme Court reversed its earlier stand and ruled that the Preamble is an **integral part of the Constitution**.
  - Held that the Preamble can be amended under **Article 368**, provided the **'Basic Structure'** of the Constitution is not damaged or destroyed.
- **3. LIC of India Case (1995)**:
  - Re-affirmed that the Preamble is an **integral part of the Constitution**.
- **Non-Justiciable Nature**: The provisions of the Preamble are **non-justiciable** and non-enforceable in courts of law (it is neither a source of power to the legislature nor a prohibition upon legislative powers).

Which of the following terms was NOT added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976?

- [ ] Socialist
- [ ] Secular
- [ ] Integrity
- [x] Sovereign

> The word "Sovereign" was part of the original Preamble adopted in 1949. The 42nd Amendment Act of 1976 added the words "Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity".

- [ ] 9 December 1946

> Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the 'Objectives Resolution' on 13 December 1946, which was subsequently adopted by the Assembly on 22 January 1947.
