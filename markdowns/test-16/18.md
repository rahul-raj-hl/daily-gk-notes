# 📌 Types of Writs & Constitutional Remedies (Articles 32 & 226)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Constitutional Basis**: 
  - **Supreme Court**: Empowered under **Article 32** (*Right to Constitutional Remedies*, called the *"Heart and Soul of the Constitution"* by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar).
  - **High Courts**: Empowered under **Article 226** (broader scope: can issue writs for Fundamental Rights as well as ordinary legal rights).
- **Parliamentary Power**: Under **Article 32(3)**, Parliament can empower any other court to issue writs, though no such law has been enacted so far.
- **The 5 Types of Writs & Literal Meanings**:
  1. **Habeas Corpus** $\rightarrow$ *"To have the body of"* / *"To present a person"*
  2. **Mandamus** $\rightarrow$ *"We Command"*
  3. **Prohibition** $\rightarrow$ *"To forbid"*
  4. **Certiorari** $\rightarrow$ *"To be certified"* / *"To be informed"*
  5. **Quo-Warranto** $\rightarrow$ *"By what authority or warrant"*

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Detailed Breakdown of the 5 Writs

*Scope, targets, and limitations of each constitutional writ:*

#### A. Habeas Corpus ("To Have the Body of")
- **Purpose**: Protects individual liberty against arbitrary and unlawful detention.
- **Mechanism**: The court directs the detaining authority to produce the detained person before it to examine the legality of detention. If unlawful, the person is set free immediately.
- **Applicability**: Can be issued against **both public authorities and private individuals**.
- **When NOT Issued**: When detention is lawful, for contempt of court/legislature, by a competent court, or outside jurisdiction.

#### B. Mandamus ("We Command")
- **Purpose**: A command issued to a public official, public body, corporation, inferior court, tribunal, or government asking them to perform a mandatory statutory/public duty they have failed or refused to perform.
- **Applicability**: Issued only against **public authorities**.
- **When NOT Issued**: Against a private individual/body, to enforce discretionary (non-mandatory) duty or contractual obligations, against the **President of India** or **State Governors**, or against a Chief Justice acting in judicial capacity.

#### C. Prohibition ("To Forbid")
- **Purpose**: Issued by a higher court (Supreme Court/High Court) to an inferior court or tribunal to **prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction** or usurping powers it does not possess.
- **Nature**: Purely **preventive** (issued while proceedings are pending; directs *inactivity*).
- **Applicability**: Available only against **judicial and quasi-judicial authorities** (not against administrative bodies, legislative bodies, or private individuals).

#### D. Certiorari ("To Be Certified" / "To Be Informed")
- **Purpose**: Issued by a higher court to quash an order already passed by a lower court/tribunal or transfer a pending case due to excess/lack of jurisdiction or error of law on the face of the record.
- **Nature**: **Both preventive and curative**.
- **Applicability**: Originally only against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies; since 1991 (SC ruling), it can also be issued against **administrative authorities** affecting rights of individuals.

#### E. Quo-Warranto ("By What Authority or Warrant")
- **Purpose**: Enquires into the legality of a person's claim to a public office to prevent illegal usurpation of public office.
- **Condition**: Can be issued only against a **substantive public office of permanent character** created by statute or Constitution.
- **When NOT Issued**: Cannot be issued against ministerial offices or private offices.
- **Special Feature**: Can be sought by **any interested person**, not necessarily the aggrieved party.

### 2. Writ Jurisdiction Comparison: Supreme Court (Art 32) vs. High Court (Art 226)

| Parameter | Supreme Court (Article 32) | High Court (Article 226) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope of Rights** | Enforces **Fundamental Rights only** (Part III) | Enforces **Fundamental Rights AND ordinary legal rights** |
| **Territorial Reach** | Entire territory of India | Within its territorial state jurisdiction (or cause of action) |
| **Constitutional Nature** | Article 32 is itself a **Fundamental Right** (SC cannot refuse remedy) | Article 226 is a **Discretionary Remedy** (HC may refuse) |
| **Breadth of Power** | Narrower in scope, but broader in territorial reach | **Broader in scope**, but narrower in territorial reach |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Writ Pair / Concept | Key Difference | Critical Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prohibition vs. Certiorari** | Prohibition is **only preventive** (during pendency); Certiorari is **both preventive & curative** (quashes completed orders). | Prohibition stops action; Certiorari quashes completed decision. |
| **Mandamus vs. Quo-Warranto** | Mandamus forces an official to **perform a duty**; Quo-Warranto questions **their right to hold the office**. | Quo-Warranto does not require the petitioner to be personally aggrieved. |
| **Mandamus Immunity** | Cannot be issued against the **President** or **Governors** for official duties. | Article 361 provides personal immunity. |
| **Private Entity Application** | **Only Habeas Corpus** can be issued against a private individual. | Mandamus, Prohibition, and Certiorari apply to public/judicial bodies. |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "H-M-P-C-Q $\rightarrow$ BODY, COMMAND, FORBID, CERTIFY, AUTHORITY"**
> - **H**abeas Corpus $\rightarrow$ **Body** (Produce the person)
> - **M**andamus $\rightarrow$ **Command** (Do your public duty)
> - **P**rohibition $\rightarrow$ **Forbid** (Stop exceeding power)
> - **C**ertiorari $\rightarrow$ **Certify / Quash** (Correct the judgment)
> - **Q**uo-Warranto $\rightarrow$ **Authority** (Show your qualification/warrant)

Match the following writs with their literal meanings:
1. Habeas Corpus — To have the body of
2. Mandamus — We command
3. Prohibition — To be certified
4. Quo-Warranto — By what authority
5. Certiorari — To forbid

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- [ ] 1, 2 and 3 only
- [x] 1, 2 and 4 only
- [ ] 2, 3 and 5 only
- [ ] 1, 4 and 5 only

> Habeas Corpus ("To have the body of"), Mandamus ("We command"), and Quo-Warranto ("By what authority") are correctly matched. Prohibition means "To forbid" and Certiorari means "To be certified".

### PYQ 2

Which of the following writs can be issued against BOTH public authorities and private individuals?

- [x] Habeas Corpus
- [ ] Mandamus
- [ ] Prohibition
- [ ] Quo-Warranto

> The writ of Habeas Corpus is the only writ that can be issued against both public officials and private individuals who have unlawfully detained someone.

---

### PYQ 3

Which writ is issued by a superior court to prevent an inferior court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction?

- [ ] Mandamus
- [x] Prohibition
- [ ] Certiorari
- [ ] Quo-Warranto

> The writ of Prohibition is issued to prevent a lower court or tribunal from usurping or exceeding its judicial jurisdiction.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Under which Article of the Constitution of India can the High Courts issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights as well as legal rights?

- [ ] Article 32
- [ ] Article 131
- [x] Article 226
- [ ] Article 143

> Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for any other ordinary legal purpose.

---

### Q2

The writ of 'Mandamus' CANNOT be issued against which of the following authorities?

- [ ] A Municipal Corporation
- [ ] An inferior tribunal
- [x] The President of India or State Governors
- [ ] A public department secretary

> Under constitutional conventions and Article 361, Mandamus cannot be issued against the President of India or State Governors for the discharge of their official functions.

---

### Q3

Which of the following writs does NOT require the petitioner to be an "aggrieved person" (allowing any citizen to file in public interest)?

- [ ] Mandamus
- [ ] Habeas Corpus
- [ ] Certiorari
- [x] Quo-Warranto

> The writ of Quo-Warranto can be moved by any interested person to challenge unlawful occupation of a public office, without needing to show personal injury.

---

### Q4

In which landmark case did the Supreme Court hold that judicial review of administrative actions via writ jurisdiction is an integral part of the 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution?

- [ ] Shankari Prasad Case (1951)
- [ ] Golaknath Case (1967)
- [x] Kesavananda Bharati / L. Chandra Kumar Case (1997)
- [ ] Berubari Union Case (1960)

> In the *L. Chandra Kumar Case (1997)*, the Supreme Court affirmed that the writ jurisdictions of the Supreme Court (Art 32) and High Courts (Art 226) form part of the inviolable Basic Structure.
