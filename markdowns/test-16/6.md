# 📌 The Rolling Plan & Five Year Plans of India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Fifth five year plan**.
- **Context of Termination**: The **Fifth Five Year Plan (1974–1979)** was officially terminated one year ahead of schedule in **1977–78** by the newly elected **Janata Party Government** led by Prime Minister **Morarji Desai**.
- **Implementation of the Rolling Plan (1978–1980)**: The Janata Government rejected the conventional 5-year fixed format and launched a **Rolling Plan for 1978–1983**.
- **Conceptual Origin**: Propounded by Swedish Nobel laureate economist **Gunnar Myrdal** in his seminal work *Asian Drama: An Inquiry into the Poverty of Nations*, and implemented in India under Planning Commission Deputy Chairman **Prof. D.T. Lakdawala**.
- **Scrapping**: In **1980**, when the Indian National Congress returned to power, the Rolling Plan was scrapped and a fresh **Sixth Five Year Plan (1980–1985)** was enacted.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Mechanism of a Rolling Plan

*A continuous, dynamic economic model that avoids rigid multi-year targets:*

- **Annual Re-assessment**: Every single year, the performance of the economy is assessed against previous targets.
- **Dynamic Re-allocation**: Based on the annual evaluation, both the financial allocations and physical targets are updated and rolled forward into the subsequent year's plan.
- **Core Advantage**: Offers high **macroeconomic flexibility** to absorb economic shocks, such as monsoon failure or inflation spikes, without abandoning the entire long-term roadmap.

---

### 2. Overview of Planning Gaps in India

*Historical interruptions in India's Five Year Plan timeline:*

- **1. Plan Holiday (1966–1969)**: 
  - Occurred after the failure of the **Third Five Year Plan**.
  - Triggered by consecutive wars (1962 Sino-Indian War, 1965 Indo-Pak War) and severe countrywide droughts.
  - Replaced by three successive Annual Plans.
- **2. The Rolling Plan (1978–1980)**:
  - Occurred after the premature termination of the **Fifth Five Year Plan** by the Janata Party.
  - Replaced fixed targets with rolling annual revisions.
- **3. Annual Plans (1990–1992)**:
  - Occurred between the **Seventh** and **Eighth Five Year Plans**.
  - Triggered by political instability and the severe Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis leading up to the 1991 LPG reforms.
