# 📌 Ancient Indian Schools of Art: Mathura, Gandhara & Amaravati

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Mathura School of Art**.
- **Indigenous Character**: Developed **completely indigenously** without major foreign Hellenistic influence, retaining deep Indian aesthetic traditions (fleshy bodies, transparent drapery, smiling countenance).
- **Tri-Religious Influence**: The only prominent post-Mauryan school to produce sculptures and imagery across all three major contemporary Indian religions: **Buddhism**, **Hinduism**, and **Jainism**.
- **Material Used**: Exclusively utilized **spotted red sandstone** quarried from Sikri/Mathura.
- **Imperial Patronage**: Flourished under the patronage of the **Kushan rulers** (1st century BCE to 3rd century CE) alongside the Gandhara school.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Tri-Religious Synthesis of the Mathura School

*Religious traditions represented in Mathura art:*

- **Buddhism**:
  - Depicted the Buddha in human form (Mahayana phase) with shaved head/curled hair, fleshy physical form, broad shoulders, right hand in *Abhay Mudra* (fearlessness), and smiling expressions.
  - Accompanied by **Bodhisattvas** such as **Padmapani** (holding a lotus) and **Vajrapani** (holding a thunderbolt).
  - Head halos (*Prabhavali*) decorated with intricate geometric, floral, and solar patterns.
- **Hinduism**:
  - Sculpted the earliest iconic and anthropomorphic representations of Hindu deities:
    - **Shiva**: Represented both in human form and as *Mukhalinga* (linga with faces).
    - **Vishnu**: Depicted with four arms holding attributes (*Sankha*, *Chakra*, *Gada*, *Padma*), alongside *Avataras* like Varaha and Krishna.
    - **Goddesses & Others**: Images of Lakshmi, Durga as *Mahishasuramardini*, Surya, Kartikeya, and Balarama.
- **Jainism**:
  - Produced numerous monolithic images of **Jain Tirthankaras** (such as Rishabhanatha, Parshvanatha, and Mahavira) in meditation (*Dhyana Mudra*) and standing (*Kayotsarga*) postures.
  - Famous for **Ayagapattas** (votive stone tablets used for worship/donation) discovered at **Kankali Tila** in Mathura.

---

### 2. Comparison: Gandhara vs. Mathura vs. Amaravati Schools

*Key characteristics across the three major schools:*

| Feature | Gandhara School | Mathura School | Amaravati School |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Geographical Region** | Northwest Frontier (Peshawar, Taxila, Swat) | Mathura, Sonkh, Kankali Tila (UP) | Krishna-Godavari Basin, Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda (AP) |
| **External Influence** | **Greco-Roman / Hellenistic** | **Purely Indigenous** | **Purely Indigenous** |
| **Religious Focus** | **Exclusively Buddhist** (Mahayana) | **Buddhism + Hinduism + Jainism** | **Exclusively Buddhist** (Hinayana & Mahayana) |
| **Primary Material** | **Bluish-grey slate / Schist stone**; Stucco | **Spotted Red Sandstone** | **White Marble / Palnad Limestone** |
| **Patron Dynasties** | **Kushans** (Kanishka, Huvishka) | **Kushans** (and later Guptas) | **Satavahanas** and **Ikshvakus** |
| **Physical Features of Buddha** | Greek god Apollo-like facial features, sharp nose, wavy hair, realistic heavy drapery, ascetic calm | Fleshy cheeks, smiling face, tight/transparent diaphanous drapery, patterned halo | Focus on narrative relief panels depicting Jataka tales; dynamic human postures |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "MATHURA = ALL THREE RELIGIONS ON RED STONE"**
> - **M**athura $\rightarrow$ **M**ulti-religious (Buddhist, Hindu, Jain)
> - **R**ed Sandstone $\rightarrow$ Indigenous Indian roots
> - **G**andhara $\rightarrow$ **G**reco-Roman **G**rey slate (Only Buddhist)

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

Which of the following Schools of Art has more Indian features as compared to others and is influenced by Buddhism, Hinduism, and Jainism?

- [ ] Gandhara
- [x] Mathura
- [ ] Greek
- [ ] Amaravati

> The Mathura School developed indigenously and catered to the iconography of all three contemporary religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism.

---

### PYQ 2

The Gandhara School of Art was a synthesis of Indian art traditions with which foreign art style?

- [ ] Persian (Achaemenid)
- [x] Greco-Roman (Hellenistic)
- [ ] Chinese
- [ ] Egyptian

> Gandhara art is often termed the "Greco-Buddhist" school because it rendered Buddhist themes using Greek and Roman anatomical realism and drapery.

---

### PYQ 3

Which type of material was predominantly used by sculptors of the Amaravati School of Art?

- [ ] Spotted Red Sandstone
- [ ] Grey Schist
- [x] White Marble / Limestone
- [ ] Granite

> The artists of the Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda stupas primarily worked with white marble/Palnad limestone for carving relief panels.
