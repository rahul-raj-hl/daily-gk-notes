# 📌 Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Option**: **2 and 3 only**.
- **Context & Key Figures**: Enacted during the tenure of **Lord Morley** (Secretary of State for India) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy of India).
- **Statement Analysis**:
  - **Statement 1 is Incorrect**: It did **NOT** introduce a responsible government. The executive remained completely unaccountable to the legislature. Responsible government elements were first introduced later by the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act 1919)**.
  - **Statement 2 is Correct**: It gave Indians **"a shadow rather than substance"** (mere consultative powers with no real executive control or legislative veto over the colonial administration).
  - **Statement 3 is Correct**: It legally institutionalized communalism by introducing a **Separate Electorate for Muslims**, which effectively sowed the seed for the eventual partition of India. Lord Minto is therefore known as the **"Father of Communal Electorate"**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Provisions of the Indian Councils Act 1909

- **Introduction of Communal Representation (Separate Electorates)**:
  - Muslim members were to be elected exclusively by Muslim voters.
  - Legalized communalism as a state policy to divide the nationalist movement (*"Divide and Rule"*).
- **Expansion of Legislative Councils**:
  - The size of the **Central Legislative Council** was increased from 16 to **60 members**.
  - Retained an **official majority** in the Central Legislative Council, but allowed non-official majorities in Provincial Legislative Councils.
- **Enlargement of Legislative Functions**:
  - Members were allowed to ask **supplementary questions** and move resolutions on the Budget (though they could not vote on the budget as a whole).
- **First Indian in Viceroy’s Executive Council**:
  - **Satyendra Prasad Sinha (S.P. Sinha)** became the first Indian to join the Viceroy’s Executive Council, appointed as the **Law Member**.
  - Two Indians (K.G. Gupta and Syed Hussain Bilgrami) were also nominated to the Council of the Secretary of State in London.

---

### 2. Critique and Historical Significance

- **Lord Morley’s Confession**: Writing to Lord Minto, Morley famously remarked: *"We are sowing dragon’s teeth and the harvest will be bitter."*
- **No Path to Democracy**: Lord Morley explicitly stated in the British Parliament that if this Act was associated with establishing a parliamentary system in India, he would have nothing to do with it.
- **Indirect & Restricted Franchise**: The franchise was extremely limited and based on high property qualifications, with representation given to special interest groups like landlords, chambers of commerce, and universities.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Viceroy / Sec. of State** | **Lord Minto** (Viceroy) & **Lord Morley** (SoS) | **Lord Chelmsford** (Viceroy) & **Edwin Montagu** (SoS) |
| **Communal Electorate** | Introduced for **Muslims only** | Extended to **Sikhs, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians & Europeans** |
| **Executive System** | No responsible government; autocratic | Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces** (Transferred vs. Reserved subjects) |
| **Bicameralism** | Unicameral central legislature | Introduced **Bicameral Legislature** at the Centre (Council of State + Legislative Assembly) |
| **Executive Council Entry** | **S.P. Sinha** (1st Indian as Law Member) | 3 of 6 members in Viceroy's Council to be Indians (excluding Commander-in-Chief) |
