# 📌 The Gupta Empire: Chandragupta II & The Golden Age of India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Chandragupta II** (also known by his title **Vikramaditya**).
- **The Golden Age of India**: The Gupta era ($\approx 320\text{ to }550\text{ CE}$) is called the "Golden Age" or "Classical Age" of ancient India due to unprecedented advancements in **Sanskrit literature, mathematics, astronomy, metallurgy, sculpture, and visual arts**.
- **Cultural Apex under Chandragupta II (c. 380–415 CE)**: The empire reached its cultural and artistic pinnacle during his reign.
- **Navaratnas (Nine Gems)**: His royal court at Ujjain patronized nine extraordinary scholars and litterateurs, led by the greatest Sanskrit poet **Kalidasa**.
- **Foreign Account**: The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim **Faxian (Fa-Hien)** visited India during his reign (c. 399–412 CE), documenting peace, economic prosperity, and mild penal laws.
- **Military Achievement**: Eradicated the last Western Kshatrapas (Sakas) of Malwa and Saurashtra, assuming the title ***Sakari*** (Destroyer of Sakas) and ***Vikramaditya***.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Chronology of Major Gupta Rulers

- **1. Sri Gupta & Ghatotkacha**: Early ancestors titled *Maharaja*.
- **2. Chandragupta I (c. 319–335 CE)**:
  - Considered the **real founder** of the Gupta Empire; assumed the imperial title ***Maharajadhiraja***.
  - Started the **Gupta Samvat (319–320 CE)** upon his coronation.
  - Married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi (issued King-Queen type gold coins).
- **3. Samudragupta (c. 335–380 CE)**:
  - Known as the **"Napoleon of India"** (titled by historian V.A. Smith) due to his undefeated military campaigns recorded on the **Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription)** composed by court poet **Harisena**.
  - Patron of music; depicted playing the veena on gold coins; performed *Ashvamedha Yajna*.
- **4. Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya (c. 380–415 CE)**:
  - Son of Samudragupta and Datta Devi; brother of Ramagupta.
  - Married Naga princess Kuberanaga; married his daughter **Prabhavatigupta** to the Vakataka king Rudrasena II (securing Deccan alliance).
  - Established a secondary administrative/cultural capital at **Ujjain**.
  - Issued the first **silver coins (*Rupaka*)** among Gupta rulers following the conquest of the Sakas.
- **5. Kumaragupta I (Mahendraditya) (c. 415–455 CE)**:
  - Founded the world-renowned **Nalanda Mahavihara (Nalanda University)** in modern Bihar.
  - Performed *Ashvamedha* sacrifices and issued the maximum number of inscriptions among Gupta rulers.
- **6. Skandagupta (c. 455–467 CE)**:
  - Last great Gupta emperor; assumed the title ***Kramaditya***.
  - Successfully defended the empire against the savage invasion of the **White Hunas (Ephthalites)**.
  - Repaired the embankment of **Sudarshana Lake** in Saurashtra (Junagadh Inscription) through governor Parnadatta and his son Chakrapalita.

### 2. The Nine Gems (Navaratnas) of Chandragupta II

| Navaratna | Field / Domain | Masterpiece / Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kalidasa** | Classical Sanskrit Literature | Plays: *Abhijnanashakuntalam*, *Malavikagnimitram*, *Vikramorvashiyam*; Epics: *Raghuvamsha*, *Kumarasambhava*; Poems: *Meghaduta*, *Ritusamhara* |
| **Varahamihira** | Astronomy & Astrology | *Brihat Samhita*, *Pancha Siddhantika*, *Brihat Jataka* |
| **Amarasimha** | Sanskrit Lexicography | *Amarakosha* (Sanskrit Thesaurus/Vocabulary) |
| **Dhanvantari** | Medicine & Ayurveda | Known as the father of Ayurveda in Gupta traditions |
| **Vararuchi** | Grammar & Linguistics | *Prakrita Prakasha* (Earliest Prakrit Grammar) |
| **Shanku** | Architecture (*Vastu Shastra*) | Treatises on classical building and structural design |
| **Kshapanka** | Astrology | Astrological calculations and horoscopes |
| **Ghatakarpara** | Poetry & Sculpture | Sanskrit lyrical poetry |
| **Vetala Bhatta** | Occult Science / Magic | *Mantra Shastra* (attributed) |

*(Note: The mathematician-astronomer **Aryabhata** (*Aryabhatiya*, *Surya Siddhanta*) lived during the later Gupta period under Buddhagupta/Kumaragupta, not in Chandragupta II's immediate court circle).*
