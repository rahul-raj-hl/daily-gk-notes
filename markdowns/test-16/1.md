# 📌 Joule's Law of Heating & Electrical Resistance

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Half**.
- **Fundamental Law**: Governed by **Joule's Law of Heating**, which states that heat produced in a conductor is given by:

$$H = I^2 R t$$

- **Mathematical Relationship**:
  - For a given constant electric current ($I$) flowing over a specific time ($t$), heat produced is **directly proportional to electrical resistance** ($H \propto R$).
  - If resistance ($R$) is reduced to half ($R' = \frac{R}{2}$):

$$H' = I^2 \left(\frac{R}{2}\right) t = \frac{H}{2} \quad \text{(Becomes Half)}$$

- **Key Distinction**:
  - At **constant current** (Series connection): $H \propto R \implies$ Halving $R$ **halves** the heat.
  - At **constant voltage** (Parallel connection): $H = \frac{V^2}{R}t \implies H \propto \frac{1}{R} \implies$ Halving $R$ **doubles** the heat.
  *(In standard SSC/general science conceptual questions without a specified source, the direct statement of Joule's Law $H = I^2 R t$ is applied).*

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Joule's Law of Heating Explained

*Three direct proportionality relationships defined by James Prescott Joule:*

1. **Square of Current**: Heat generated is directly proportional to the square of the current passing through the conductor ($H \propto I^2$).
2. **Resistance**: Heat generated is directly proportional to the electrical resistance of the conductor ($H \propto R$).
3. **Time**: Heat generated is directly proportional to the duration for which the current flows ($H \propto t$).

Combining these gives:

$$H = I^2 R t \quad \text{Joules (J)}$$

Using Ohm's Law ($V = IR$):

$$H = V I t = \frac{V^2}{R} t$$

---

### 2. Practical Heating Elements & Materials

*Materials designed to maximize heating effect ($H = I^2 R t$):*

- **Nichrome (Alloy of Nickel 80% + Chromium 20%)**:
  - Used in: Electric heaters, geysers, toasters, and irons.
  - Key properties: **Very high electrical resistivity** and **high melting point**; does not oxidize (burn) easily at high temperatures (up to 1400°C).
- **Tungsten**:
  - Used in: Incandescent light bulb filaments.
  - Key properties: Extremely high melting point ($\approx 3422^\circ\text{C}$); glows white-hot without melting.
- **Electric Fuse (Alloy of Lead 37% + Tin 63%)**:
  - Used as a safety device in series with live wire.
  - Key properties: **High resistance** and **low melting point** so that it melts quickly during overcurrent/short circuits.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resistance is Halved ($R/2$)** | **Heat is Halved ($H/2$)** | $H \propto R$ |
| **Resistance is Doubled ($2R$)** | **Heat is Doubled ($2H$)** | $H \propto R$ |

| **Current is Halved ($I/2$)** | **Heat becomes One-Fourth ($H/4$)**| $H \propto I^2$ |
| **Time of flow is Doubled ($2t$)** | **Heat is Doubled ($2H$)** | $H \propto t$ |
| **Voltage constant, $R$ halved** | **Heat is Doubled ($2H$)** | $H \propto \frac{1}{R}$ (from $H = \frac{V^2}{R}t$) |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "CURRENT SQUARES, RESISTANCE SHARES (DIRECTLY)"**
> - **Current ($I$)** $\rightarrow$ Squared ($I^2 \implies 2\times I \to 4\times H$)
> - **Resistance ($R$)** $\rightarrow$ Direct ($R/2 \to H/2$)

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

If the resistance of a conductor is reduced to half its initial value, then the heating effect in the conductor will become:

- [ ] One-fourth
- [x] Half
- [ ] Double
- [ ] Four-times

> According to Joule's Law of Heating ($H = I^2 R t$), the heat produced is directly proportional to the resistance ($R$). Halving the resistance reduces the heat produced to half.

---

### PYQ 2

If the current flowing through a fixed resistor is doubled, the rate of heat dissipation in the resistor will increase by a factor of:

- [ ] 2
- [x] 4
- [ ] 8
- [ ] 16

> Since $H \propto I^2$, doubling the current ($2I$) results in $(2)^2 = 4$ times the rate of heat dissipation.

---

### PYQ 3

Which of the following materials is preferred for making heating elements in appliances like electric toasters and room heaters?

- [ ] Copper
- [ ] Aluminium
- [x] Nichrome
- [ ] Lead

> Nichrome has high resistivity and a very high melting point, resisting oxidation (burning) even at glowing red-hot temperatures.

An electric fuse wire melts and breaks an overloaded circuit because it has:

- [ ] Low resistance and high melting point
- [x] High resistance and low melting point
- [ ] Low resistance and low melting point
- [ ] High resistance and high melting point

How much energy in Joules is equivalent to $1\text{ Kilowatt-hour (kWh)}$?

### Q3

Two resistors of resistances $R$ and $2R$ are connected in series across a constant DC voltage supply. Which resistor will generate more heat?

- [x] The resistor of resistance $2R$
- [ ] The resistor of resistance $R$
- [ ] Both will generate equal heat
- [ ] Depends on the room temperature

> In a series combination, the current ($I$) is identical through both resistors. Since $H = I^2 R t$, the resistor with larger resistance ($2R$) generates twice as much heat.
