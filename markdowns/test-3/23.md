# 📌 Jamini Roy & Modern Indian Art (Bengal Folk Style)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Statement 1 is Correct**: Like traditional village artisans, Jamini Roy made his own natural colours from **vegetables, plants, and minerals** (*such as lampblack, chalk, and natural earth pigments*) instead of using commercial European paints.
- **Statement 2 is Correct**: Although he adopted the folk art idiom and worked with workshop assistants (similar to village patuas), **he signed his paintings**, which differentiated his work as individual modern art from traditional anonymous village folk art.
- **Key Influence**: Inspired by the traditional **Kalighat Patachitra** (pottery and scroll painting) of rural Bengal.
- **Teacher**: Studied under **Abanindranath Tagore** at the Government School of Art, Calcutta.
- **Recognition**: Honoured with the **Padma Bhushan** in 1954 and declared one of the Nine Masters (*Navaratnas*) by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Statement Analysis

- **Statement 1: He made his own colours from vegetables and minerals.**
  - *Correct.* Rejecting Western oil paints, Roy returned to indigenous materials used by Bengal folk artists, making pigments from natural mineral earth, rock dust, plants, and vegetables bound with tamarind seed glue or egg white.
- **Statement 2: His signature on his paintings differentiated his art from that of village artists.**
  - *Correct.* Traditional folk art in rural India was collective and anonymous. Jamini Roy adopted the workshop style where assistants helped produce copies, but he signed each completed painting, establishing individual authorship in the modern art tradition.

---

### 2. Art Style and Philosophy of Jamini Roy

*Rejection of Western Academic Realism in favor of Folk Primitivism.*

- **Rejection of Academic Art**: Trained in Western realism, he later rejected academic naturalism to seek inspiration in the simple, flat, bold lines of Bengali folk art.
- **Kalighat Influence**: Adopted bold sweeping outlines, almond-shaped eyes, flat color fields, and simplified forms characteristic of Kalighat patuas.
- **Popular Themes**:
  - Rural Bengali folk life (*Gopinis, Santhal women, mothers and children*).
  - Indian Mythology (*Ramayana scenes, Krishna Leela*).
  - Life of Jesus Christ (*rendered in flat folk style*).
