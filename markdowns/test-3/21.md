# 📌 Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution (Distribution of Legislative Powers)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Article 246** and the **Seventh Schedule** divide legislative powers between the Union and State governments into **three lists**:
  1. **Union List (List I)**: Central government holds exclusive authority.
  2. **State List (List II)**: State governments hold exclusive authority.
  3. **Concurrent List (List III)**: Both Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws.
- **Criminal Law** lies in the **Concurrent List (Entry 1)**, *not* the Union List.
- **Agriculture** lies in the **State List (Entry 14)**.
- **Intra-state Trade and Commerce** lies in the **State List (Entry 26)**.
- Therefore, **Statements 2 and 3 are correct**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Statement Analysis

*Evaluating each statement given in the question:*

- **Statement 1: Criminal law lies in Union List.**
  - *Incorrect.* Criminal Law (including all matters included in the Indian Penal Code/BNS) falls under **Concurrent List (List III, Entry 1)**. This allows both Union and State legislatures to pass criminal laws and amendments.
- **Statement 2: Agriculture is a State subject.**
  - *Correct.* Agriculture, including agricultural education and research, protection against pests, and plant diseases, is placed under **State List (List II, Entry 14)**.
- **Statement 3: Intra-state trade is in State List.**
  - *Correct.* Trade and commerce within the state (intra-state trade) fall under **State List (List II, Entry 26)**. *(Note: Inter-state trade falls under Union List Entry 42).*

---

### 2. Breakdown of the Three Lists (Seventh Schedule)

*Categorization of constitutional subjects:*

- **Union List (List I)**:
  - Subjects of national importance requiring uniform legislation across India.
  - *Examples*: Defence, Foreign Affairs, Banking, Currency, Atomic Energy, Railways, Inter-state Trade.
- **State List (List II)**:
  - Subjects of local/regional importance.
  - *Examples*: Public Order, Police, Public Health & Sanitation, Agriculture, Irrigation, Intra-state Trade.
- **Concurrent List (List III)**:
  - Subjects of common interest to both Union and States.
  - *Examples*: Criminal Law, Criminal Procedure, Marriage & Divorce, Education, Forests, Succession, Contracts.
  - *Conflict Provision*: In case of conflict between Union and State law on a Concurrent subject, the **Union law prevails** (Article 254).

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Police & Public Order** | **State List** (List II) | Primary state subject |

| **Inter-state Trade** | **Union List** (List I) | Trade across state boundaries |

Under which Schedule of the Indian Constitution is the distribution of powers between the Union and the States specified?

### PYQ 3

Which of the following subjects was transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976?

- [ ] Public Health
- [x] Education
- [ ] Agriculture
- [ ] Police

> The 42nd Amendment (1976) transferred 5 subjects (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of Wild Animals/Birds, Administration of Justice) from State List to Concurrent List.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

According to Article 248 of the Indian Constitution, who holds the 'Residuary Powers' to make laws on subjects not mentioned in any of the three lists?

- [ ] State Legislatures
- [x] Union Parliament
- [ ] Supreme Court of India
- [ ] President of India

> Residuary powers (matters not listed in List I, II, or III) are vested exclusively in the Union Parliament under Article 248.

---

### Q2

'Public Order' and 'Police' are subjects listed under which list in the Seventh Schedule?

- [ ] Union List
- [x] State List
- [ ] Concurrent List
- [ ] Residuary List

> Both Public Order and Police are placed under the State List (List II).

---

### Q3

Under Article 249 of the Indian Constitution, who can authorize Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the national interest?

- [ ] Lok Sabha
- [x] Rajya Sabha
- [ ] Governor
- [ ] Prime Minister

> Rajya Sabha can pass a resolution supported by two-thirds majority authorizing Parliament to legislate on a State subject in the national interest under Article 249.

---

### Q4

Which of the following subjects falls under the Union List of the Indian Constitution?

- [ ] Agriculture
- [ ] Public Health and Sanitation
- [x] Atomic Energy and Foreign Affairs
- [ ] Trade and Commerce within state

> Atomic Energy, Foreign Affairs, Defence, and Banking fall under the Union List (List I).

---

### Q5

In case of a conflict between a Central Law and a State Law on a subject in the Concurrent List, which law generally prevails?

- [ ] State Law always
- [x] Central Law prevails
- [ ] The law enacted earlier
- [ ] Both laws are declared void

> Under Article 254(1), the Central Law prevails over the State Law in case of conflict regarding a Concurrent List entry.
