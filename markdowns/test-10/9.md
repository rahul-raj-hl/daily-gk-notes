# 📌 Chhau Dance & Guru Tapan Kumar Pattanayak

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Chhau Dance**.
- **Awardee**: **Guru Tapan Kumar Pattanayak** was honored with the prestigious **Sangeet Natak Akademi Award** for his exceptional contribution to **Chhau dance** (specifically Seraikella Chhau).
- **Origins of Chhau**: A semi-classical martial and tribal dance form from Eastern India, practiced primarily in **Jharkhand**, **West Bengal**, and **Odisha**.
- **Etymology**: Derived from the Sanskrit root word *"Chhaya"*, which signifies **"shadow"** or **"mask"**.
- **UNESCO Recognition**: Inscribed on the **UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** in **2010**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Three Distinct Regional Styles of Chhau

*Characteristics, mask usage, and geographical distribution across Eastern India:*

- **1. Seraikella Chhau (Jharkhand)**:
  - Dancers wear stylized, symbolic, and lightweight masks that express specific moods and characters.
  - Characterized by delicate, lyrical body movements and theatrical storytelling.
  - Guru Tapan Kumar Pattanayak is one of the foremost masters of this tradition.
- **2. Purulia Chhau (West Bengal)**:
  - Known for large, vibrant, and ornate headgears/masks depicting mythological demons, deities, and warriors.
  - Highly acrobatic, athletic, and martial in nature, performed to the energetic rhythm of *Dhol*, *Dhumsa*, and *Shehnai*.
- **3. Mayurbhanj Chhau (Odisha)**:
  - **Does NOT use masks**; emotions are conveyed entirely through dynamic facial expressions, intricate footwork, and mock-combat postures (*Chalis* and *Topkas*).

---

### 2. Classical Dances in the Options

*Origins and core features of other dance forms:*

- **Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh)**:
  - Originated in the Krishna district (Kuchipudi village) of Andhra Pradesh.
  - Combines *Natya* (drama), *Nritta* (pure dance), and *Nritya* (expressive storytelling); incorporates elements of spoken dialogue and dancing on the rim of a brass plate (*Tarangam*).
- **Sattriya (Assam)**:
  - Created in the 15th century by the Vaishnavite saint-reformer **Srimanta Sankardeva** as a medium of worship in monastic institutions called *Sattras* and prayer halls (*Naamghars*).
  - Recognized as a classical dance by the Sangeet Natak Akademi in the year 2000.
- **Odissi (Odisha)**:
  - One of the oldest surviving dance traditions of India, deeply rooted in the temple sculptures of Odisha (Konark, Puri) and the *Mahari* / *Gotipua* traditions.
  - Characterized by the iconic **Tribhanga** posture (three-bend body stance) and **Chowka** (square stance).
