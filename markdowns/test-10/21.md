# 📌 Reactivity Series of Metals & Native State Elements

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Gold and platinum**.
- **What is Native State?**: When a metal is found in nature in its **pure, free, elemental form** rather than combined as an oxide, sulphide, or carbonate ore.
- **Why are Gold and Platinum in Native State?**: They lie at the **bottom of the reactivity series** (least reactive / noble metals), making them highly resistant to corrosion, oxidation, water, and atmospheric gases.
- **Why Sodium and Potassium are NEVER in Native State**: They are at the **very top of the reactivity series** (most reactive metals) and react vigorously with moisture and oxygen, so they are always found in combined states (e.g., halides, carbonates).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Reactivity Series & Natural Occurrence

The reactivity series arranges metals in decreasing order of their chemical reactivity:

- **High Reactivity Metals (Top of Series)**:
  - **Metals**: Potassium ($\text{K}$), Sodium ($\text{Na}$), Calcium ($\text{Ca}$), Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$), Aluminium ($\text{Al}$).
  - **Natural Form**: Never found in free/native state; always occur as combined compounds (oxides, chlorides, silicates).
  - **Extraction Method**: Extracted via **electrolytic reduction** of molten salts.
- **Moderate Reactivity Metals (Middle of Series)**:
  - **Metals**: Zinc ($\text{Zn}$), Iron ($\text{Fe}$), Lead ($\text{Pb}$), Copper ($\text{Cu}$).
  - **Natural Form**: Occur mainly as oxides, sulphides, or carbonates.
  - **Extraction Method**: Reduced using **carbon / carbon monoxide** (calcination and roasting).
- **Low Reactivity / Noble Metals (Bottom of Series)**:
  - **Metals**: Silver ($\text{Ag}$), Gold ($\text{Au}$), Platinum ($\text{Pt}$).
  - **Natural Form**: Found primarily in the **free / native state** due to high chemical stability.
  - **Note on Silver and Copper**: Can occur in both native state as well as combined state (sulphides and oxides).

---

### 2. Properties and Uses of Noble Metals

- **Gold ($\text{Au}$)**:
  - Highly malleable and ductile.
  - Highly resistant to tarnish and oxidation; dissolves only in **Aqua Regia** (mixture of concentrated $\text{HCl}$ and concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ in a $3:1$ ratio).
- **Platinum ($\text{Pt}$)**:
  - Known as **"White Gold"**; chemically inert with a high melting point.
  - Widely used as a catalyst in automobile **catalytic converters** to convert harmful exhaust gases into harmless substances.

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

According to the reactivity series of metals, which metal pair is found in native state?

- [ ] Calcium and aluminium
- [x] Gold and platinum
- [ ] Calcium and magnesium
- [ ] Sodium and potassium

> Gold and platinum have very low chemical reactivity and do not readily form compounds, allowing them to exist in nature in their pure elemental (native) state.

---

### PYQ 2

Which of the following metals occurs in nature in BOTH free state as well as in combined state as sulphide and oxide ores?

- [ ] Potassium
- [ ] Sodium
- [x] Copper
- [ ] Aluminium

> Copper and Silver lie low in the reactivity series and can be found both in the free native state and in combined forms (e.g., Copper Pyrites, Argentite).

---

### PYQ 3

Which mixture is capable of dissolving noble metals like Gold ($\text{Au}$) and Platinum ($\text{Pt}$)?

- [ ] Concentrated Sulphuric Acid
- [x] Aqua Regia ($3\text{ HCl} : 1\text{ HNO}_3$)
- [ ] Acetic Acid
- [ ] Dilute Nitric Acid

> Aqua Regia, a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in a $3:1$ volume ratio, dissolves noble metals.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Which of the following metals is the MOST reactive among the given options?

- [ ] Iron ($\text{Fe}$)
- [ ] Zinc ($\text{Zn}$)
- [ ] Copper ($\text{Cu}$)
- [x] Potassium ($\text{K}$)

> Potassium sits at the top of the reactivity series and reacts violently even with cold water.

---

### Q2

Why are alkali metals like sodium and potassium never found in their native elemental form in the Earth's crust?

- [ ] They are radioactive
- [x] They have very high chemical reactivity and readily form compounds
- [ ] They have very high density
- [ ] They sublimate at room temperature

> Because of their high electropositive nature and low ionization enthalpy, alkali metals readily lose electrons to form stable ionic compounds.

---

### Q3

Which noble metal is extensively used in automobile exhaust systems as a catalyst to convert toxic gases into less harmful emissions?

- [ ] Copper
- [ ] Lead
- [x] Platinum
- [ ] Zinc

> Platinum (along with Palladium and Rhodium) is widely used in catalytic converters due to its catalytic stability and resistance to corrosion.

---

### Q4

Which method is generally employed for the extraction of highly reactive metals located at the top of the activity series?

- [ ] Roasting
- [ ] Calcination
- [x] Electrolytic Reduction
- [ ] Smelting with Coke

> Highly reactive metals have strong affinity for oxygen and cannot be reduced by carbon; they are extracted by electrolysis of their molten chlorides or oxides.
