# 📌 Treaty of Allahabad (1765) & Dual System of Government

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Statement 1 is Incorrect**: The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) was signed under Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** (not Ahmad Shah) and **Robert Clive** after the **Battle of Buxar (1764)**.
- **Statement 2 is Correct**: Under this treaty, the British East India Company received **Diwani Rights** (tax collection rights) for **Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa**.
- Since the question asks for the **INCORRECT** statement, the answer is **1 only**.
- **Dual System of Government**: Started by Robert Clive in Bengal, separating **Diwani** (tax collection held by the British) from **Nizamat** (police and civil administration retained by the local Nawab).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Statement Analysis

- **Statement 1: Allahabad Treaty concluded (1765) Under the Rule of Ahmad Shah.**
  - *Incorrect.* Ahmad Shah ruled earlier from 1748 to 1754. The Treaty of Allahabad was signed on **August 12, 1765**, between Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** and **Robert Clive**.
- **Statement 2: Under This Treaty Diwani Rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa Granted to the British.**
  - *Correct.* Shah Alam II gave the *Diwani* (tax/revenue collection powers) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the Company for an annual fixed payment (tribute) of ₹26 Lakhs.

---

### 2. Key Terms & Provisions of the Treaty of Allahabad (1765)

*Two separate agreements signed by Robert Clive with Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah:*

- **With Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II**:
  - Handed over **Diwani Rights** (revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the British.
  - Gave Shah Alam II the districts of **Kora and Allahabad** (taken from the Nawab of Awadh).
  - The Company promised to pay an annual tax share (tribute) of **₹26 Lakhs** to the Mughal Emperor.
- **With Nawab of Awadh (Shuja-ud-Daulah)**:
  - Forced the Nawab to surrender Kora and Allahabad to Shah Alam II.
  - Made the Nawab pay **₹50 Lakhs** as war fine (indemnity) to the Company.
  - Forced Awadh into a military agreement (defensive alliance) where British troops were placed in Awadh at the Nawab's expense.
