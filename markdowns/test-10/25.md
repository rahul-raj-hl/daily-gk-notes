# 📌 Separation Techniques of Mixtures & Everyday Applications

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Principle**: Separation techniques separate mixtures by using differences in the physical and chemical properties of their components (e.g., boiling point, solubility, volatility, density).
- **Sublimation**: Separates a mixture where one solid turns directly into gas without melting into a liquid (e.g., **Naphthalene balls + Salt**, Camphor + Sand).
- **Crystallisation**: Purifies and separates soluble solids from a liquid solution by forming pure crystals (e.g., **Sugar + Water**, Copper sulphate crystals).
- **Evaporation**: Retrieves a soluble solid from a liquid solution by boiling off the liquid solvent (e.g., **Salt + Water**).
- **Separating Funnel**: Separates two **immiscible liquids** based on differences in density (e.g., **Vegetable oil + Water**, Kerosene + Water).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Overview of Key Separation Methods

*How each method works and when to apply it:*

- **1. Sublimation**:
  - **Concept**: Phase transition directly from **solid $\rightarrow$ gas** upon heating.
  - **Common Sublimable Solids**: Camphor, Naphthalene, Ammonium chloride ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$), Anthracene, and Iodine.
  - **Example**: A mixture of powdered naphthalene and common salt can be easily separated by gently heating it; naphthalene vaporizes and deposits on the cooler upper surface, leaving pure salt behind.
- **2. Crystallisation**:
  - **Concept**: A purification technique where a solid separates out in crystalline form from a hot, saturated liquid solution as it cools down.
  - **Why Better than Evaporation?**: Unlike simple evaporation, crystallisation prevents soluble solids (like sugar) from decomposing or charring due to excessive direct heat.
- **3. Evaporation**:
  - **Concept**: Vaporizing the liquid solvent from a homogeneous mixture to obtain the dissolved non-volatile solid residue.
  - **Example**: Extraction of common salt from seawater in open shallow pans.
- **4. Separating Funnel**:
  - **Concept**: Uses gravity to separate **immiscible liquids** (liquids that do not dissolve into each other).
  - **Mechanism**: The denser liquid settles into the lower layer and is drained off through the stopcock, leaving the lighter liquid inside the funnel.

### 2. Matching Common Mixtures with Techniques

*Standard exam pairings:*

- **(A) Powdered naphthalene + Salt** $\rightarrow$ **Sublimation**
- **(B) Sugar + Water** $\rightarrow$ **Crystallisation**
- **(C) Vegetable oil + Water** $\rightarrow$ **Separating Funnel**
- **(D) Salt + Water** $\rightarrow$ **Evaporation**

| **Alcohol + Water** | Two miscible liquids | **Fractional Distillation** | Difference in boiling points ($< 25\text{ K}$) |
| **Dyes in Black Ink** | Soluble colored pigments | **Chromatography** | Difference in rate of adsorption |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Method | Target Mixture Type | Key Differentiating Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Separating Funnel** | **Immiscible Liquids** (e.g., Oil + Water) | Liquids do NOT mix; separate into two distinct layers |
| **Fractional Distillation** | **Miscible Liquids** (e.g., Acetone + Water) | Liquids DO mix; separated by difference in boiling point |
| **Crystallisation** | **Soluble Solids** (e.g., Pure Copper Sulphate) | Gives pure crystals without charring/decomposition |
| **Evaporation** | **Soluble Solids** (e.g., Common Salt) | Fast recovery; residue may contain dried impurities |

- [ ] Liebig condenser
- [x] Separating funnel
- [ ] Fractionating column
- [ ] Filter paper

> Immiscible liquids separate into two distinct layers based on their densities and can be separated cleanly using a separating funnel.

### PYQ 3

Why is crystallisation considered a superior method compared to simple evaporation for obtaining pure solids from solutions?

- [ ] It requires more heat energy
- [x] It prevents the solids from charring or decomposing due to excess heat

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Which of the following substances does NOT undergo sublimation upon heating?

- [ ] Ammonium chloride
- [ ] Naphthalene
- [x] Sodium chloride (Common salt)
- [ ] Anthracene

> Common salt (sodium chloride) melts at a very high temperature and does not sublime, whereas ammonium chloride, naphthalene, and anthracene sublime readily.

---

### Q2

In a separating funnel containing a mixture of water and vegetable oil, which layer forms at the bottom?

- [x] Water layer (due to higher density)
- [ ] Oil layer (due to higher density)
- [ ] Both mix into a single layer
- [ ] An emulsion layer forms throughout

> Water is denser than oil ($1.0\text{ g/cm}^3 > 0.92\text{ g/cm}^3$), so it settles at the bottom of the separating funnel and is drained out first.

---

### Q3

The technique used to separate different pigments present in natural plant extracts or ink is known as:

- [ ] Crystallisation
- [ ] Centrifugation
- [x] Chromatography
- [ ] Sublimation

> Chromatography is used to separate solutes that dissolve in the same solvent based on their varying rates of migration/adsorption.

---

### Q4

Which method is best suited for separating a mixture of two miscible liquids with a boiling point difference greater than 25 K?

- [x] Simple Distillation
- [ ] Fractional Distillation
- [ ] Separating Funnel
- [ ] Sedimentation

> Simple distillation works effectively when separating miscible liquids that have a significant boiling point difference (greater than 25 K).
