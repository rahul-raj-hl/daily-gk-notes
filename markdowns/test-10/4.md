# 📌 Treaty of Allahabad (1765) & Dual System of Government

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Statement 1 is Incorrect**: The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) was concluded under Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** (not Ahmad Shah) and **Robert Clive** following the **Battle of Buxar (1764)**.
- **Statement 2 is Correct**: Under this treaty, the East India Company obtained the **Diwani Rights** (right to collect revenues) of **Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa**.
- Since the question asks for the **INCORRECT** statement(s), the correct answer is **1 only**.
- **Dual System of Government**: Introduced by Robert Clive in Bengal, separating **Diwani** (Revenue collection held by the British) from **Nizamat** (Police/Civil Administration retained by the Nawab).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Statement Analysis

- **Statement 1: Allahabad Treaty concluded (1765) Under the Rule of Ahmad Shah.**
  - *Incorrect.* Ahmad Shah ruled earlier from 1748 to 1754. The Treaty of Allahabad was signed on **August 12, 1765**, between Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** and **Robert Clive** of the East India Company.
- **Statement 2: Under This Treaty Diwani Rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa Granted to the British.**
  - *Correct.* Shah Alam II granted the *Diwani* (financial/revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the East India Company in return for an annual payment of ₹26 Lakhs.

---

### 2. Key Terms & Provisions of the Treaty of Allahabad (1765)

*Two separate treaties signed by Robert Clive with Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah:*

- **With Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II**:
  - Granted **Diwani Rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa** to the British East India Company.
  - Shah Alam II was given the districts of **Kora and Allahabad** (surrendered by Nawab of Awadh).
  - The Company agreed to pay an annual tribute of **₹26 Lakhs** to the Mughal Emperor.
- **With Nawab of Awadh (Shuja-ud-Daulah)**:
  - Surrendered Kora and Allahabad to Shah Alam II.
  - Paid **₹50 Lakhs** to the Company as war indemnity.
  - Entered into a defensive alliance where the Company provided troops for protection at Awadh's expense.
