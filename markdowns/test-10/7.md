# 📌 Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Named After**: **John Morley** (Secretary of State for India) and **Lord Minto** (Viceroy of India).
- **Limited Self-Government**: Increased the number of elected Indians in the Legislative Councils, providing for limited self-government.
- **Council Majorities**: Retained an **official majority** in the Central Legislative Council, but permitted a **non-official majority** in the provincial legislative councils.
- **Separate Electorates**: Formally introduced a system of communal representation for Muslims through a **separate electorate** (where Muslim members were elected exclusively by Muslim voters).
- **Colonial Self-Government**: Did nothing to grant colonial self-government, which was the Indian National Congress's main demand.
- **Shortest Lifespan**: It was the most short-lived of the major constitutional acts, remaining in effect for only **10 years** (1909–1919).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Provisions of the Act

*The core changes introduced by the British Parliament under the 1909 reforms:*

- **Expansion of Representation**:
  - Increased the involvement of Indians (on a limited scale) in the governance and legislative councils of British India.
  - Increased the number of elected Indian members in the Legislative Councils.
- **Structure of Council Majorities**:
  - **Central Legislative Council**: Retained an official majority.
  - **Provincial Legislative Councils**: Permitted non-official majorities.
- **Communal Representation**:
  - Accepted the principle of **separate electorates** for Muslims.
  - Under this mechanism, seats reserved for Muslims could only be voted on and decided by Muslim electors.
- **Limitations**:
  - Failed to concede the core political demand of the Congress—colonial self-government / Swaraj.

---

### 2. Lifespan Comparison of Major British Acts

*Comparative duration of constitutional reforms in British India:*

- **Indian Councils Act of 1861**: Lasted for **31 years** (1861–1892).
- **Indian Councils Act of 1892**: Lasted for **17 years** (1892–1909).
- **Indian Councils Act of 1909**: Lasted for **10 years** (1909–1919) — *The most short-lived*.
- **Government of India Act of 1919**: Lasted for **16 years** (1919–1935).
