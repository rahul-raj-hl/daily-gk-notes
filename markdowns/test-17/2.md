# 📌 Economic Liberalization & The 1991 LPG Reforms

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Removal of barriers or restrictions set by the government**.
- **Definition of Liberalization**: The relaxation or removal of state-imposed regulatory barriers, bureaucratic licensing requirements, quotas, and tariff restrictions to facilitate free market competition and provide greater operational autonomy to private enterprises.
- **Why the Selected Option was Incorrect**: Creating special protections/opportunities exclusively for public sector enterprises (PSUs) against the private sector is the hallmark of a **statist/socialist command economy**, not liberalization.
- **The Historic Context (1991)**: Initiated under Prime Minister **P. V. Narasimha Rao** and Finance Minister **Dr. Manmohan Singh** via the **New Economic Policy (NEP) of July 1991** in response to a severe Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Three Pillars of the 1991 Reforms (LPG Model)

*The structural adjustment and stabilization policy framework of 1991:*

| Pillar | Core Meaning | Key Policy Measures Undertaken |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liberalization (L)** | Dismantling state controls, quotas, and licensing | • Abolition of the **"License-Permit-Quota Raj"** (industrial licensing scrapped for all except 18, now only 4 industries).<br>• Removal of price controls and quantitative import restrictions.<br>• Liberalization of the financial sector and foreign exchange market (rupee devaluation and partial convertibility). |
| **Privatization (P)** | Transferring ownership/management from public to private hands | • **Disinvestment** of government equity in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).<br>• Reduction of sectors reserved exclusively for the public sector (originally 17, now reduced to just **2: Atomic Energy & Railway Operations**).<br>• Granting greater autonomy via *Maharatna*, *Navratna*, and *Miniratna* status. |
| **Globalization (G)** | Integrating the domestic economy with the global economy | • Reduction of peak customs/import tariffs.<br>• Opening sectors to **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)** and Foreign Institutional Investment (FII).<br>• Phased introduction of current account convertibility (adopted under Article VIII of IMF in 1994). |

---

### 2. Specific Reforms Under Economic Liberalization in India

- **Industrial Sector Reforms**:
  - Abolition of industrial licensing (**Industrial De-licensing**) for almost all categories.
  - De-reservation of items previously reserved exclusively for Small Scale Industries (SSIs).
  - Amendment of the **MRTP Act (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act, 1969)**, eliminating the requirement of prior government approval for expansion, mergers, and amalgamations (later replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002**).
- **Financial & Banking Sector Reforms**:
  - Based on the recommendations of the **Narasimham Committee I (1991) & II (1998)**.
  - Reduction in mandatory reserve ratios: **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** and **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**.
  - Allowing the entry of private and foreign banks to foster banking competition.
- **Tax & Trade Reforms**:
  - Simplification of direct and indirect taxes based on the **Raja Chelliah Committee** recommendations.
  - Removal of export duties and significant reduction in basic customs import tariffs.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Terminology | Exact Meaning | Key Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liberalization** | Relaxing rules, reducing licensing, and eliminating red tape | Making market entry and business operations free from government barriers |
| **Privatization** | Transferring equity, asset control, or management from state to private | Dilution of government stake / Disinvestment |
| **Globalization** | Cross-border flow of goods, services, capital, and labor | Global economic integration and tariff reduction |
| **FERA vs. FEMA** | FERA (1973) = Criminal offence; FEMA (1999) = Civil contravention | FEMA liberalized forex management |
