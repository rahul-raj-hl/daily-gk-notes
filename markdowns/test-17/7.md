# 📌 Oscillations & Waves: Amplitude, Frequency, Period & Phase

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Amplitude** ($A$).
- **Definition of Amplitude**: The **maximum displacement** of an oscillating or vibrating body from its central mean/equilibrium position.
- **SI Unit**: Measured in **meters ($\text{m}$)** for linear oscillations and **radians ($\text{rad}$)** for angular oscillations.
- **Energy & Intensity Relation**: The total energy of an oscillating system and the intensity of a wave are directly proportional to the **square of the amplitude** ($E \propto A^2$, $I \propto A^2$).
  - In sound waves, amplitude determines **loudness** ($\text{Loudness} \propto \text{Amplitude}^2$).
  - In light waves, amplitude determines **brightness/intensity**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Fundamental Parameters of Oscillatory & Wave Motion

*Definitions, symbols, formulas, and units of the four options:*

| Parameter | Symbol | Scientific Definition | Mathematical Formula | SI Unit |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Amplitude** | $A$ | **Maximum displacement** from the mean/equilibrium position | $x_{\text{max}} = A$ | Meter ($\text{m}$) |
| **Frequency** | $f$ or $\nu$ | Number of complete oscillations/cycles executed per unit time | $f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$ | Hertz ($\text{Hz} = \text{s}^{-1}$) |
| **Time Period** | $T$ | Time taken by an oscillating body to complete **one full cycle** | $T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$ | Second ($\text{s}$) |
| **Phase** | $\phi$ or $(\omega t + \phi_0)$ | The state or condition of motion (specifies both **position and direction**) at any instant | $x(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi)$ | Radian ($\text{rad}$) |

---

### 2. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) Essentials

*The mathematical motion of an ideal oscillator governed by Hooke's Law ($F = -kx$):*

- **Displacement Equation**: 

$$x(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$

- **Velocity of Oscillator**: 

$$v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$$

  - Maximum velocity ($v_{\text{max}} = A\omega$) occurs at the **mean position ($x = 0$)**.
  - Minimum velocity ($v = 0$) occurs at the **extreme positions ($x = \pm A$)**.
- **Acceleration of Oscillator**: 

$$a(t) = -\omega^2 x$$

  - Acceleration is directly proportional to displacement and always directed toward the mean position.
  - Maximum acceleration ($a_{\text{max}} = \omega^2 A$) occurs at the **extreme positions ($x = \pm A$)**.
  - Zero acceleration occurs at the **mean position ($x = 0$)**.

---

### 3. Sound Wave Characteristics & Their Physical Determinants

- **Loudness**: Determined strictly by **Amplitude** ($\text{Loudness} \propto A^2$). A large amplitude produces a loud sound; a small amplitude produces a feeble sound.
- **Pitch (Shrillness / Graveness)**: Determined strictly by **Frequency** ($f$). High frequency produces high pitch/shrill sound (e.g., female voice, bird chirping); low frequency produces deep/grave sound (e.g., male voice, lion's roar).
- **Quality / Timbre**: Determined by the **Waveform / Harmonics** present, allowing the ear to distinguish between two sounds of the same pitch and loudness.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Physical Feature | Governed Exclusively By | Common Misconception |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Loudness of Sound** | **Amplitude** | Assuming loudness depends on frequency or pitch |
| **Pitch / Shrillness** | **Frequency** | Assuming pitch depends on loudness or amplitude |
| **Speed of Sound in Medium** | **Medium properties** (Elasticity & Density) | Assuming wave speed increases if loudness or pitch increases |
| **Total Energy of Oscillation** | **Square of Amplitude** ($E \propto A^2$) | Assuming energy is linearly proportional to amplitude ($E \propto A$) |

- [ ] Frequency

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

For a particle executing Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM), what happens to its kinetic and potential energies at the mean equilibrium position?

- [ ] Potential energy is maximum, Kinetic energy is zero
- [x] Kinetic energy is maximum, Potential energy is zero
- [ ] Both energies are equal to zero
- [ ] Both energies reach their maximum values simultaneously

> At the equilibrium position ($x=0$), displacement is zero so potential energy is zero, while velocity and kinetic energy reach their maximum values.

---

### Q2

If the amplitude of a vibrating body is tripled ($3\times$), by what factor will its total vibrational energy increase?

- [ ] $3\text{ times}$
- [ ] $6\text{ times}$
- [x] $9\text{ times}$
- [ ] $27\text{ times}$

> Because energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude ($E = \frac{1}{2} k A^2$), tripling the amplitude ($3A$) scales the energy by $3^2 = 9\text{ times}$.

---

### Q3

The physical quantity that specifies both the exact position and direction of motion of an oscillating particle at any given instant of time is called:

- [ ] Time period
- [ ] Wavenumber
- [x] Phase
- [ ] Wavelength

> Phase represents the argument of the harmonic function $(\omega t + \phi)$ and defines both the instantaneous position and direction of motion of the oscillator.

---

### Q4

What is the frequency of a simple pendulum if it takes $0.05\text{ seconds}$ to complete one full oscillation?

- [ ] $5\text{ Hz}$
- [ ] $10\text{ Hz}$
- [x] $20\text{ Hz}$
- [ ] $50\text{ Hz}$

> Frequency $f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{0.05\text{ s}} = \frac{100}{5} = 20\text{ Hz}$.
