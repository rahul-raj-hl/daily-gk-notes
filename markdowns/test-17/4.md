# 📌 Tughlaq Dynasty & Nasir-ud-din Mahmud Shah

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Nasir ud-din Mahmud** (also known as *Nasir-ud-din Mahmud Shah Tughlaq*).
- **Dynastic Span**: The **Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414 CE)** was the longest-ruling dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate (94 years).
- **The Last Ruler**: **Nasir-ud-din Mahmud** (reigned 1394–1413/1414 CE) was the final sultan of the dynasty.
- **Timur's Devastating Invasion (1398 CE)**: Central Asian conqueror **Amir Timur (Tamerlane)** invaded northern India and sacked Delhi during Nasir-ud-din Mahmud's reign, causing the Sultan to flee to Gujarat.
- **Famous Proverb**: Due to his drastically shrunken realm, a famous contemporary Persian phrase mocked: *"Hukm-e Khudavand-e-Alam, az Dilli ta Palam"* (*The rule of the Lord of the World extends from Delhi to Palam*).
- **Successor Dynasty**: Following the collapse of the Tughlaqs, Timur's appointed deputy **Khizr Khan** founded the **Sayyid Dynasty in 1414 CE**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Chronology of the Delhi Sultanate Dynasties

*The five ruling houses of the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE):*

| Dynasty | Duration | Founder | Last Ruler | Key Distinction |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty** | **1206 – 1290** | Qutb-ud-din Aibak | Muiz-ud-din Qaiqabad / Kayumars | Established the Delhi Sultanate |
| **Khalji Dynasty** | **1290 – 1320** | Jalal-ud-din Khalji | Khusrau Khan | Shortest ruling dynasty (30 years); market reforms |
| **Tughlaq Dynasty** | **1320 – 1414** | **Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq** | **Nasir-ud-din Mahmud** | **Longest ruling dynasty (94 years)** |
| **Sayyid Dynasty** | **1414 – 1451** | Khizr Khan | Ala-ud-din Alam Shah | Claimed descent from the Prophet Muhammad |
| **Lodi Dynasty** | **1451 – 1526** | Bahlul Lodi | Ibrahim Lodi | First Afghan dynasty; ended at First Battle of Panipat |

---

### 2. Major Rulers of the Tughlaq Dynasty

*Key sovereigns and their administrative/historical significance:*

#### A. Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) (1320–1325 CE)
- Founded the dynasty after overthrowing Khusrau Khan.
- Founded the third city of Delhi: **Tughlaqabad** (fortified citadel).
- Built early canal irrigation systems; had famous strained relations with Sufi saint **Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya** (*"Hunuz Dilli dur ast"*).

#### B. Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan) (1325–1351 CE)
- Known as the "wisest fool" / mixture of opposites.
- **Five Ambitious Experiments**:
  1. Transfer of capital from **Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri)** (1327).
  2. Introduction of **Token Currency (Bronze/Copper coins)** (1329–1330).
  3. Increased taxation in the fertile **Doab region** during famine.
  4. Planned **Khurasan Expedition**.
  5. The disastrous **Qarachil (Kumaon hills) Expedition**.
- Established the agricultural department called **Diwan-i-Kohi**.
- Moroccan traveler **Ibn Battuta** visited India during his reign (author of *Rihla*; appointed as Qazi of Delhi).

#### C. Feroz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388 CE)
- Instituted an orthodox Islamic state; introduced the four Quranic taxes: **Kharaj** (land tax), **Jizya** (poll tax on non-Muslims, including Brahmins for the first time), **Zakat** (property tax on Muslims), and **Khams** (1/5th share of war booty), plus **Haqq-i-Sharb** (irrigation water tax).
- Created major public departments:
  - **Diwan-i-Khairat** (Department of charity for poor/widows)
  - **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (Department of slaves)
  - **Dar-ul-Shafa** (Free charitable hospitals)
- Founded several historic cities: **Firozabad**, **Fatehabad**, **Hissar**, **Jaunpur** (in memory of Jauna Khan), and **Firozpur**.

#### D. Nasir-ud-din Mahmud (1394–1413 CE) & Timur's Invasion (1398 CE)
- The empire fragmented into rival sultanates (Jaunpur Sharqi Sultanate, Malwa, Gujarat, Khandesh).
- **Timur's Sack of Delhi (December 1398)**: Amir Timur invaded via Multan and Punjab, ruthlessly plundered Delhi, massacred inhabitants, and returned to Samarkand, leaving Khizr Khan as governor of Multan and Lahore.
- With Mahmud's death, Tughlaq rule effectively ceased.
