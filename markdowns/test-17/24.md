# 📌 Natural Habitats of Flamingos in India (North-West Region)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **North-West**.
- **The Epicenter ("Flamingo City")**: The **Rann of Kutch** (specifically the mudflats of the Great Rann of Kutch and Little Rann of Kutch in **Gujarat**, situated in North-West India) is the largest breeding and congregation site for flamingos in Asia.
- **Flamingo Species in India**:
  1. **Greater Flamingo** (*Phoenicopterus roseus*): Taller, pale pink/whitish plumage with a pink beak tipped with black.
  2. **Lesser Flamingo** (*Phoeniconaias minor*): Smaller, deep pink/crimson plumage with a dark red/blackish bill; classified as *Near Threatened* on the IUCN Red List.
- **Why North-West India?**: Hypersaline mudflats, shallow lagoons, and vast salt marshes provide ideal breeding grounds and abundant food sources like blue-green algae (*Spirulina*) and brine shrimp (*Artemia*).

### 2. Ecological Adaptations of Flamingos

- **Feeding Mechanism**: Filter feeders with specialized, bent downward bills containing microscopic structures called *lamellae* that sieve tiny organisms, brine shrimp, and algae from mud and water.
- **Pink Coloration**: Derived from carotenoid pigments found in their diet (algae and crustaceans).
- **Breeding Mounds**: Build volcano-shaped mud mounds in isolated salt flats to protect eggs from floodwaters and predatory ground animals.
