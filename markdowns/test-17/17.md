# 📌 Women's Reservation in Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Rajasthan**.
- **State Chronology of 50% Reservation for Women in PRIs**:
  1. **First State**: **Bihar** (became the first state in India to provide **50% reservation** for women in PRIs via the *Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006*).
  2. **Second State**: **Rajasthan** (subsequently amended its state Panchayati Raj Act to enhance women's reservation from one-third to 50%).
  3. **Other Leading States**: Followed by **Madhya Pradesh**, **Uttarakhand**, **Himachal Pradesh**, and **Odisha**.
- **Constitutional Minimum (Article 243D)**: The **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** constitutionally mandates a minimum of **not less than one-third (33.33%)** of the total seats and offices of chairpersons reserved for women across all three tiers of Panchayats. State legislatures are constitutionally empowered to expand this reservation up to 50%.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Constitutional Provisions for Panchayati Raj Reservations

*Key provisions governing representation under Part IX of the Constitution:*

- **Article 243D(1) & (2) — SC/ST Reservation**:
  - Seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) in every Panchayat in direct proportion to their population in the Panchayat area.
- **Article 243D(3) — Women's Reservation in Direct Seats**:
  - **Not less than one-third (including SC/ST women)** of the total number of seats to be filled by direct election in every Panchayat must be reserved for women.
  - These seats are allocated by rotation to different constituencies in a Panchayat.
- **Article 243D(4) — Reservation for Chairperson Offices**:
  - Not less than one-third of the total number of offices of Chairpersons (Sarpanch / Pradhan / Adhyaksha) at each level must be reserved for women.
- **Article 243D(6) — Backward Class Reservation**:
  - Leaves discretion to state legislatures to provide reservation of seats or offices of chairpersons in favour of backward classes (OBCs).

### 2. Historical Milestones of Panchayati Raj in India

*Evolutionary milestones of democratic decentralization:*

| Milestone / Event | Date / Year | Key Personality / Committee | Historical Significance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** | **1957** | Balwant Rai Mehta | Recommended 3-tier Panchayati Raj system (Gram, Block, District) |
| **First State to Launch PRIs** | **2 October 1959** | Jawaharlal Nehru | Ingested at **Nagaur district, Rajasthan** |
| **Second State to Launch PRIs** | **October 1959** | Government of Andhra Pradesh | Launched across Andhra Pradesh shortly after Rajasthan |
| **Ashok Mehta Committee** | **1977** | Ashok Mehta (Janata Govt.) | Recommended a 2-tier system (Mandal Panchayat & Zila Parishad) |
| **L.M. Singhvi Committee** | **1986** | L.M. Singhvi | First recommended constitutional status and protection for PRIs |
| **73rd Constitutional Amendment** | **1992** | P. V. Narasimha Rao | Added **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243O)** and **11th Schedule** (29 functional items) |
| **National Panchayati Raj Day** | **24 April** | Celebrated annually | Commemorates the coming into force of the 73rd Amendment in 1993 |

---

### 3. States with 50% Reservation for Women in PRIs

*To date, more than 20 Indian states have enhanced women's reservation in local self-governments from the mandatory 33.33% to 50%:*

- **Pioneering Northern & Western States**: Bihar (1st in 2006), Rajasthan (2nd), Madhya Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Punjab, Haryana.
- **Southern & Western States**: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat.
- **Eastern & North-Eastern States**: Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Assam, Tripura, Sikkim.
