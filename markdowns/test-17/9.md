# 📌 Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316 CE) & The Khalji Dynasty

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **1296–1316**.
- **The Ruler**: **Alauddin Khalji** (born *Ali Gurshasp*) was the second and most powerful monarch of the Khalji Dynasty, reigning from **1296 to 1316 CE** (20 years).
- **Ascension**: Ascended the throne of Delhi after assassinating his uncle and father-in-law, **Jalal-ud-din Khalji** (who ruled from 1290 to 1296 CE), at Kara (Manikpur).
- **Regal Title**: Assumed the title ***Sikandar-i-Sani*** (The Second Alexander) and minted coins bearing this title, dreaming of world conquest and establishing a new religion before being dissuaded by Ala-ul-Mulk (Kotwal of Delhi).
- **Other Rulers in the Options**:
  - **1290–1296**: **Jalal-ud-din Khalji** (Founder of the Khalji dynasty).
  - **1266–1287**: **Ghiyas-ud-din Balban** (Slave/Mamluk dynasty).
  - **1210–1236**: **Shams-ud-din Iltutmish** (Real founder of the Slave dynasty).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Military & Administrative Reforms

- **Permanent Standing Army**: First Sultan of Delhi to maintain a large, centralized, permanent standing army paid strictly in **cash (*Naqad*)** rather than through *Iqta* land assignments.
- **Dagh and Huliya/Chehra Systems**:
  - **Dagh**: Branding of royal warhorses to prevent fraudulent substitution.
  - **Huliya / Chehra**: Descriptive physical biometric roll of every recruit/soldier.
- **Espionage Network**: Revamped the intelligence department headed by the **Barid-i-Mamalik** (spies named *Munhiyans* and *Barids*) to monitor nobility and markets.
- **Four Royal Ordinances to Curb Nobility**:
  1. Confiscated free land grants, endowments (*Waqf*), and religious properties.
  2. Banned alcohol, intoxicating drugs, and gambling parties in Delhi.
  3. Prohibited nobles from hosting social gatherings or inter-marrying without prior royal permission.
  4. Established a ubiquitous network of secret spies (*Munhiyans*).

---

### 2. Market Control & Price Regulation (*Shahna-i-Mandi*)

*Alauddin instituted fixed price controls across three separate specialized markets in Delhi:*

| Market Category | Commodities Sold | Regulating Officer / System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mandi** | Food grains & daily provisions | Supervised by **Shahna-i-Mandi**; state buffer stocks built in grain warehouses |
| **Sarai-i-Adl** | Cloth, sugar, dried fruits, herbs, and oils | Subsidized "Market of Justice"; regulated directly under state permit |
| **Livestock & Slave Market** | Horses, cattle, and domestic slaves | Elimination of middlemen/brokers (*Dallals*); prices fixed strictly by quality tiers |

- **Department of Commerce**: Established the **Diwan-i-Riyasat** (headed by *Naib-i-Riyasat* Malik Yaqut) to oversee commercial activity and enforce state price limits.
- **Severe Penalties**: Under strict surveillance, any merchant caught short-weighing had an equivalent weight of flesh cut from their body.

---

### 3. Agrarian & Revenue Reforms

- **Measurement of Land (*Masahat*)**: First Sultan to introduce standard land measurement based on the **Biswa** unit for fixing agricultural tax.
- **Land Tax Rate (*Kharaj*)**: Raised land revenue to **50% (half) of the gross produce**.
- **New Taxes Introduced**:
  - **Ghari** (House tax on residences).
  - **Charai** (Grazing tax on milch cattle).
- **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj**: Specialized revenue department created exclusively to recover outstanding arrears and arrears from corrupt tax collectors (*Amils*, *Muqaddams*, *Patwaris*).

---

### 4. Military Conquests & Deccan Policy

- **North Indian Campaigns**:
  - **Gujarat (1299)**: Defeated Raja Rai Karan Ghelo; acquired the commander **Malik Kafur** (*Hazar-Dinari*).
  - **Ranthambore (1301)**: Defeated Rana Hammiradeva (historical *Jauhar* described by Amir Khusrau).
  - **Chittor (1303)**: Defeated Rana Ratan Singh; renamed Chittor to **Khizrabad** (after his son Khizr Khan).
  - **Malwa (1305)** and **Siwana/Jalore (1308–1311)**.
- **Deccan Campaigns (Led by Malik Kafur)**:
  - Did not annex southern territories directly; forced rulers into tributary vassalage and extracted gold, horses, and war elephants.
  - **Devagiri (1307–1308)**: Yadava King **Ramachandra Deva** (conferred title *Rai Rayan*).
  - **Warangal (1309–1310)**: Kakatiya King **Prataparudra II** (surrendered the **Koh-i-Noor diamond**).
  - **Dwarasamudra (1310)**: Hoysala King **Veera Ballala III**.
  - **Madurai (1311)**: Pandya Kingdom under **Sundara Pandya** and **Vira Pandya**.

---

### 5. Architectural & Literary Contributions

- **Architectural Monuments**:
  - **Alai Darwaza** (1311 CE): Northern entrance gateway to the Qutb Complex featuring the first true horseshoe arch and true dome built in India.
  - **Siri Fort**: The second city of Delhi built to repel Mongol invasions.
  - **Hauz Khas (Hauz-i-Alai)**: Large water reservoir built for citizens and Siri garrison.
  - **Alai Minar**: Unfinished colossal tower intended to be twice the height of Qutub Minar.
- **Court Patronage**:
  - **Amir Khusrau** (*Tuti-e-Hind* / Parrot of India): Composed *Khazain-ul-Futuh* (recording Alauddin's victories) and *Ashiqa*.
  - **Amir Hasan Sijzi** (author of *Fawa'id-ul-Fu'ad*).
