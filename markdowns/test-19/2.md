# 📌 The Moplah Uprising (1921): Malabar Peasant Movement & Khilafat Link

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Kerala** (specifically the **Malabar region** of northern Kerala).
- **Year & Nature**: Broke out in **August 1921** as an armed uprising by the **Mappila (Moplah) Muslim tenant cultivators** against both the oppressive landed aristocracy (**Jenmis**, mostly upper-caste Hindus) and British colonial administrators.
- **National Movement Linkage**: Deeply intertwined with the broader **Khilafat Movement** and Mahatma Gandhi's **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)**.
- **Prominent Leaders**: **Variyamkunnath Kunjahammed Haji**, **Ali Musliyar**, and **Seethi Koya Thangal**.
- **Infamous Tragedy**: The **Wagon Tragedy** (November 1921), in which 64 Mappila prisoners died of suffocation while being transported in a closed railway freight wagon from Tirur to Bellary Central Jail.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Root Causes & Nature of the Rebellion

| Dimension | Contributing Factors | Key Historical Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Agrarian Grievances** | • High security of tenure denial to tenant cultivators (*Verumpattamdars*).<br>• Exorbitant rents and illegal cesses imposed by landlords (*Jenmis*).<br>• Arbitrary evictions backed by British civil courts and police. | Created deep-seated anti-landlord and anti-British hostility across the rural Malabar peasantry. |
| **Religious & Political Impetus** | • Resentment over the dismantling of the Ottoman Caliphate (Khilafat cause).<br>• Speeches by national leaders (Gandhi, Shaukat Ali, Maulana Azad) visiting Malabar in 1920. | Galvanized local religious and political leadership to form underground volunteer resistance cells. |
| **Trigger Event (August 1921)** | • British police raided the mosque at Tirurangadi to arrest Khilafat leader **Ali Musliyar**.<br>• Rumors spread that the historic mosque was desecrated. | Triggered full-scale armed insurgency across Eranad and Valluvanad taluks. |
| **British Suppression** | • British declared martial law; inducted special forces (**Malabar Special Police - MSP**).<br>• Rebellion was crushed by late 1921/early 1922; leaders were executed or deported. | Resulted in severe communal polarization and widespread loss of life. |

### 2. Major Peasant Uprisings Across British India

*Overview of key peasant struggles for direct historical comparison:*

| Movement / Rebellion | Year(s) | Region / Modern State | Key Leaders | Primary Target / Grievance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Indigo Revolt (Nil Bidroha)** | 1859–1860 | **Bengal** | Digambar Biswas & Bishnu Biswas | Oppressive European planters forcing indigo cultivation. |
| **Pabna Agrarian League** | 1873–1876 | **Bengal** (now Bangladesh)| Ishan Chandra Roy, Shambhu Pal | Illegal rent enhancement and tenant eviction by zamindars. |
| **Deccan Riots** | 1875 | **Maharashtra** (Pune, Ahmednagar) | Local Ryots | Predatory moneylenders (*Marwaris* and *Gujaratis*); led to *Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act 1879*. |
| **Moplah (Mappila) Rebellion** | **1921** | **Kerala (Malabar)** | **Variyamkunnath Haji, Ali Musliyar** | High land rents, British police raids & Khilafat suppression. |
| **Bardoli Satyagraha** | 1928 | **Gujarat** | **Vallabhbhai Patel** | Arbitrary $22\%$ hike in land revenue; earned Patel the title *"Sardar"*. |
| **Tebhaga Movement** | 1946–1947 | **Bengal** | Kanshi Ram, Krishna Binod Ray | Sharecroppers (*bargadars*) demanding two-thirds ($2/3$) share of produce. |
| **Telangana Peasant Movement**| 1946–1951 | **Telangana (Hyderabad State)**| Ravi Narayana Reddy, Sundarayya | Oppression of *Deshmukhs* / *Jagirdars* and *Vetti* (forced labor) under the Nizam. |
