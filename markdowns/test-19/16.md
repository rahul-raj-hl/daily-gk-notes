# 📌 Mughal Land Revenue: Raja Todarmal's Dahsala & Zabti System

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Introduced By**: **Raja Todarmal**, the renowned Finance Minister (*Diwan-i-Ashraf / Wazir*) of Emperor **Akbar**, formally instituted in **1580 CE (24th regnal year)**.
- **Dahsala System Mechanics**: Derived the **average produce and average prices over a 10-year period** (1570–1580 CE / 15th to 24th regnal years).
- **State Demand & Peasant Share**:
  - **State's Share (*Mal*)**: Fixed at **one-third ($1/3\text{rd}$)** of the average produce.
  - **Cultivator's Share (*Kharaj*)**: The remaining **two-thirds ($2/3\text{rd}$)** was retained by the peasant.
- **Conversion to Cash (*Dastur-i-Amal*)**: Although assessment was computed in maunds per bigha, conversion from kind to cash was done using localized cash rate schedules called **Dastur-i-Amal** for each revenue circle (*dastur*).
- **Core Pillars of the Zabti System**: Precise land measurement (*Jarib* made of bamboo with iron rings), crop yield surveys, fixed cash schedules, and cash collection directly from cultivators.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Land Classification Under the Zabti / Dahsala System

*Land was categorized into four classes based on continuity and regularity of cultivation:*

| Land Classification | Cultivation Frequency & Characteristics | Revenue Demand Category |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polaj** | Annually cultivated for every harvest continuously; never allowed to lie fallow. | Paid full state revenue ($1/3\text{rd}$) every year without remission. |
| **Parauti** | Left uncultivated (fallow) temporarily for $1\text{ to }2\text{ years}$ to recover fertility. | Paid full revenue rate only when brought back under cultivation. |
| **Chachar** | Left fallow for $3\text{ to }4\text{ years}$ due to waterlogging or depletion. | Concessional, progressive revenue rates until fully restored. |
| **Banjar** | Uncultivated for $5\text{ years}$ or more (barren/waste land). | Paid minimal token rates in early years to encourage reclamation. |

---

### 2. Operational Framework of the Zabti System

| Stage | Administrative Function | Official / Instrument Involved | Purpose & Working |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Measurement (*Paimash*)** | *Tanab / Jarib* (Bamboo with iron rings) | Replaced the hemp rope (*tenab*) with standardized *Ilahi Gaz* (41 digits, ~33 inches) to measure land accurately in *Bighas*. |
| **2** | **Assessment (*Tashkhis*)** | *Amilguzar* (Revenue Collector) & *Patwari* | Calculated 10-year average yield for good, middling, and bad lands to determine average standard yield per bigha. |
| **3** | **Cash Fixation (*Dastur*)** | *Dastur-i-Amal* (Schedule of Rates) | Converted state share ($1/3\text{rd}$) into cash based on 10-year local market prices across designated revenue circles. |
| **4** | **Record & Title Deeds** | *Patta* and *Qabuliyat* | State issued *Patta* (title deed showing land & demand) and peasant signed *Qabuliyat* (deed of agreement accepting payment). |
| **5** | **Collection** | *Muqaddam* (Village Headman) / *Qanungo* | Collected directly in cash in two installments per year (Kharif and Rabi). |

---

### 3. Alternative Mughal Land Revenue Assessment Methods

*Other prevailing systems across provinces where Zabti was not implemented:*

| Assessment System | Alternative Names | Mode of Operation & Assessment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zabti / Dahsala** | *Bandobast System / Todarmal's System* | Revenue based on actual land measurement and fixed cash rate tables (*Dastur*). Prevalent in Lahore, Agra, Delhi, Awadh, Malwa, and Allahabad. |
| **Batai / Ghalla-Bakhshi** | *Bhaoli / Crop Sharing* | Physical division of the standing or threshed crop between the State and the peasant (*Khet-Batai*, *Lanka-Batai*, *Rasi-Batai*). Prevalent in Sindh, Kashmir. |
| **Nasaq / Kankut** | *Appraisal / Estimation* | Rough estimation/appraisal of the standing crop yield based on previous years' records without annual land remeasurement. Prevalent in Bengal, Gujarat. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Revenue Term | Exact Historical Meaning | Common Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polaj vs. Parauti** | • **Polaj**: Annually cultivated (No fallow)<br>• **Parauti**: Left fallow for 1–2 years | Mixing up the fallow duration between classes |
| **Chachar vs. Banjar** | • **Chachar**: Fallow for 3–4 years<br>• **Banjar**: Fallow for 5+ years | Confusing the years of abandonment |
| **Dastur-i-Amal** | **Schedule of cash revenue rates** | Confusing with the imperial autobiography/chronicles |
| **Zabti vs. Batai** | • **Zabti**: Measurement & cash demand<br>• **Batai**: Physical division of actual grain | Assuming all Mughal revenue was paid in kind |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "P-P-C-B: POLAJ PLANTS YEARLY, PARAUTI PAUSES BRIEFLY, CHACHAR COOLS 3 YEARS, BANJAR IS BARREN 5+"**
> - **P** $\rightarrow$ **Polaj** (Continuous cultivation every year)
> - **P** $\rightarrow$ **Parauti** (Fallow for 1–2 years)
> - **C** $\rightarrow$ **Chachar** (Fallow for 3–4 years)
> - **B** $\rightarrow$ **Banjar** (Uncultivated for 5+ years)
