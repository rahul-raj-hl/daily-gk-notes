# 📌 Types of Volcanoes: Classification & Characteristics

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Composite Volcanoes (Stratovolcanoes)**.
- **Key Identifying Features**:
  - Eruptions consist of **cooler, highly viscous, and silica-rich (andesitic/felsic/rhyolitic) lavas** compared to basalt.
  - Large quantities of **pyroclastic material, cinders, and volcanic ash** accumulate along with viscous lava around the vent opening.
  - This alternating accumulation of lava flows and ash deposits creates distinct **stratified layers**, giving them their alternate name: **Stratovolcanoes**.
- **Prominent Global Examples**: **Mount Fuji (Japan)**, **Mount Mayon (Philippines)**, **Mount Vesuvius (Italy)**, and **Mount St. Helens (USA)**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Classification of Major Volcano Types

| Volcano Type | Lava Composition & Viscosity | Eruption Nature & Mechanism | Morphology & Structure | Prominent Global Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Composite Volcanoes (Stratovolcanoes)** | **High viscosity, cooler, silica-rich** (Andesitic/Rhyolitic) | Highly explosive; ejects massive pyroclastic debris and volcanic ash. | Tall, symmetrical, steep-sided cones formed by alternating layers of lava and pyroclastic ash. | **Mt. Fuji (Japan), Mt. Mayon (Philippines), Mt. Cotopaxi (Ecuador)** |
| **Shield Volcanoes** | **Low viscosity, hot, fluid basaltic lava** | Low explosivity (effusive eruptions); explosive only if water enters the vent. | Broad, gently sloping domes with low profiles resembling a warrior's shield. | **Hawaiian Volcanoes (Mauna Loa, Kilauea)** |
| **Caldera** | Highly viscous and volatile-rich magma | **Most explosive** volcanoes on Earth; eruptions empty the magma chamber rapidly. | The structure collapses into the emptied magma chamber, leaving a massive, deep depression or crater lake. | **Lake Toba (Indonesia), Yellowstone (USA), Crater Lake (USA)** |
| **Flood Basalt Provinces** | Extremely fluid, highly mobile basaltic lava | Highly effusive fissure eruptions without explosive cones. | Vast, thick, flat-lying horizontal basalt layers covering thousands of square kilometers. | **Deccan Traps (India), Columbia River Basalt (USA)** |
| **Mid-Ocean Ridge Volcanoes** | Basaltic oceanic crust lava | Continuous underwater seafloor spreading eruptions along divergent boundaries. | Submarine mountain chains with a central rift system ($>70,000\text{ km}$ globally). | **Mid-Atlantic Ridge** |

---

### 2. Physical Evolution of a Composite Volcano

- **Stage 1 (Magma Ascent)**: High-silica magma with trapped dissolved gases rises slowly through the central vent due to its high viscosity and resistance to flow.
- **Stage 2 (Explosive Eruption)**: High internal gas pressure triggers violent explosive eruptions, blasting shattered rocks, pumice, cinders, and fine ash into the air as pyroclastic debris.
- **Stage 3 (Layer Deposition)**: Heavy pyroclastic matter settles around the vent, followed by sluggish, cooling lava flows that seal and cement the material.
- **Stage 4 (Cone Building)**: Repeated episodic eruptions build a towering, steep-sided composite mountain composed of stratified ash and lava beds.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Volcano Type | Defining Feature | Common Trap / Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Composite vs. Shield** | • **Composite**: Steep slope, viscous/silica lava, explosive<br>• **Shield**: Gentle slope, fluid basaltic lava, non-explosive | Assuming shield volcanoes are steep because they are large |
| **Caldera vs. Crater** | • **Caldera**: Large collapse depression formed after explosion<br>• **Crater**: Small circular vent opening formed by outward explosion | Using the two terms interchangeably |
| **Acidic (Felsic) vs. Basic (Mafic) Lava** | • **Acidic/Felsic**: High silica, high viscosity, light color, highly explosive<br>• **Basic/Mafic**: Low silica, low viscosity, dark color, flows easily | Swapping silica content and viscosity properties |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "COMPOSITE CONES ARE COOL, VISCOUS & LAYERED; SHIELDS SPREAD FLUID BASALT"**
> - **Composite Cones** $\rightarrow$ Cooler, viscous lava with layered pyroclastic ash (Steep)
> - **Shields Spread** $\rightarrow$ Fluid, hot basalt spreading into gentle domes (Broad)
