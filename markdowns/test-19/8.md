# 📌 The Santhal Rebellion (Santhal Hool, 1855–56) & Damin-i-Koh

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **The Outbreak**: On **30 June 1855**, brothers **Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu** (along with **Chand and Bhairav**) mobilized over 10,000 Santhals at Bhagnadih to declare a full-scale armed rebellion (*Hool*) against British colonial authorities, zamindars, and moneylenders (*Dikus*).
- **Damin-i-Koh**: In **1832**, the British demarcated a large tract of land in the **foothills of Rajmahal** as *Damin-i-Koh* ("skirts of the hills"), legally designating it as Santhal territory for settled plough agriculture.
- **Rapid Demography**: Between 1838 and 1851, the number of Santhal villages in Damin-i-Koh skyrocketed from **40 to 1,473**, accompanied by heavy exploitation from high state taxes, usurious moneylenders, and corrupt police.
- **Creation of Santhal Pargana**: To pacify the region after suppressing the rebellion in 1856, the British government carved out **5,500 square miles** from the districts of **Bhagalpur and Birbhum** to form the non-regulation district of **Santhal Parganas**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Root Causes & Sequence of the Santhal Hool (1855–1856)

*Key chronological stages of the Santhal displacement, settlement, and insurrection:*

| Phase / Feature | Historical Mechanism / Event | Core Impact & Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paharias Displacement (Late 18th C)** | British pushed shifting-cultivator **Paharias** deeper into the interior Rajmahal hills and invited Santhals to clear dense forests. | Replaced nomadic hill farming with permanent plough agriculture. |
| **Demarcation of *Damin-i-Koh* (1832)** | Formal surveying, mapping, and marking of boundary pillars around the Rajmahal foothills. | Land declared as Santhal territory; stipulated that at least $1/10\text{th}$ of land had to be cleared and cultivated within 10 years. |
| **Economic Exploitation (1830s–1850s)** | • Zamindars claimed uncultivated lands and levied steep rents.<br>• Moneylenders (*Mahajans/Dikus*) charged interest rates of $50\%\text{ to }500\%$.<br>• Bonded labor systems (*Kamiyoti*) trapped Santhal families. | Pushed the indigenous peasantry into absolute indebtedness and land alienation. |
| **Armed Insurrection (30 June 1855)** | Sidhu and Kanhu declared divine sanction (*Thakur's revelation*) to overthrow British rule and eradicate *Dikus*. | Sacked police stations, post offices, railway stations, and zamindari katcheris across Bengal and Bihar. |
| **Military Suppression (1855–1856)** | Major-General Lloyd deployed extensive troop columns with martial law; thousands of Santhals were killed; leaders were captured. | Armed phase ended; Sidhu and Kanhu were hanged. |
| **Creation of Santhal Pargana (1855–1856)** | British passed the **Santhal Parganas Act, 1855**, creating a specialized administrative zone carved from Bhagalpur and Birbhum. | Enacted special protection tenancy laws preventing direct alienation of tribal land to non-tribals. |

### 2. Major 19th-Century Tribal Revolts in India: Quick Comparison

*High-frequency tribal uprisings for comparative exam recall:*

| Tribal Movement | Period | Region / Modern State | Key Leaders | Primary Grievance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Santhal Rebellion (Hool)** | **1855–1856** | **Rajmahal / Santhal Pargana (Jharkhand/Bihar)** | **Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand, Bhairav** | **Feudal rent hikes, debt traps, police extortion & Diku exploitation.** |

| **Rampa Rebellion** | 1922–1924 | Andhra Pradesh (Godavari) | **Alluri Sitarama Raju** | Madras Forest Act 1882 curbing *Podu* (shifting cultivation). |
