# 📌 Uniform Civil Code (UCC): Constitutional Mandate & Personal Laws

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Definition of UCC**: A standardized, common set of civil laws applicable uniformly to all citizens of India regardless of religion, gender, or community in personal civil matters.
- **Core Scope of Personal Matters**: Covers four primary domains:
  1. **Marriage**
  2. **Divorce & Dissolution of Marriage**
  3. **Inheritance & Succession of Property**
  4. **Adoption, Maintenance & Guardianship**
- **Constitutional Provision**: Placed under **Article 44** in **Part IV (Directive Principles of State Policy - DPSP)** of the Indian Constitution: *"The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India."*
- **Legal Enforceability**: Being a Directive Principle under Article 37, Article 44 is **non-justiciable** (cannot be directly enforced by courts of law), but fundamental in the governance of the country.
- **State Precedents**: 
  - **Goa**: Governed by the Portuguese Civil Code, 1867.
  - **Uttarakhand**: Became the first state in independent India to pass and implement a state-level Uniform Civil Code Act.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Analysis: Existing Personal Laws vs. Uniform Civil Code

*Contrast between fragmented religious personal laws and a unified civil framework:*

| Parameter | Religious Personal Laws System | Uniform Civil Code (UCC) System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Basis** | Based on religious scriptures, traditions, customs, and community-specific statutes | Based on a single, uniform statutory civil code passed by the legislature |
| **Applicability** | Separate laws for Hindus/Sikhs/Jains/Buddhists (Hindu Code Bills 1955–56), Muslims (Muslim Personal Law Shariat Application Act 1937), Christians (Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872), Parsis | Uniformly applicable to every citizen across all religious communities |
| **Marriage & Polygamy** | Varies widely across personal codes (e.g., restricted under Hindu Marriage Act, permitted under traditional Muslim personal law) | Universal standard of **monogamy** for all citizens |
| **Divorce Procedure** | Dissolution procedures vary according to personal statutes and customary practices | Standardized civil grounds for divorce adjudicated by family courts |
| **Gender Rights & Inheritance** | Disparities exist across ancestral inheritance, coparcenary, and testamentary succession | Equal succession and inheritance rights for sons, daughters, and spouses across all communities |

---

### 2. Prominent Judicial Milestones Related to UCC

*Landmark Supreme Court rulings interpreting Article 44:*

- **Shah Bano Case (1985)**: The Supreme Court upheld the right of a divorced Muslim woman to claim maintenance under Section 125 of the CrPC and urged the Parliament to enact a Uniform Civil Code to end discrimination.
- **Sarla Mudgal Case (1995)**: The apex court held that a Hindu husband cannot convert to Islam solely to solemnize a second marriage without dissolving the first, reiterating the constitutional vision of Article 44.
- **John Vallamattom Case (2003)**: Struck down Section 118 of the Indian Succession Act restricting Christian testamentary dispositions, noting that a common civil code helps national integration.
- **Shayara Bano Case (2017)**: The 5-judge Constitution Bench held the practice of *Talaq-e-Biddat* (Instant Triple Talaq) unconstitutional and void, leading to the enactment of the *Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019*.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Dimension | Personal / Civil Law | Criminal Law | Common Trap / Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uniformity in India** | **Currently Diverse** (Except general civil statutes like Contract Act, CPC) | **Completely Uniform** across all states and religions | Assuming India has no uniform legal codes at all |
| **DPSP Classification** | **Liberal-Intellectual** | — | Confusing Article 44 with Gandhian or Socialist DPSPs |
| **Enforceability** | **Non-enforceable in Courts** | Enforceable by Law | Assuming citizens can file a writ petition to mandate UCC enactment |
| **7th Schedule List** | **Concurrent List (List III)** | Concurrent List (List III) | Mistakenly thinking personal law is exclusively in the State List or Union List |
