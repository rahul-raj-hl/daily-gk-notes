# 📌 Factors Affecting Work Done in Physics

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Mathematical Formula**: $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F \cdot d \cdot \cos(\theta)$, where $F$ is force, $d$ is displacement, and $\theta$ is the angle between force and displacement[cite: 1].
- **Direct Factors**: Work done depends **strictly** on:
  1. Magnitude of the **applied force ($F$)**[cite: 1].
  2. Magnitude of the **displacement ($d$)**[cite: 1].
  3. **Angle ($\theta$)** between force and displacement vectors[cite: 1].
- **Independent Factor**: Work done does **not** directly depend on the **initial speed** or initial velocity of the object[cite: 1].
- **SI Unit**: **Joule (J)** or **Newton-meter ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$)**. Dimensions: $[\text{M}^1 \text{L}^2 \text{T}^{-2}]$.

---

## 📖 Main Concept & Conceptual Breakdown

### 1. The Physics of Work Done

In physics, work is said to be done when a force acts upon an object causing it to displace in the direction of or at an angle to the applied force[cite: 1].

$$\text{Work Done } (W) = F \cdot d \cdot \cos(\theta)$$[cite: 1]

- **Force ($F$)**: The applied push or pull measured in Newtons ($\text{N}$)[cite: 1]. Higher force yields greater work[cite: 1].
- **Displacement ($d$)**: The shortest distance moved by the object from its initial to final position[cite: 1]. Zero displacement results in **zero work**, regardless of how much force is applied[cite: 1].
- **Angle ($\theta$)**: Determines the effective component of force in the direction of motion[cite: 1].

---

### 2. Cases Based on Angle ($\theta$)

| Type of Work | Angle Range ($\theta$) | $\cos(\theta)$ Value | Example Scenario |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive Work** | $0^\circ \le \theta < 90^\circ$ | Positive ($> 0$) | A horse pulling a cart; a body falling under gravity ($\theta = 0^\circ$). |
| **Zero Work** | $\theta = 90^\circ$ | Zero ($\cos 90^\circ = 0$) | A coolie carrying luggage on his head walking on a level horizontal road ($\theta = 90^\circ$). |
| **Negative Work** | $90^\circ < \theta \le 180^\circ$ | Negative ($< 0$) | Force of friction acting on a moving vehicle; applying brakes to stop a car ($\theta = 180^\circ$). |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Scenario | Displacement ($d$) | Angle ($\theta$) | Work Done ($W$) | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pushing a fixed wall** | $d = 0$ | Any | **Zero** | No motion/displacement occurs[cite: 1]. |
| **Carrying luggage horizontally** | $d > 0$ | $\theta = 90^\circ$ | **Zero** | Gravity acts downwards, motion is horizontal. |
| **Lifting luggage vertically** | $d > 0$ | $\theta = 0^\circ$ | **Positive** | Force applied upwards matches movement direction. |
| **Frictional force** | $d > 0$ | $\theta = 180^\circ$ | **Negative** | Friction opposes the direction of motion. |

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

Work done upon a body depends on which of the following factors?
1. Force applied
2. Displacement
3. Angle between force and displacement
4. Initial velocity of the object

- [ ] 1 and 2 only
- [ ] 1, 2 and 3 only
- [x] 1, 2 and 3 only[cite: 1]
- [ ] 1, 2, 3 and 4

> Work done mathematically depends on force, displacement, and the cosine of the angle between them ($W = F \cdot d \cdot \cos\theta$)[cite: 1]. Initial speed does not directly affect work done[cite: 1].

---

### PYQ 2

When a porter lifts a luggage of $15\text{ kg}$ from the ground and puts it on his head $1.5\text{ m}$ above the ground, what is the work done by him on the luggage? ($g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$)

- [ ] $150\text{ J}$
- [x] $225\text{ J}$
- [ ] $200\text{ J}$
- [ ] $100\text{ J}$

> $W = m \cdot g \cdot h = 15\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 \times 1.5\text{ m} = 225\text{ J}$.

---

### PYQ 3

What is the work done when the direction of force applied and the direction of displacement are perpendicular to each other ($\theta = 90^\circ$)?

- [ ] Maximum Positive Work
- [ ] Negative Work
- [x] Zero Work
- [ ] Double the maximum work

> Since $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$, the work done is zero when force and displacement are perpendicular[cite: 1].

---

### PYQ 4

Which among the following is the relation between the SI unit and CGS unit of work?

- [ ] $1\text{ Joule} = 10^5\text{ ergs}$
- [x] $1\text{ Joule} = 10^7\text{ ergs}$
- [ ] $1\text{ Erg} = 10^7\text{ Joules}$
- [ ] $1\text{ Joule} = 10^{-7}\text{ ergs}$

> $1\text{ Joule} = 1\text{ N} \times 1\text{ m} = 10^5\text{ dynes} \times 10^2\text{ cm} = 10^7\text{ ergs}$.

---

### PYQ 5

The work done by the force of friction on a sliding block is always:

- [ ] Zero
- [ ] Positive
- [x] Negative
- [ ] Infinite

> Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion ($\theta = 180^\circ$), so $\cos(180^\circ) = -1$, resulting in negative work.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

According to the Work-Energy Theorem, the net work done on an object is equal to:

- [ ] Change in its potential energy
- [x] Change in its kinetic energy
- [ ] Change in its linear momentum
- [ ] Change in its total mass

> The Work-Energy Theorem states that the work done by the net force acting on a particle equals the change in its kinetic energy ($W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K$).

---

### Q2

If the force applied on an object is doubled and the displacement is halved, the work done will:

- [ ] Double
- [ ] Become four times
- [x] Remain unchanged
- [ ] Become zero

> $W' = (2F) \times (d / 2) \times \cos(\theta) = F \cdot d \cdot \cos(\theta) = W$. The work done remains unchanged.

---

### Q3

A person holds a heavy suitcase standing still at a bus stop for 30 minutes. What is the mechanical work done by the person on the suitcase?

- [ ] $30\text{ Joules}$
- [ ] Dependent on the weight of the suitcase
- [x] Zero
- [ ] Negative work

> Since the suitcase is standing still, its displacement is zero ($d = 0$). Therefore, the mechanical work done is zero[cite: 1].
