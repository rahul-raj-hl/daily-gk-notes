# 📌 Policy of Paramountcy & Imperial Expansion

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Introduced By**: **Lord Hastings** (Governor-General of Bengal, 1813–1823).
- **Core Concept**: The British East India Company claimed its authority/power was **"paramount"** (supreme) over all Indian states; hence, its interest was superior to that of Indian states.
- **Strategic Assertion**: Justified the annexation or threat of annexation of any Indian state to protect British imperial interests.
- **Military & Foreign Monopoly**: Deprived princely states of independent foreign policy, military autonomy, and sovereignty.
- **First Major Resistance**: Famous rebellion led by **Rani Chennamma** and Rayanna of Kittur (Karnataka) against the British assertion of paramountcy.

---

## 📖 Key Territorial Expansion Policies Matrix

### 1. Comparative Analysis of British Expansion Doctrines

| Policy / System | Initiator / Introduced By | Core Principle | Primary States Affected / Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Policy of Paramountcy** | **Lord Hastings** (1813–1823) | Company's power is supreme; can annex or control any state to protect imperial interests. | Kittur, Anglo-Maratha States, Gurkhas (Nepal), Punjab. |
| **Subsidiary Alliance** | **Lord Wellesley** (1798–1805) | Indian rulers surrender external affairs, keep British troops at state expense, and accept a British Resident. | Hyderabad (1798 - 1st), Mysore, Tanjore, Awadh, Peshwa. |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | **Lord Dalhousie** (1848–1856) | States under British protection lapse to Company control if ruler dies without a natural male heir. | Satara (1848 - 1st), Sambalpur, Jhansi, Nagpur, Udaipur. |
| **Ring Fence Policy** | **Warren Hastings** (1772–1785) | Creating buffer zones to defend Company borders from invaders (e.g., Afghan/Maratha threats). | Awadh (used as buffer state for Bengal). |

## 🧠 Exam Booster

| Strategic Point | Fact / Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rani Chennamma (Kittur)** | Led an armed rebellion against British Paramountcy when the Company tried to annex the small state of Kittur (Karnataka). |
| **Crown Paramountcy (1858)** | Post-1857 Revolt (Government of India Act 1858), Paramountcy transferred from the EIC to the **British Crown**; stopped further territorial annexations. |
| **Lapse of Paramountcy (1947)** | Under Section 7 of the **Indian Independence Act 1947**, British Paramountcy over princely states lapsed, making them legally independent. |
| **Role of British Resident** | Appointed under Subsidiary Alliance & Paramountcy; served as senior diplomats/spies who indirectly ran princely state administrations. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Governor-General | Associated Policy | Common Exam Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lord Hastings** | **Policy of Paramountcy** | Do NOT confuse Lord Hastings with Warren Hastings (Ring Fence Policy). |
| **Lord Wellesley** | **Subsidiary Alliance** | First to accept: **Nizam of Hyderabad** (1798). |
| **Lord Dalhousie** | **Doctrine of Lapse** | First annexed: **Satara** (1848). Awadh was annexed on grounds of **Misgovernance** (1856), NOT Lapse! |
