# 📌 Fundamental Rights vs. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Constitutional Balance**: Fundamental Rights (FRs - Part III) and Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs - Part IV) are **complementary and harmonious** pillars of the Indian Constitution[cite: 1].
- **Primacy of Certain DPSPs**: DPSPs are generally non-justiciable, but **Article 31C** gives primacy to laws implementing **Article 39(b) & 39(c)** over **Articles 14 (Equality) and 19 (Freedoms)**[cite: 1].
- **Key Amendments**: **25th Amendment (1971)** introduced Article 31C[cite: 1]. **42nd Amendment (1976)** tried to extend primacy to ALL DPSPs, but this expansion was struck down by the Supreme Court[cite: 1].
- **Landmark Case**: **Minerva Mills Case (1980)** established that the Constitution is founded on the **"bedrock of balance"** between Part III and Part IV[cite: 1].

---

## 📖 Main Concept & Legal Evolution

### 1. Evolution of the FR vs. DPSP Relationship

The legal relationship between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs has evolved across four major judicial milestones:

* **Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)**: The Supreme Court held that FRs prevail over DPSPs. DPSPs were deemed subsidiary to FRs.
* **Golaknath Case (1967)**: The Court ruled that FRs are sacrosanct and cannot be amended or diluted to give effect to DPSPs.
* **25th Amendment Act (1971)**: Introduced **Article 31C**[cite: 1], stating that no law giving effect to **Article 39(b) or 39(c)** shall be void for violating Articles 14 or 19[cite: 1].
* **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**: Upheld the validity of Article 31C specifically regarding Articles 39(b) and 39(c)[cite: 1].
* **Minerva Mills Case (1980)**: Struck down the 42nd Amendment expansion and affirmed that harmony between FRs and DPSPs is a **Basic Structure** of the Constitution[cite: 1].

---

### 2. Status of Article 31C Today

| Aspect | Current Legal Position |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Protected DPSPs** | **Article 39(b)** (Distribution of material resources) and **Article 39(c)** (Prevention of concentration of wealth)[cite: 1]. |
| **Overridden Rights** | **Article 14** (Right to Equality) and **Article 19** (Six Freedom Rights)[cite: 1]. |
| **Judicial Review** | A law enacted under Article 31C remains subject to **Judicial Review** to verify if it genuinely implements 39(b)/39(c)[cite: 1]. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Feature | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | Directive Principles (Part IV) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Negative obligations (limits state power). | Positive obligations (guides state action). |
| **Enforceability** | **Justiciable** (Enforceable in courts). | **Non-Justiciable** (Not directly enforceable in courts)[cite: 1]. |
| **Objective** | Establishes **Political Democracy**. | Establishes **Social and Economic Democracy**. |
| **Overriding Power** | Prevails in most general cases. | Prevails over Articles 14 & 19 **ONLY** via Article 31C for Art 39(b) & (c)[cite: 1]. |
