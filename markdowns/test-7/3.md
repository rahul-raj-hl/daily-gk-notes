# 📌 Performance Grant Scheme for Local Governing Bodies

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Primary Objective**: **Enhance financial revenue generation** and augment own financial resources of Gram Panchayats and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)[cite: 1].
- **Key Incentive**: Rewards local bodies for **better performance in revenue collection** and improved financial management practices[cite: 1].
- **Origin**: Strongly emphasized under the **14th Finance Commission** recommendations to strengthen local governance and service delivery[cite: 1].
- **Core Focus**: Boosting **Own Source Revenue (OSR)**, transparency, accountability, and fiscal autonomy[cite: 1].

---

## 📖 Main Concept & Structural Breakdown

### 1. Key Features of Performance Grants

The Central Government allocates performance grants as part of financial devolution to motivate local self-governments to become fiscally self-reliant rather than remaining purely dependent on central/state grants.

- **Incentivizing Fiscal Discipline**: Local bodies must meet specific benchmark criteria (e.g., publishing audited accounts, increasing property tax collection) to qualify[cite: 1].
- **Transparency & Accountability**: Ensures public access to financial statements and standard service delivery benchmarks[cite: 1].
- **Own Source Revenue (OSR)**: Refers to internal revenue generated through local property taxes, professional taxes, user charges, municipal fees, and trade licenses[cite: 1].

---

### 2. Finance Commissions & Local Body Grants

| Commission | Key Focus / Recommendation for Local Bodies | Grant Types |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **14th Finance Commission** | Introduced structured performance-based grants alongside basic grants[cite: 1]. | **Basic Grant** + **Performance Grant**[cite: 1] |
| **15th Finance Commission** | Tied grants to drinking water, sanitation, and air quality (for million-plus cities). | **Tied Grants** + **Untied Grants** |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Term | Meaning & Scope | Key Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Devolution of Funds** | Transfer of financial resources from central/state governments to local self-governments[cite: 1]. | Inter-governmental transfer[cite: 1] |
| **Own Source Revenue (OSR)** | Internal revenue generated independently by local bodies via local taxes and fees[cite: 1]. | Financial self-sustainability[cite: 1] |
| **Tied Grants** | Grants earmarked strictly for specific sectors (e.g., sanitation, water supply). | Restricted usage |
| **Untied Grants** | Grants provided to local bodies with full flexibility for local development needs. | Flexible usage |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

What is 'Devolution of Funds' in the context of Indian Panchayati Raj?

- [ ] Direct taxation levied on rural citizens by the Union Ministry
- [x] Transfer of financial resources from central or state governments to local governing bodies[cite: 1]
- [ ] Privatization of municipal water supply projects
- [ ] Foreign direct investment into rural Infrastructure

> Devolution refers to the statutory transfer of resources and power from higher tiers of government to local self-governing bodies[cite: 1].

---

### Q2

Which of the following is NOT typically classified under the Own Source Revenue (OSR) of an Urban Local Body?

- [ ] Property Tax
- [ ] Trade Licenses Fee
- [ ] Parking Fees
- [x] Grant-in-aid from the Central Government

> Grants-in-aid are central transfers, whereas property taxes, trade licenses, and parking fees constitute the internal Own Source Revenue (OSR) of local bodies[cite: 1].

---

### Q3

Under Article 280 of the Indian Constitution, who appoints the Finance Commission of India?

- [ ] Prime Minister
- [ ] Parliament
- [x] President of India
- [ ] Union Finance Minister

> The Finance Commission of India is constituted by the President of India every five years (or earlier) under Article 280.
