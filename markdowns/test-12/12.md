# 📌 Modern Periodic Law: Atomic Number & Periodic Classification

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Modern Periodic Law**: States that *"the physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their **atomic numbers ($Z$)**"*.
- **Discovery & Scientist**: Formulated in **1913** by English physicist **Henry Moseley** through his pioneering X-ray spectroscopy experiments ($\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$).
- **Fundamental Quantity**: The **Atomic Number ($Z$)** represents the number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom (which equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom), governing its valence shell configuration and chemical reactivity.
- **Resolution of Mendeleev’s Anomalies**: Arranging elements by atomic number solved major flaws of Mendeleev's atomic-mass-based table, such as:
  - **Position of Isotopes**: Isotopes of an element share the same atomic number and occupy the same position.
  - **Inverted Pairs / Anomalous Sequence**: Iodine ($Z = 53$, atomic mass $126.9$) correctly follows Tellurium ($Z = 52$, atomic mass $127.6$).
  - **Cobalt & Nickel**: Cobalt ($Z = 27$, mass $58.93$) correctly precedes Nickel ($Z = 28$, mass $58.69$).

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Evolution of Periodic Classification: Historical Milestones

| Law / Model | Proposed By (Year) | Fundamental Basis | Key Feature & Inherent Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Law of Triads** | **Johann Wolfgang Döbereiner (1817)** | Atomic Mass | Grouped elements in threes where the atomic mass of the middle element was roughly the mean of the other two (e.g., $\text{Li-Na-K}$, $\text{Ca-Sr-Ba}$, $\text{Cl-Br-I}$). Applicable to only a few elements. |
| **Law of Octaves** | **John Newlands (1865)** | Atomic Mass | Stated every eighth element had properties similar to the first (like musical notes). Worked only up to **Calcium ($Z = 20$)**. |
| **Periodic Curve** | **Lothar Meyer (1869)** | Atomic Volume vs. Atomic Mass | Plotted physical properties (atomic volume, melting point) against atomic mass; alkali metals occupied peaks of the curve. |
| **Mendeleev's Periodic Law** | **Dmitri Mendeleev (1869)** | **Atomic Mass** | Arranged 63 known elements in order of increasing atomic mass. Left gaps for undiscovered elements (**Eka-Boron = Scandium, Eka-Aluminium = Gallium, Eka-Silicon = Germanium**). |
| **Modern Periodic Law** | **Henry Moseley (1913)** | **Atomic Number ($Z$)** | Showed atomic number is more fundamental than atomic mass; forms the foundation of the modern 18-group, 7-period table. |

---

### 2. General Trends in the Modern Periodic Table

| Periodic Property | Trend Across a Period (Left to Right) | Reason (Left to Right) | Trend Down a Group (Top to Bottom) | Reason (Top to Bottom) |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Atomic Radius** | **Decreases** | Effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$) increases, pulling electrons closer. | **Increases** | Additional electron shells are added. |
| **Ionization Energy** | **Increases** | Smaller atomic radius and higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$ hold electrons tightly. | **Decreases** | Increased distance and shielding effect make electron removal easier. |
| **Electronegativity** | **Increases** | Stronger nuclear pull attracts shared electron pairs. | **Decreases** | Larger atomic size reduces nuclear attraction for electrons. |
| **Electron Affinity** | **Increases** | Smaller size and higher nuclear attraction release more energy on adding an electron. | **Decreases** | Larger atomic size reduces attraction for incoming electrons. |
| **Metallic Character** | **Decreases** | Tendency to lose electrons decreases. | **Increases** | Tendency to lose electrons increases due to weaker hold. |
| **Non-Metallic Character**| **Increases** | Tendency to gain electrons increases. | **Decreases** | Tendency to gain electrons decreases. |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Which of the following elements possesses the highest electron gain enthalpy (electron affinity) in the periodic table?

- [ ] Fluorine ($\text{F}$)
- [x] Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$)
- [ ] Bromine ($\text{Br}$)
- [ ] Oxygen ($\text{O}$)

> Although Fluorine is the most electronegative element, Chlorine has the highest electron affinity because the compact $2p$ subshell in Fluorine experiences higher electron-electron repulsions when an incoming electron is added.

---

### Q2

Newlands' Law of Octaves, which stated that every eighth element repeats properties of the first, broke down after which element?

- [ ] Magnesium
- [x] Calcium
- [ ] Iron
- [ ] Zinc

> Newlands' Law of Octaves was valid and applicable only up to Calcium ($Z = 20$); beyond calcium, the heavier elements did not fit the musical octave pattern.

---

### Q3

On moving from left to right across a period in the Modern Periodic Table, which of the following properties DECREASES?

- [ ] Ionization Energy
- [ ] Electronegativity
- [x] Atomic Radius
- [ ] Non-metallic Character

> As you move left to right across a period, the increasing positive nuclear charge pulls the valence electron cloud inward, causing the atomic radius to decrease.

---

### Q4

Döbereiner’s triad rule successfully applied to which of the following sets of elements?

- [ ] $\text{Na, Mg, Al}$
- [ ] $\text{Fe, Co, Ni}$
- [x] $\text{Li, Na, K}$
- [ ] $\text{F, Cl, Br}$

> Johann Wolfgang Döbereiner identified the alkali triad ($\text{Lithium}$, $\text{Sodium}$, $\text{Potassium}$), where the atomic mass of Sodium ($23$) equals the arithmetic mean of Lithium ($7$) and Potassium ($39$): $\frac{7 + 39}{2} = 23$.
