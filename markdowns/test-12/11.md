# 📌 Indigo Revolt (1859–60): The Shift to Bihar & Agrarian Transition

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Relocation of Operations**: Following the successful **Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** in Bengal, European planters collapsed their production in Bengal and shifted their base of operations to **Bihar** (primarily Champaran, Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, and Saran).
- **Core Cause of Bengal Revolt**: European planters coerced ryots into cultivating indigo under the exploitative **Dadni system** (forced advance contracts) on their most fertile land at non-remunerative prices.
- **Leaders of Bengal Revolt**: Initiated in Autumn 1859 at Govindpur village in Nadia district, led by **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Charan Biswas**.
- **Outcome in Bengal**: The colonial government appointed the **Indigo Commission (1860)** under W.S. Seton-Karr, which ruled that ryots could not be compelled to grow indigo, effectively ending coercive indigo planting in Bengal.
- **Exploitation in Bihar (*Tinkathia System*)**: In Bihar, planters instituted the **Tinkathia System**, forcing peasants to cultivate indigo on $\frac{3}{20}\text{th}$ of their landholding, which eventually culminated in Mahatma Gandhi’s historic **Champaran Satyagraha of 1917**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Matrix: Indigo Cultivation in Bengal vs. Bihar

| Dimension | Indigo in Bengal (*Nil Bidroha*) | Indigo in Bihar (*Champaran*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Period** | **1859 – 1860** | **Late 19th Century – 1917** |
| **Major Centers** | Nadia, Jessore, Pabna, Khulna, Malda | Champaran, Motihari, Bettiah, Muzaffarpur |
| **Coercive Mechanism** | **Dadni System** (fraudulent advance loans) & *Ryoti/Nij* cultivation | **Tinkathia System** (mandatory indigo on $\frac{3}{20}\text{th}$ of land) |
| **Key Leadership** | **Digambar Biswas & Bishnu Biswas** | **Raj Kumar Shukla** (invited Mahatma Gandhi) |
| **Intellectual Support** | • **Harish Chandra Mukherjee** (*The Hindoo Patriot*)<br>• **Dinabandhu Mitra** (*Nil Darpan* play) | Mahatma Gandhi, Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, Mazhar-ul-Haq, Mahadev Desai |
| **Inquiry Body & Result** | **Indigo Commission (1860)** $\rightarrow$ Ruled coercion illegal; planters moved to Bihar. | **Champaran Agrarian Committee (1917)** $\rightarrow$ Abolished Tinkathia; refunded $25\%$ illegal dues. |

---

### 2. Modes of Indigo Cultivation: Nij vs. Ryoti

| Cultivation System | Operating Methodology & Structural Flaw |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nij Cultivation** | • The planter cultivated indigo directly on land controlled by him (either bought or rented).<br>• Required large investments in bullocks, ploughs, and hired labor, which were difficult to mobilize during the rice-sowing season. |
| **Ryoti System** | • Planters forced the ryots (peasants) to sign a contract (*satta*) and gave cash advances (*dadni*).<br>• The farmer committed to cultivate indigo on at least $25\%$ of their holding.<br>• Led to a vicious cycle of generational debt since low prices never covered loan repayments. |
