# 📌 Chief Justice of India (CJI): Succession, Constitutional Provisions & Office Profile

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Head of Judiciary**: The **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** is the head of the Supreme Court of India and the highest-ranking judicial officer in the country.
- **Succession Order**:
  - **50th CJI**: Justice **D.Y. Chandrachud** (assumed office 9 November 2022, succeeding Justice U.U. Lalit).
  - **51st CJI**: Justice **Sanjiv Khanna** (took oath on 11 November 2024, serving until 13 May 2025).
  - **52nd CJI**: Justice **B.R. Gavai** (succeeded Justice Sanjiv Khanna in May 2025).
  - **53rd CJI**: Justice **Surya Kant** (sworn in on 24 November 2025, serving until February 2027).
- **Appointment Authority**: Appointed by the **President of India** under **Article 124(2)** of the Constitution, strictly adhering to the convention of appointing the senior-most sitting judge of the Supreme Court.
- **Retirement Age**: Judges of the Supreme Court, including the CJI, hold office until attaining the age of **65 years** (under Article 124(2)).

### 2. Key Constitutional Provisions Related to Supreme Court & CJI

| Article | Constitutional Subject / Mandate | Key Exam Dimension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Article 124** | Establishment and Constitution of the Supreme Court | Appointment of CJI & judges by the President; tenure up to 65 years. |
| **Article 126** | Appointment of **Acting Chief Justice** | Appointed by the President when the office of CJI is vacant or CJI is unable to perform duties. |
| **Article 127** | Appointment of **Ad-hoc Judges** | Appointed by CJI with prior consent of the President for quorum shortfall. |
| **Article 129** | Supreme Court to be a **Court of Record** | Powers to punish for contempt of itself. |
| **Article 130** | Seat of the Supreme Court | New Delhi (or other places as decided by CJI with President's approval). |
| **Article 131** | **Original Jurisdiction** | Federal disputes (Centre vs. State / State vs. State). |
| **Article 143** | **Advisory Jurisdiction** | Power of the President to consult the Supreme Court on questions of law/fact. |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Under which Article of the Indian Constitution does the President appoint an 'Acting Chief Justice of India' when the office of CJI is vacant?

- [ ] Article 124
- [ ] Article 125
- [x] Article 126
- [ ] Article 127

> Article 126 provides that when the office of the Chief Justice of India is vacant or when the CJI is unable to perform duties, the duties of the office shall be performed by another judge of the Court appointed by the President.

---

### Q2

Who holds the distinction of being the longest-serving Chief Justice of India in Indian judicial history?

- [ ] Justice P. N. Bhagwati
- [x] Justice Y. V. Chandrachud
- [ ] Justice K. G. Balakrishnan
- [ ] Justice Dipak Misra

> Justice Y. V. Chandrachud served as the 16th CJI for 7 years and 139 days (from February 1978 to July 1985), making his tenure the longest in history.

### Q3

The administrative authority of the Chief Justice of India to allocate cases and constitute benches in the Supreme Court is commonly known as:

- [ ] Suo Motu Authority
- [x] Master of the Roster
- [ ] Rule of Law Discretion
- [ ] Advisory Prerogative

> The CJI is the sole 'Master of the Roster' who possesses the exclusive authority to constitute benches and allocate judicial matters to different benches of the Supreme Court.

- [ ] 101st Constitutional Amendment Act
- [ ] 103rd Constitutional Amendment Act
