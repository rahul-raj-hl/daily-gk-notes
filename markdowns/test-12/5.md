# 📌 Dispersion of Light: White Light and the Visible Spectrum

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **White** light produces a continuous spectrum similar to sunlight.
- **Nature of Sunlight**: Sunlight is polychromatic **white light** composed of a continuous band of visible wavelengths ranging from approximately **$380\text{ nm}$ (violet) to $700\text{ nm}$ (red)**.
- **Dispersion of Light**: The phenomenon of splitting white light into its seven constituent colors when passing through a transparent refracting medium (such as a glass prism) is called **dispersion**.
- **Discovery**: **Sir Isaac Newton (1665)** was the first to demonstrate using a glass prism that white sunlight consists of seven distinct colors, and recombining them through an inverted prism reproduces white light.
- **Monochromatic Light**: Single-wavelength light (such as pure **yellow** or **blue** light) bends upon refraction but does not produce a multicolored continuous spectrum like sunlight.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Visible Light Spectrum (VIBGYOR)

| Color Band | Approximate Wavelength ($\lambda$) | Frequency ($\nu$) | Speed in Glass | Angle of Deviation ($\delta$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Violet (V)** | **$\approx 380 - 450\text{ nm}$** *(Shortest)* | **Highest** | **Slowest** | **Maximum Deviation** |
| **Indigo (I)** | $\approx 450 - 475\text{ nm}$ | High | Slow | Very High |
| **Blue (B)** | $\approx 475 - 495\text{ nm}$ | Moderate-High | Moderate-Slow | High |
| **Green (G)** | $\approx 495 - 570\text{ nm}$ | Moderate | Moderate | Medium |
| **Yellow (Y)** | $\approx 570 - 590\text{ nm}$ | Moderate-Low | Moderate-Fast | Low (Mean Ray) |
| **Orange (O)** | $\approx 590 - 620\text{ nm}$ | Low | Fast | Very Low |
| **Red (R)** | **$\approx 620 - 750\text{ nm}$** *(Longest)* | **Lowest** | **Fastest** | **Minimum Deviation** |

---

### 2. Optical Concepts: Monochromatic vs. Polychromatic Light

| Property / Parameter | Polychromatic Light (e.g., White Light / Sunlight) | Monochromatic Light (e.g., Laser, Sodium D-line) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Component Wavelengths** | Consists of multiple/continuous wavelengths across the visible spectrum. | Consists of a **single discrete wavelength / frequency**. |
| **Behavior in Prism** | Undergoes **refraction and dispersion**, splitting into a multi-colored **VIBGYOR** spectrum. | Undergoes **refraction (bending) only**; no color splitting occurs. |
| **Natural Examples** | Direct sunlight, tungsten incandescent bulbs, broad-spectrum white LEDs. | Low-pressure sodium vapour lamps ($589\text{ nm}$ yellow), Helium-Neon lasers ($632.8\text{ nm}$ red). |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First to Discover Dispersion** | **Sir Isaac Newton (1665)** | Used glass prism experiment |

| **Primary Colors of Light** | **Red, Green, Blue (RGB)** | Additive primary colors |
| **Secondary Colors of Light** | **Cyan, Magenta, Yellow** | Combinations of two primary colors |
| **Mean Optical Ray** | **Yellow Light** | Used to calculate mean refractive index |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Optical Phenomenon | Physical Cause | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dispersion** | Splitting of white light into constituent colors due to different speeds/wavelengths in a medium | Confusing dispersion with simple refraction or diffraction |
| **Scattering of Light** | Redirection of light in all directions by atmospheric gas molecules/particles ($I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$) | Blaming dispersion for the blue color of the sky (it is **Rayleigh Scattering**) |
| **Rainbow Formation** | Combined effect of **Dispersion, Refraction, and Internal Reflection** inside water droplets | Assuming rainbow formation is purely total internal reflection |
| **Deviation vs. Wavelength** | • Red $\rightarrow$ Maximum $\lambda$, Minimum Deviation<br>• Violet $\rightarrow$ Minimum $\lambda$, Maximum Deviation | Inverting the deviation order between Red and Violet |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "VIBGYOR: VIOLET BENDS VIGOROUSLY, RED RUNS RELAXED"**
> - **Violet Bends Vigorously** $\rightarrow$ Violet has the highest deviation (shortest $\lambda$)
> - **Red Runs Relaxed** $\rightarrow$ Red has the lowest deviation (longest $\lambda$)
> - **White Light** $\rightarrow$ The mother of all seven VIBGYOR colors

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

What happens when monochromatic yellow light is passed through a transparent glass prism?

- [ ] It splits into seven rainbow colors.
- [ ] It splits into green and red rays.
- [x] It deviates/refracts without splitting into different colors.
- [ ] It turns into white light.

> Monochromatic light contains only a single wavelength; therefore, it undergoes refraction (bending) according to Snell's Law without exhibiting spectral dispersion.

---

### Q2

Which color in the visible spectrum is designated as the 'Mean Ray' for calculating the mean refractive index of an optical material?

- [ ] Green
- [x] Yellow
- [ ] Blue
- [ ] Orange

> Yellow light lies near the middle of the visible spectrum and is conventionally taken as the mean spectral ray for determining optical dispersion indices.

---

### Q3

The formation of a rainbow in the sky after a rain shower is primarily caused by which combination of optical phenomena?

- [ ] Reflection and interference
- [x] Dispersion, refraction, and internal reflection of sunlight by tiny water droplets
- [ ] Diffraction and polarization
- [ ] Total absorption of blue light

> Suspended raindrops act like tiny prisms that refract incoming sunlight, disperse it into its component colors, internally reflect it, and refract it again upon exiting toward the observer.

Which of the following represents the correct sequence of primary additive colors in physics?

- [ ] Red, Yellow, Blue
- [x] Red, Green, Blue
- [ ] Cyan, Magenta, Yellow

> The three primary colors of light are Red, Green, and Blue (RGB); combining them in equal intensities yields white light.
