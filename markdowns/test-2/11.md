# 📌 Bhagoria Festival and Major Tribal Festivals of India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Bhagoria** (or **Bhagoria Haat**) is a major tribal festival celebrated a **week before Holika Dahan**.
- Celebrated mainly by the **Bhil** and **Bhilala** tribes in **Madhya Pradesh** (Jhabua, Alirajpur) and **Maharashtra**.
- It marks the end of the harvest season and welcomes the spring season.
- Serves as a **marriage fair** (matchmaking market) where young men and women choose their life partners.
- Name derived from the word **'Bhag'** (*to run away* / elope), symbolizing the custom where young couples elope with family acceptance later.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Bhagoria Festival & Haat (Marketplace)

*Cultural, economic, and social gathering of indigenous communities.*

- **Timing**: Celebrated for 7 days in the Hindu lunar month of **Phalguna** (February–March), right before **Holika Dahan** (*Chhoti Holi*).
- **Key Tribes**: **Bhil**, **Bhilala**, and **Patelia** communities.
- **Main Regions**: **Jhabua** and **Alirajpur** districts of Madhya Pradesh, along with parts of Maharashtra.
- **Unique Feature**: Acts as a **Bhagoria Haat** (*local weekly market/fair*), combining crop harvest celebration with matchmaking rituals.

---

### 2. Other Famous Tribal Fairs and Festivals

*Important tribal events frequently asked in competitive exams.*

- **Baneshwar Fair** (*Rajasthan*): Known as the **"Kumbh of Tribals"**, celebrated by the Bhil tribe at the confluence (meeting point) of Som, Mahi, and Jakham rivers.
- **Hornbill Festival** (*Nagaland*): Called the **"Festival of Festivals"**, showcases the traditions of 16 major Naga tribes.
- **Sarhul** (*Jharkhand*): Spring festival celebrated by Oraon, Munda, and Ho tribes worshipping the **Sal tree**.
- **Medaram Jathara** (*Telangana*): Asia's second-largest fair after Kumbh Mela, celebrated by the **Koya tribe**.
