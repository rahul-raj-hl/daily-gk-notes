# 📌 Ashokan Pillars and Mauryan Architecture

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Ashokan Pillars** were erected during the 3rd century BCE under Emperor Ashoka.
- Materials used: Made almost entirely of **polished stone**, specifically **Chunar sandstone** (sourced from Chunar, Uttar Pradesh).
- Built as **monolithic structures** (carved out of a single large piece of stone).
- Used to spread **Dhamma** (moral and ethical duties) and Buddhist teachings across India.
- Famous example: **Sarnath Lion Capital**, which serves as India's **National Emblem**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Structure and Materials of Ashokan Pillars

*Craftsmanship of Mauryan monumental architecture.*

- **Material**: Carved from single pieces of **Chunar sandstone** or spotted red sandstone.
- **Monolithic Nature**: Unlike Persian pillars (made of multiple joined stone pieces), Ashokan pillars are carved out of a **single stone block**.
- **Mauryan Polish**: Famous for an extraordinarily high glass-like polish (*lustrous polish*).
- **Structure Parts**: Comprises a long tapering **Shaft**, an inverted lotus **Capital** (top head), an **Abacus** (circular or square base), and an **Animal Motif** (lion, bull, elephant) on top.

---

### 2. Languages, Scripts, and Edicts

*How Ashoka communicated with his subjects.*

- **Main Scripts Used**: **Brahmi** (most common in India), **Kharosthi** (North-West India), **Aramaic**, and **Greek** (Afghanistan region).
- **Primary Language**: **Prakrit** (language spoken by common people) and Greek/Aramaic in border areas.
- **Decipherment** (reading the script): Brahmi script was first decoded in **1837** by **James Prinsep**.
- **Important Inscriptions**:
  - **13th Rock Edict**: Mentions the **Kalinga War** (261 BCE) and Ashoka's shift toward peace.
  - **Rummindei Pillar Inscription** (Nepal): Mentions tax reduction in Lumbini (birthplace of Lord Buddha).

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Parameter | Ashokan (Mauryan) Pillars | Persian (Achaemenid) Pillars |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Construction** | **Monolithic** (Carved from 1 single stone block) | Constructed by joining multiple stone pieces together |
| **Placement** | Stood independently in public spaces/trade routes | Built inside imperial palace buildings/halls |
| **Purpose** | Propagate moral duties (*Dhamma*) and teachings | Symbol of royal power and palace decoration |
