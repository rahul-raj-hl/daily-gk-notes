# 📌 Parliamentary Motions (Calling Attention Motion & Zero Hour)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Calling Attention Motion** is an **Indian innovation** introduced in **1954**.
- It is **explicitly mentioned** in the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.
- Purpose: Used by an MP (Member of Parliament) to draw a minister's attention to an **urgent matter of public importance**.
- **Zero Hour** is also an **Indian innovation** (introduced in **1962**), but it is **NOT mentioned** in the Rules of Procedure.
- Both devices are unique to the Indian parliamentary system and not borrowed from any foreign constitution.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Calling Attention Motion

*An Indian device to seek official statements from ministers.*

- **Origin**: An **Indian parliamentary innovation** introduced in **1954**.
- **Mentioned in Rules**: Yes, it is **explicitly mentioned** in the official Rules of Procedure.
- **Purpose**: To request a Minister to give an official statement on an urgent matter of public importance.
- **Nature**: Combines features of a question and a short debate to ensure government accountability.

---

### 2. Zero Hour (*Tunya Kaal*)

*Informal time slot to raise urgent issues without prior notice.*

- **Origin**: Another **Indian parliamentary innovation** (in practice since **1962**).
- **Mentioned in Rules**: **No**, it is an informal device **NOT mentioned** in the Rules of Procedure.
- **Timing**: Starts immediately after the **Question Hour** (around 12:00 PM) and lasts until the regular agenda for the day is taken up.
- **Notice Period**: MPs can raise issues **without prior notice** (unlike Calling Attention Motion, which requires formal notice).
