# 📌 Depositional Landforms of Rivers

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Depositional Landforms (landforms made by deposited sediments)** are formed when a river **drops (deposits)** the materials it carries.
- Important depositional landforms are **Floodplains, Natural Levees, Deltas and Alluvial Fans**.
- **Oxbow Lake** is mainly an **erosional + depositional landform**, not a purely depositional one.
- SSC frequently asks **Landform ↔ Formation**, **Erosion vs Deposition**, and **Outer Bank vs Inner Bank**.
- Remember: **Outer Bank = Erosion**, **Inner Bank = Deposition**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### What are Depositional Landforms?

*These are landforms formed when a river deposits (drops) sand, silt and mud.*

- River carries **Sediments (small rock particles)**.
- When river speed becomes slow, sediments get deposited.
- Deposition mostly happens in the **middle and lower course** of a river.
- These landforms are generally **flat and fertile**.

---

### Meander

*A Meander is a curved (bent) path of a river.*

- Common in **plain areas**.
- River flows in a zig-zag pattern.
- Two important sides:
  - **Outer Bank (Concave Side)** → Erosion (cutting)
  - **Inner Bank (Convex Side)** → Deposition (sediment settles)

> ⭐ SSC Favourite:
> **Concave = Erosion**
>
> **Convex = Deposition**

---

### Oxbow Lake

*An Oxbow Lake is a horseshoe-shaped lake formed when a river cuts off a meander.*

- Formed when a **Meander Loop** gets separated from the main river.
- Shape looks like a **horseshoe (U-shape)**.
- Found in old-stage (mature) rivers.
- Formed due to **both erosion and deposition**.
- **Deposition occurs on the Convex (Inner) Side**, not the Concave side.

---

### Natural Levees

*Natural Levees are raised banks formed naturally along river sides.*

- Formed during floods.
- Heavy sediments are deposited near river banks.
- They act like **Natural Embankments (raised walls)**.
- Help reduce the spread of floodwater during normal flow.

---

### Floodplains

*Floodplains are flat lands formed beside rivers.*

- Formed by repeated deposition of **Alluvium (fertile river soil)**.
- Floodwater spreads fine sediments over nearby land.
- Soil becomes **highly fertile**.
- Ideal for farming.

---

### Other Important Depositional Landforms

| Landform | Formed Where |
|----------|--------------|
| Delta | River Mouth (where river meets sea/lake) |
| Alluvial Fan | Foot of Mountains |
| Floodplain | Along River Banks |
| Natural Levee | Beside River Channel |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

### 🌊 **"COE-COD" Trick**

- **C**oncave (Outer) → **E**rosion
- **C**onvex (Inner) → **D**eposition

👉 **COE-COD**

**COE** = Concave → Erosion

**COD** = Convex → Deposition

Another easy trick:

🏇 **Horse = Oxbow Lake**

Think of a **Horse Shoe** → Oxbow Lake.

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

With reference to depositional landforms, consider the following statements:

1. Oxbow lakes are formed due to deposition on the concave side of meanders.
2. Natural levees are raised embankments formed due to sediment deposition along riverbanks.
3. Floodplains become highly fertile because floods deposit nutrient-rich sediments.

How many statements are correct?

- [ ] Only one
- [x] Only two
- [ ] All three
- [ ] None

> Explanation: Statement 1 is incorrect because deposition occurs on the **Convex (Inner) Side**, while erosion occurs on the **Concave (Outer) Side**.

---

### PYQ 2

Natural levees are formed due to:

- [ ] Wind erosion
- [ ] Glacial action
- [x] Deposition of sediments during floods
- [ ] Volcanic activity

> Explanation: Floods deposit heavy sediments near river banks, forming natural levees.

---

### PYQ 3

Floodplains are generally:

- [ ] Rocky
- [ ] Desert regions
- [x] Fertile agricultural lands
- [ ] Mountain peaks

> Explanation: Floodplains receive nutrient-rich alluvium during floods.
