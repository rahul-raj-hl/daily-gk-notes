# 📌 Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Samhita (BNSS) & Default Bail

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Samhita (BNSS), 2023** replaced the old **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973**.
- **Default Bail** (*Statutory Bail*) is granted if police fail to file a chargesheet (investigation report) within the specified timeline.
- **60 Days**: Investigation timeline for **minor offences** (punishable with less than 10 years imprisonment).
- **90 Days**: Investigation timeline for **serious offences** (punishable with death, life imprisonment, or 10+ years imprisonment).
- Designed to protect fundamental personal liberty (*Article 21*) and prevent prolonged pre-trial detention.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Default Bail Provisions under BNSS

*Statutory right to bail when police fail to complete investigation in time.*

- **Concept**: If the investigating agency (police) fails to submit the chargesheet before the magistrate within the statutory limit, the accused gets an automatic right to bail.
- **Minor Offences Timeline**: **60 Days**.
- **Heinous / Serious Offences Timeline**: **90 Days**.
- **Section Reference**: Covered under **Section 187 of BNSS** (corresponding to Section 167 of the former CrPC).

---

### 2. The Three New Criminal Laws in India

*Major reform in the Indian Criminal Justice System (Enforced from July 1, 2024).*

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**: Replaced the **Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860**.
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Samhita (BNSS)**: Replaced the **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973**.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)**: Replaced the **Indian Evidence Act, 1872**.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BNSS** (*Suraksha*) | **CrPC, 1973** | Criminal Procedure, Arrest, Bail & Trial Process |

| **BSA** (*Sakshya*) | **Indian Evidence Act, 1872** | Rules of Evidence & Digital Proof |
