# 📌 Peasant Mobilization in Non-Cooperation Movement: Bihar, Bengal & Midnapore

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Evaluation of All Three Statements**:
  - **Statement 1 is CORRECT**: In both **Bihar and Bengal**, the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)** achieved unprecedented mass depth due to the widespread active involvement of the peasantry against colonial and landlord oppression.
  - **Statement 2 is CORRECT**: In **Bihar**, peasants organized against oppressive British indigo planters and zamindars under the emerging network of **Kisan Sabhas** (led by leaders like Swami Vidyanand and later institutionalized by Swami Sahajanand Saraswati).
  - **Statement 3 is CORRECT**: In **Midnapore (Bengal)**, the dominant **Mahishya peasant community** was successfully mobilized by nationalist leader **Birendranath Sasmal** to refuse payment of taxes imposed by the newly established **Union Boards**, forcing the British government to withdraw them.
  - **Correct Answer**: **1, 2 and 3**.
- **Transformation of the Movement**: The Non-Cooperation Movement transitioned from an urban middle-class protest into a nationwide mass movement primarily because local agrarian grievances (rent exploitation, illegal cesses, Union Board taxes, and forest laws) merged into Mahatma Gandhi’s call for *Swaraj*.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Regional Matrix: Peasant Movements during the Non-Cooperation Era (1920–1922)

| Region / Province | Core Leadership | Key Opponent / Exploitative System | Specific Form of Agitation / Peasant Resistance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Midnapore (Bengal)** | **Birendranath Sasmal** (*Deshapran*) | **Union Board Taxes** & Village Self-Government Act (1919) | Complete **no-tax campaign**; Mahishya cultivators boycotted local courts and Union Boards, forcing the administration to revoke the tax. |
| **Bihar** | **Swami Vidyanand**, Rahul Sankrityayan, Sri Krishna Sinha | **Darbhanga Raj Zamindars** & British Indigo Planters | Boycott of estate officials, refusal to perform *begar* (forced unpaid labour), filing cases under the Kisan Sabha banner. |
| **Awadh (United Provinces)** | **Baba Ramchandra**, Jawaharlal Nehru, Gauri Shankar Mishra | Taluqdars / Big Landlords (*Awadh Rent Act*) | Formation of **Oudh Kisan Sabha (Oct 1920)**; non-payment of illegal cesses (*nazrana*, *bedakhli* eviction resistance). |
| **Eka Movement (UP)** | **Madari Pasi** | High rents ($> 50\%$ above recorded rates) & oppressive grain-sharing | Peasants took religious oaths on the River Ganga to pay only recorded rents and refuse forced labor. |
| **Andhra (Rampa / Gudem Hills)** | **Alluri Sitarama Raju** | Madras Forest Act, 1882 (*Podu* shifting cultivation ban) | Guerrilla warfare by tribal peasants against police stations using traditional weapons (*Rampa Rebellion*). |
| **Malabar (Kerala)** | Ali Musaliyar, Variyankunnath Kunjahammed Haji | British Authorities & Hindu *Jenmis* (Landlords) | **Moplah (Mappila) Rebellion (1921)**; tenant agrarian grievances merged into the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement. |

### 2. Deep Dive: Birendranath Sasmal and the Midnapore Movement

| Aspect | Historical Detail & Significance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Leader** | **Birendranath Sasmal (1881–1934)**, a barrister revered as **"Deshapran"** (Soul of the Nation), served as the unquestioned mass leader of Contai and Midnapore. |
| **The Peasant Base** | Mobilized the **Mahishyas** (a powerful agrarian caste of owner-cultivators and landless tenants who formed the backbone of Midnapore's economy). |
| **The Grievance** | Under the *Bengal Village Self-Government Act of 1919*, the British established **Union Boards**, imposing new local taxation (*Chaukidari* and municipal levies) and expanding bureaucratic surveillance. |
| **The Action & Triumph** | Sasmal directed a disciplined **refusal to pay Union Board taxes**. Villagers boycotted revenue collectors and auctions. In **December 1921**, the Bengal Government conceded and abolished Union Boards in Midnapore. |
