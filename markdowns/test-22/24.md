# 📌 Sammakka Saralamma Jatara: Asia's Largest Tribal Congregation & Koya Heritage

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Statement Evaluation**:
  - **Statement 1 is INCORRECT**: Sammakka and her daughter Saralamma fought against the Kakatiya ruler **King Prataparudra** (who levied oppressive taxes during a severe famine/drought), *not* King Ganapati Deva.
  - **Statement 2 is CORRECT**: The festival is celebrated primarily by the **Koya Tribe** (a major Scheduled Tribe inhabiting Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Odisha).
  - **Correct Option**: **Only 2**.
- **Global & National Scale**: Known as **Medaram Jatara**, it is recognized as **Asia's largest tribal festival** and the second-largest religious gathering in India after the Kumbh Mela (often referred to as the **"Kumbh Mela of the South"** or **"Kumbh of the Tribals"**).
- **Location & Frequency**: Held **biennially** (once every two years) in the month of *Magha* (January/February on *Magha Purnima*) at **Medaram village**, Mulugu district, Telangana, located inside the **Eturnagaram Wildlife Sanctuary** (Dandakaranya forest region).
- **Core Ritual Practice**: Devotees offer **jaggery** (locally revered as **"Bangaram"** / Gold) equal to their body weight (*Tulabharam*) to the ancestral tribal deities.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Cultural & Historical Pillars of Medaram Jatara

| Cultural Dimension | Specific Tradition / Historical Fact | Signficance in Exam Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Historical Conflict** | Revolt against **Kakatiya King Prataparudra** | Resistance against unjust tax extraction during drought; both mother and daughter fought bravely and attained martyrdom. |
| **Presiding Deities** | **Sammakka** (Mother) and **Saralamma / Sarakka** (Daughter) | Worshipped as manifestations of tribal power, justice, and community protection. |
| **Sacred Stream** | **Jampanna Vagu** (tributary of Godavari River) | Named after Sammakka’s son **Jampanna**, who died in battle defending the tribe against the Kakatiya army. Devotees take a sacred holy bath here. |
| **Sacred Offering** | **Jaggery (*Bangaram*)** | Devotees weigh themselves against pure jaggery and offer it to the deities. |
| **Priestly Order** | **Koya Tribal Priests (*Vaddes*)** | Strictly non-Vedic, indigenous nature-worship rituals where deities are represented by sacred vermillion-smeared bamboo poles installed on earthen platforms (*Gaddelu*). |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Historical / Cultural Entity | Accurate Context | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kakatiya Ruler: Prataparudra vs. Ganapati Deva** | • **Prataparudra**: King during the Sammakka-Saralamma rebellion.<br>• **Ganapati Deva**: Earlier 13th-century Kakatiya ruler (Father of Rudrama Devi). | Swapping Prataparudra with Ganapati Deva or Rudrama Devi |
| **Koya Tribe vs. Chenchu / Gond Tribe** | Sammakka Jatara is strictly organized and ministered by the **Koya Tribe**. | Assuming it is organized by the Chenchus or Lambadas |
| **Medaram Jatara vs. Nagoba Jatara** | • **Medaram Jatara**: Koya Tribe, Mulugu district.<br>• **Nagoba Jatara**: Mesram Gond Tribe, Adilabad district. | Confusing the two premier tribal Jataras of Telangana |
| **Offering Material: Gold vs. Jaggery** | Devotees offer **Jaggery** which is symbolically referred to as *Gold* (*Bangaram*). | Taking "Bangaram" literally as real physical gold metal in multiple-choice questions |
