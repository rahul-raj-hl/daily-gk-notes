# 📌 Permanent Settlement (1793): Lord Cornwallis & Colonial Land Revenue Systems

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Governor-General**: **Lord Charles Cornwallis** introduced the **Permanent Settlement** (*Istamarari / Bandobast*) in **1793**.
- **Regions Covered**: Implemented across **Bengal, Bihar, Odisha**, the Northern Circars (Madras Presidency), and the Banaras division (covering approximately **$19\%$ of British India**).
- **Core Concept**:
  - Zamindars were recognized as the **absolute proprietors/owners of the land** with hereditary transfer rights.
  - The land revenue demand of the State was **fixed permanently in perpetuity**, meaning it could never be increased in the future.
- **Revenue Distribution Ratio**:
  - **$\frac{10}{11}\text{th}$ ($89\%$)** of the collected rent went to the **East India Company**.
  - **$\frac{1}{11}\text{th}$ ($11\%$)** was retained by the **Zamindar** for collection costs and profit.
- **Architect of the Plan**: Planned and devised primarily by **Sir John Shore** (President of the Board of Revenue, who later succeeded Cornwallis as Governor-General).
- **Sunset Clause (*Sunset Law*)**: If a zamindar failed to deposit the fixed revenue by the sunset of the specified cutoff date, their estate (*zamindari*) was confiscated and auctioned to the highest bidder.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Matrix: The Three British Land Revenue Systems

| Land Revenue System | Introduced In (Year) | Introduced By / Key Pioneer | Geographic Coverage & Territorial Extent | Unit of Assessment & Tax Collector | Key Features & Weaknesses |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Permanent Settlement (*Zamindari*)** | **1793** | **Lord Cornwallis** & Sir John Shore | **Bengal, Bihar, Odisha**, Banaras, Northern Circars ($\approx \mathbf{19\%}$ of British India). | **Zamindar (Intermediary)** | • Revenue fixed in perpetuity.<br>• $\frac{10}{11}\text{th}$ to Company, $\frac{1}{11}\text{th}$ to Zamindar.<br>• Heavy eviction and peasant exploitation via sub-infeudation. |
| **Ryotwari System** | **1820** | **Thomas Munro** & Captain Alexander Read | **Madras, Bombay Presidencies**, parts of Assam & Coorg ($\approx \mathbf{51\%}$ of British India). | **Ryot (Direct Cultivator/Peasant)** | • Direct settlement between State and cultivator.<br>• Revenue not fixed; revised periodically every 20–30 years (ranged from $45\text{--}55\%$). |
| **Mahalwari System** | **1822 / 1833** | **Holt Mackenzie** (1822) & **Robert Merttins Bird** (1833) | **North-Western Provinces, Central Provinces, Punjab, Gangetic Valley** ($\approx \mathbf{30\%}$ of British India). | **Village Community / Mahal** (Collected by *Lambardar* / Village Headman) | • Unit of settlement was the entire village (*Mahal*).<br>• Joint responsibility of village co-sharers to pay revenue. |

### 2. Landmark Reforms of Governor-General Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793)

| Reform Sphere | Administrative Milestone | Historic Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Land Revenue** | **Permanent Settlement (1793)** | Established stable fiscal returns and created a loyal socio-political class of landlords. |
| **Civil Administration** | **Father of Modern Civil Services in India** | Professionalized the Covenanted Civil Services, strictly barred private trade, and introduced merit-based promotions. |
| **Judicial Administration** | **Cornwallis Code (1793)** | Separated **Revenue administration from Judicial administration** (divested Collectors of magisterial powers). |
| **Police Reforms** | **Regular Police Force & Thanas** | Abolished zamindari police duties; created police circles (*Thanas*) headed by Indian **Darogas** under District Superintendents. |
| **Military & Treaties** | **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792)** | Defeated Tipu Sultan, culminating in the **Treaty of Srirangapatna (1792)** where Tipu ceded half of his kingdom. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| System / Governor | Key Distinguishing Fact | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cornwallis vs. Warren Hastings** | • **Warren Hastings**: Introduced Quinquennial (5-year) and Annual auction systems.<br>• **Cornwallis**: Made the settlement **Permanent (1793)**. | Confusing early experimental auctions (Hastings) with Permanent Settlement (Cornwallis) |
| **Sir John Shore vs. Lord Cornwallis** | • **John Shore**: Conceptualized and planned the 10-year decennial scheme.<br>• **Cornwallis**: Upgraded it to a **Permanent** system. | Selecting Shore as the Governor-General who enacted it |
| **Ryotwari vs. Mahalwari** | • **Ryotwari**: Individual peasant settlement (Munro, Madras/Bombay).<br>• **Mahalwari**: Entire village unit/Mahal settlement (Mackenzie, NWP/Punjab). | Mixing up the geographical regions of Ryotwari and Mahalwari |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "CORNWALLIS SETTLED PERMANENTLY, MUNRO MET THE RYOT, HOLT MANAGED THE MAHAL"**
> - **Cornwallis $\rightarrow$ Permanent Settlement** (Zamindari - Bengal, 1793)
> - **Munro $\rightarrow$ Ryotwari** (Individual Cultivators - Madras, 1820)
> - **Holt Mackenzie $\rightarrow$ Mahalwari** (Village Mahals - North India, 1822)
> - **Fraction Rule**: $\frac{10}{11}$ goes to the Company, $\frac{1}{11}$ stays with the Landlord.
