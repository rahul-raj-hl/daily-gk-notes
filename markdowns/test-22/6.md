# 📌 Tilak Sendur Temple: Rock-Cut Architecture & Cave Sanctuaries of Central India

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Temple**: **Tilak Sendur Temple** (located in the Narmadapuram / Hoshangabad region of **Madhya Pradesh**).
- **Architectural Feature**: A historic **cave-like shrine carved out of a massive natural rock cliff** at an altitude of approximately **$1,230\text{ feet}$** above sea level.
- **Spiritual Dedication**: Dedicated primarily to **Lord Shiva** (housing a natural rock-cut Shivling) and revered by pilgrims who apply *sendur* (vermilion/sindoor) during sacred festivals such as Maha Shivratri.
- **Cultural & Tourist Significance**: Merges natural cave formation with ancient rock-cut craftsmanship, attracting devotees and trekkers exploring the Satpura range and Vindhyan hill systems.

### 2. Major Rock-Cut Caves & Temple Complexes of Madhya Pradesh

| Cave / Rock-Cut Site | Location (District) | Period / Dynasty | Distinctive Cultural & Architectural Highlights |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Udayagiri Caves** | **Vidisha, MP** | **Gupta Period** (Chandragupta II era) | • **20 rock-cut caves** (19 Hindu, 1 Jain).<br>• Famous for the colossal **Varaha (Boar Incarnation) relief** in Cave 5. |
| **Bagh Caves** | **Dhar, MP** | **Gupta / Post-Gupta Period** (5th–6th c. CE) | • Group of **9 Buddhist rock-cut caves** on the Baghani River.<br>• Renowned for exquisite mural paintings resembling Ajanta frescoes. |
| **Bhimbetka Rock Shelters** | **Raisen, MP** | **Paleolithic to Mesolithic Age** | • UNESCO World Heritage Site (discovered by **Dr. V. S. Wakankar** in 1957).<br>• Oldest rock art and cave dwellings in the Indian subcontinent. |
| **Dhamnar Caves** | **Mandsaur, MP** | **5th–7th Century CE** | • Features **51 rock-cut Buddhist caves** and the monolithic rock-cut **Dharmarajeshwar Temple** (resembling Ellora's Kailash Temple). |
| **Gwalior Fort Rock-Cut Sculptures** | **Gwalior, MP** | **Tomar Dynasty** (15th Century CE) | • Massive monolithic rock-carved statues of Jain Tirthankaras (including a 57-foot figure of Rishabhanatha). |
