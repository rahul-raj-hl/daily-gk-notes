# 📌 Bagurumba Dance: Bodo Community, Attire & Cultural Matrix of Assam

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **State & Community**: **Bagurumba** is the signature traditional folk dance of the indigenous **Bodo community** of **Assam**.
- **Popular Epithet**: Colloquially known as the **"Butterfly Dance"** because the slow, rhythmic steps and graceful arm movements—with dancers holding the ends of their colourful scarves outstretched—vividly mimic the gentle fluttering of butterflies in nature.
- **Festival Association**: Traditionally performed by **Bodo women** during the **Bwisagu festival** (celebrated in mid-April to mark the Bodo New Year, coinciding with *Bohag Bihu / Rongali Bihu* in Assam).
- **Traditional Costumes**:
  - **Dokhona**: Vibrant, wrap-around traditional dress worn from the chest down to the ankles.
  - **Jwmgra / Fasra**: Traditional scarf/shawl draped over the upper body.
  - **Aronai**: Sacred woven cloth/muffler of pride and honour.
- **Traditional Musical Instruments**:
  - **Sifung**: A long bamboo flute with five finger holes.
  - **Kham**: A large wooden drum covered with goat skin on both sides.
  - **Serja**: A traditional bowed violin-like instrument.
  - **Gongona**: A vibrating reed / jaw harp made of cured bamboo.
  - **Tharkha**: A split-bamboo clapper providing rhythmic timing.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Prominent Folk & Tribal Dances of Assam

| Dance Form | Community / Tribe | Occasion / Theme | Unique Feature & Instruments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bagurumba** | **Bodo Tribe** | Celebrated during **Bwisagu** (Bodo Spring/New Year festival). | Known as the **"Butterfly Dance"**; features *Sifung, Kham, Serja, Gongona*. |
| **Bihu Dance** | Assamese society (All communities) | Performed during **Rongali (Bohag) Bihu** in spring to usher in the agricultural cycle. | Characterized by rapid hand movements and brisk hip swaying; uses *Dhol, Pepa (buffalo horn pipe), Taal, Gagana*. |
| **Jhumur Dance** | **Tea Tribe community** (*Adivasi* tea-garden workers) | Performed during the autumn festival and post-harvest period. | Performed in linked chains with synchronized rhythmic footwork; accompanied by the *Madal*. |
| **Bhur Tal Dance** | Monastic / Folk tradition (Barpeta) | Originated in the Vaishnavite Satras of Assam; developed by Narahari Burha Bhakat. | Performers clash large brass cymbals (**Bhur Tal**) with energetic acrobatic formations. |
| **Deodhani Dance** | Shakti cult devotees | Dedicated to the serpent goddess **Manasa**. | A shamanistic, trance-like solo or group dance performed with sword and shield (*Daa*). |
| **Ali-Ai-Ligang (Gumrag)** | **Mishing (Miri) Tribe** | Sowing festival celebrated in the month of *Ginmur Kolo* (February). | Mimics agricultural actions like seed sowing and harvesting to traditional drum rhythms. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Dance Form | State of Origin | Distinctive Identity / Prop | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bagurumba** | **Assam** | Bodo women mimicking butterflies with *Dokhona* | Confusing Bagurumba with dances of Odisha or West Bengal |
| **Cheraw** | **Mizoram** | Rhythmic hopping over crossed **bamboo sticks** | Marking Nagaland or Assam for Bamboo dance |
| **Hojagiri** | **Tripura** | Balancing on an **earthen pitcher (*Kalas*)** | Thinking Hojagiri belongs to Assam or Manipur |
| **Jhumur vs. Jhumar** | • **Jhumur**: Assam / Bengal / Jharkhand (Tea tribes)<br>• **Jhumar**: Punjab / Haryana (Harvest folk dance) | Confusing north-eastern tribal Jhumur with Punjabi Jhumar |
