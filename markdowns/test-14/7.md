# 📌 Automatic Stabilisers & Proportional Income Tax in Macroeconomics

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Proportional income tax**.
- **What is an Automatic Stabiliser?**: An economic feature/mechanism (fiscal policy instrument) that automatically cushions and dampens fluctuations in real GDP without requiring explicit, discretionary intervention by lawmakers or governments.
- **How Proportional Income Tax Acts as a Stabiliser**:
  - **During Economic Boom / Inflation**: Aggregate national income rises $\rightarrow$ tax collections rise automatically $\rightarrow$ disposable income and consumer demand are dampened $\rightarrow$ prevents economic overheating.
  - **During Recession / Economic Downturn**: Aggregate national income drops $\rightarrow$ total tax collection automatically falls $\rightarrow$ disposable income falls by less than total output $\rightarrow$ consumer spending is supported, cushioning the fall in GDP.
- **Other Classic Automatic Stabilisers**: Progressive income tax, unemployment benefits, welfare transfer payments, and food stamps.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Automatic Stabilisers vs. Discretionary Fiscal Policy

- **Automatic Stabilisers**:
  - Built-in fiscal shock absorbers that adjust automatically with the phases of the business cycle.
  - **Key Advantage**: Zero implementation lag (operates instantaneously without waiting for parliamentary approval or new tax laws).
- **Discretionary Fiscal Policy**:
  - Deliberate policy actions taken by the government (e.g., passing a new infrastructure stimulus package or altering statutory tax rates).

---

### 2. Analysis of Other Taxes in the Options

- **Professional Tax**:
  - A direct tax levied by **State Governments** (under **Article 276** of the Indian Constitution) on individuals earning income from professions, trades, callings, or employment.
  - Capped at a constitutional maximum ceiling of **₹2,500 per annum** per individual; fixed in nature and does not fluctuate automatically with macro business cycles.
- **Wealth Tax**:
  - A direct tax levied on the net wealth/accumulated assets of individuals.
  - *Note*: Abolished in India in the 2015–16 Union Budget and replaced with an additional surcharge on high-net-worth individuals.
- **Capital Gains Tax**:
  - A tax levied on the profit/gain realized from the transfer or sale of capital assets (e.g., real estate, stocks, mutual funds).
  - Classified into Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Taxation System | Tax Rate as Income Increases | Primary Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proportional Tax (Flat Tax)** | **Remains Constant** ($t$) across all income levels | Proportional Income Tax, Flat Corporate Rate |
| **Progressive Tax** | **Increases** as taxable income increases | Personal Income Tax Slabs in India |
| **Regressive Tax** | **Decreases as a proportion of total income** | Indirect taxes (like GST on basic essentials) |
| **Degressive Tax** | Rate increases initially, then becomes flat | Modified progressive system |

### Q1

How does the introduction of a proportional income tax rate ($t$) affect the simple Keynesian investment multiplier ($k$)?

- [ ] It increases the multiplier
- [x] It reduces the value of the multiplier
- [ ] It keeps the multiplier unchanged
- [ ] It makes the multiplier infinite

> With taxes included, the multiplier formula becomes $\frac{1}{1 - c(1 - t)}$, which reduces the multiplier's magnitude and helps dampen shocks to aggregate output.
