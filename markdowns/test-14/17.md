# 📌 Apsara & India's Nuclear Research Reactors

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Apsara**.
- **First Nuclear Reactor**: **Apsara** was the first nuclear research reactor built in India as well as in all of **Asia**.
- **Criticality Date**: Achieved criticality on **4 August 1956** at the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) campus in **Trombay, Mumbai**.
- **Design & Type**: A highly versatile **swimming pool-type** reactor designed indigenously under the leadership of **Dr. Homi J. Bhabha** (with enriched uranium fuel supplied by the UK).
- **Naming**: Formally dedicated to the nation and named **"Apsara"** by Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru** in January 1957.
- **Upgraded Version**: Shut down in 2009 for an overhaul and recommissioned as **Apsara-Upgraded (Apsara-U)** in September 2018 using indigenous low-enriched uranium (LEU) fuel.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Key Indian Nuclear Research Reactors

*Distinct features and roles of key research reactors at BARC (Mumbai) and IGCAR (Kalpakkam):*

- **1. Apsara (1956)**:
  - **Type**: 1 MW thermal swimming pool reactor.
  - **Significance**: Asia’s first nuclear reactor; used for basic research in neutron physics, material testing, and production of radioisotopes for medicine and agriculture.
- **2. CIRUS (1960)**:
  - **Full Form**: *Canada-India Reactor Utility Services* (or *Canada-India Reactor, U.S.*).
  - **Type**: 40 MW heavy water-moderated research reactor at Trombay.
  - **Significance**: Supplied by Canada with heavy water provided by the United States; supplied plutonium used in India's 1974 *Smiling Buddha* peaceful nuclear test; decommissioned in December 2010.
- **3. Dhruva (1985)**:
  - **Type**: 100 MW high-flux thermal research reactor at BARC, Trombay.
  - **Significance**: India's **largest nuclear research reactor**; primary domestic source for medical/industrial radioisotopes and weapons-grade plutonium.
- **4. KAMINI (1996)**:
  - **Full Form**: *Kalpakkam Mini Reactor*.
  - **Location**: Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR), **Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu**.
  - **Significance**: The world's only operating experimental reactor fueled by **Thorium-derived Uranium-233 ($^{233}\text{U}$)**.

---

### 2. India's Three-Stage Nuclear Power Program

*Formulated by Dr. Homi J. Bhabha to utilize India's vast domestic Thorium reserves:*

1. **Stage I – Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)**:
   - **Fuel**: Natural Uranium ($^{238}\text{U}$).
   - **Byproduct**: Plutonium-239 ($^{239}\text{Pu}$).
2. **Stage II – Fast Breeder Reactors (FBRs)**:
   - **Fuel**: Plutonium-239 ($^{239}\text{Pu}$) + Uranium/Thorium blanket.
   - **Core Facility**: Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR - 500 MWe) at Kalpakkam.
   - **Outcome**: Breeds fissile Uranium-233 ($^{233}\text{U}$) from Thorium-232 ($^{232}\text{Th}$).
3. **Stage III – Advanced Thorium-Based Reactors (AHWRs)**:
   - **Fuel**: Thorium-232 ($^{232}\text{Th}$) + Uranium-233 ($^{233}\text{U}$).
   - **Goal**: Long-term energy self-sufficiency using India's vast coastal monazite sand deposits.
