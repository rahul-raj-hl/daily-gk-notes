# 📌 Ghoomar Dance & Traditional Folk Dances of Rajasthan

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Saraswati**.
- **The Dance Form**: **Ghoomar** (also called *Ghumur* or *Jhoomar*) is the quintessential traditional folk dance of **Rajasthan**.
- **Deity Worshipped**: Originally performed as a devotional ritual to invoke the blessings of **Goddess Saraswati** (the deity of wisdom, arts, and learning).
- **Tribal Roots & Royal Adoption**: Developed by the **Bhil tribe**; later adopted and patronized by **Rajput royal households**, where women performed it during auspicious occasions like welcoming newlywed brides.
- **Key Characteristics**: Characterized by synchronized circular pirouettes (*Ghoomna*), rhythmic clapping, and the graceful swirling of long flared skirts (*Ghaghra*).
- **Major Occasions**: Performed during festive occasions such as **Gangaur, Teej, Navratri, weddings, and harvest celebrations**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Unique Cultural Features of Ghoomar

- **Etymology**: The word *Ghoomar* is derived from the Hindi word **"Ghoomna"**, referring to the graceful twirling/spinning movement of the dancers.
- **Attire**: Dancers wear vibrant, heavily embroidered **Ghaghra-Choli** and translucent veils (*Odhni / Ghoonghat*). As the dancers spin rapidly, the broad circumference of the skirt flares outward into a colorful circular display.
- **Musical Instruments**: Accompanied by traditional instruments such as the **Dholak, Nagada, Shehnai, Sarangi, and Morchang**.

---

### 2. Prominent Folk Dances of Rajasthan

*Key traditional dance forms of Rajasthan often tested in competitive exams:*

- **1. Kalbelia**:
  - Performed by women of the nomadic **Kalbelia (snake charmer) community**.
  - Dancers wear flowing black skirts and mimic the graceful, fluid movements of a serpent.
  - Inscribed on **UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2010)**.
  - Iconic maestro: **Gulabo Sapera**.
- **2. Chari Dance**:
  - Popular among the **Gurjar and Saini communities** of Kishangarh/Ajmer.
  - Women balance brass pots (*Chari*) topped with lit cotton seeds soaked in oil on their heads while dancing.
- **3. Bhavai Dance**:
  - Highly acrobatic balancing dance where women balance **7 to 9 brass/earthen pots** on their heads while dancing on the edge of swords, glass shards, or brass thalis.
- **4. Gair Dance**:
  - Traditional martial folk dance performed primarily by **Bhil and Meena men** using wooden sticks during Holi and Janmashtami.
- **5. Kachhi Ghodi**:
  - A lively mock-horse martial dance performed by male dancers riding dummy horses, popular in the **Shekhawati region**.
- **6. Terah Taali**:
  - Devotional dance performed by the **Kamar tribe** in honor of folk deity **Baba Ramdevji**, where women tie 13 brass cymbals (*Manjiras*) across their bodies and strike them in rhythm.
