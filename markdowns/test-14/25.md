# 📌 South Indian Dynasties & The Post-Kalabhra Era (6th Century AD)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Kalabhra Interregnum**: A transitional era (circa 3rd to 6th century AD) where the **Kalabhras** overthrew and suppressed the traditional Sangam-era powers (Cholas, Cheras, and Pandyas).
- **Major Dynastic Revivals (Late 6th Century AD)**:
  - **Pallavas**: Rose to prominence under **Simhavishnu** (who overthrew the Kalabhras in the north) and his son **Mahendravarman I**, expanding northern borders up to the **Krishna River**.
  - **Pandyas**: Revived under **King Kadungon** (late 6th century AD), who defeated the Kalabhras in the southern deep South (Madurai & Vaigai basin).
  - **Chalukyas of Badami**: Consolidated sovereign Deccan power under **Pulakesin I** (c. 543 AD) and reached their zenith under **Pulakesin II**.
  - **Cholas**: Ancient Sangam-era lineage; overshadowed during this phase until **Vijayalaya Chola** revived Imperial Chola rule in 850 AD.
- **Architectural Milestone**: Pallavas pioneered **Dravidian rock-cut architecture** (e.g., Mahabalipuram/Mamallapuram), later culminating in massive structural temples under the Cholas (e.g., Brihadeshwara Temple).

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Four Major Ruling Powers of Early Medieval South India

*Comparative overview of territorial base, key rulers, and historical contributions:*

#### A. Pallavas of Kanchipuram (Tondaimandalam)
- **Simhavishnu (Avanisimha)**: Crushed the Kalabhras and firmly established the Pallava imperial line.
- **Mahendravarman I (600–630 AD)**:
  - Polymath ruler (*Mattavilasa*, *Gunabhara*, *Vichitrachitta*); author of the Sanskrit farce ***Mattavilasa Prahasana***.
  - Extended Pallava boundaries northwards to the **Krishna River**.
  - Initiated rock-cut cave architecture in Tamil country (Mandagapattu, Mahabalipuram).
  - Converted from Jainism to Shaivism by the Nayanar saint **Appar (Tirunavukkarasar)**.
- **Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla)**: Defeated Pulakesin II at the Battle of Manimangalam (642 AD), assumed the title ***Vatapikonda*** (Conqueror of Vatapi), and constructed the monolithic Rathas (Seven Pagodas) at Mahabalipuram.

#### B. Pandyas of Madurai
- **Capital & Heartland**: Centered on **Madurai** and the **Vaigai River basin**.
- **King Kadungon (c. 560–590 AD)**: Credited in the *Velvikkudi Copper Plates* with defeating and ending the oppressive rule of the Kalabhras, establishing the First Pandyan Empire.
- **Economic Prominence**: Ancient trade in pearl fisheries (Korkai port) and documented maritime commerce with the Roman and Hellenistic empires.

#### C. Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi)
- **Pulakesin I (543 AD)**: Established the sovereign dynasty, built the hill fortress of **Vatapi (Badami, Karnataka)**, and performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice.
- **Pulakesin II (610–642 AD)**:
  - Greatest Chalukya emperor; his military achievements are recorded in the famous **Aihole Inscription** composed by court poet **Ravikirti**.
  - Defeated the northern emperor **Harshavardhana** on the banks of the **Narmada River**.
- **Architecture**: Blended North and South styles to develop the **Vesara style** of architecture (Badami cave temples, Aihole, Pattadakal).

#### D. Cholas of Uraiyur & Thanjavur
- **Sangam Roots**: Mentioned in Ashoka's Major Rock Edicts (Edict II & XIII) as independent southern neighbours alongside Pandyas, Keralaputras, and Satyaputras.
- **Imperial Revival (850 AD)**: Revived by **Vijayalaya Chola** (captured Thanjavur from the Muttaraiyars).
- **Golden Era**: Under **Raja Raja Chola I** (built the Great Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur) and **Rajendra Chola I** (Gangaikonda Cholapuram, naval expeditions to Srivijaya/Southeast Asia).

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Statement / Misconception | Historical Reality | Critical Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"King Kadungon revived the Chola dynasty."* | **Incorrect** | Kadungon revived the **Pandya dynasty** (c. 6th century AD). The Cholas were revived by **Vijayalaya** in 850 AD. |
| *"Cholas began in the 6th century AD."* | **Incorrect** | Cholas date back to the **Sangam Period (3rd century BC)**; mentioned in Ashokan Edicts. |
| *"Pulakesin I defeated Harshavardhana."* | **Incorrect** | Harsha was defeated by **Pulakesin II** on the Narmada River. |
| *"Rock-cut temples at Mahabalipuram were built by Cholas."* | **Incorrect** | Built by the **Pallavas** (Mahendravarman I & Narasimhavarman I). |
