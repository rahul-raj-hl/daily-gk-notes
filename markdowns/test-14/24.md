# 📌 Article 270: Center-State Tax Devolution & GST Framework

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Mandate of Article 270**: Outlines the constitutional mechanism for the sharing and distribution of taxes levied and collected by the Union between the Union and the States (forming the **Divisible Pool of Central Taxes**).
- **101st Constitutional Amendment Act (2016)**: Amended **Article 270** to formally incorporate revenues from the **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** into the divisible pool.
- **Exclusion of Compensation Cess**: The **GST Compensation Cess** is **NOT** shared under Article 270. It is governed separately under the statutory provisions of the **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017** solely to compensate states for transition-period revenue shortfalls.
- **Recommendation Authority**: The vertical and horizontal distribution formulas are recommended by the **Finance Commission of India (Article 280)**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Structure of Article 270 (Taxes Levied and Distributed Between Union & States)

*The constitutional mechanism of the divisible tax pool:*

- **The Divisible Pool**: Consists of all central taxes and duties (e.g., **Corporation Tax, Personal Income Tax, Central GST (CGST), Union Excise Duties, Customs Duties**).
- **Exclusions from the Divisible Pool**:
  1. Duties and taxes mentioned in **Articles 268, 269, and 269A**.
  2. Surcharges on taxes and duties levied for Union purposes under **Article 271**.
  3. Any **Cess** levied for specific purposes under any law made by Parliament (including GST Compensation Cess).
- **Role of the Finance Commission**: Recommends the percentage share allocated to States (Vertical Devolution) and the interstate distribution criteria (Horizontal Devolution, based on population, area, income distance, and forest cover).

---

### 2. Impact of the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016

*Key changes to the constitutional taxation structure:*

- **Introduction of GST (July 1, 2017)**: Subsumed multiple indirect taxes (Central Excise, Service Tax, State VAT, Entry Tax, Octroi, Luxury Tax) into a unified tax regime.
- **Amendment to Article 270**: Added specific clauses ensuring that the Central GST (CGST) and the Union's share of Integrated GST (IGST) under Article 269A are brought under the tax-sharing mechanism with the States.
- **GST Council (Article 279A)**: Established as a constitutional joint forum comprising the Union Finance Minister and State Finance Ministers to decide GST tax slabs, exemptions, and rules.

---

### 3. GST Compensation Cess vs. Article 270 Tax Sharing

*Comparing the regular divisible pool and the transition compensation mechanism:*

| Parameter | Taxes Shared Under Article 270 | GST Compensation Cess |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional Basis** | **Article 270** of the Indian Constitution | Statutory: **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017** |
| **Component Taxes** | Income Tax, CGST, Customs, Excise Duty | Specific cess levied on demerit/sin and luxury goods |
| **Sharing Mechanism** | Devolution to all states via **Finance Commission formula** | Distributed **only to states suffering revenue shortfalls** |
| **Part of Divisible Pool?** | **Yes** (Shared permanently) | **No** (Earmarked for debt/compensation repayment) |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Constitutional Term | Meaning | Shared with States under Article 270? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tax** | General compulsory contribution to state revenue | **Yes** (Corporation tax, Income tax, GST) |
| **Surcharge (Art 271)** | "Tax on tax" for general expenditure of the Union | **No** (Goes exclusively to the Union) |
| **Cess** | Dedicated levy for a specific purpose (e.g., Health & Edu Cess, Compensation Cess) | **No** (Credited to dedicated funds; not in divisible pool) |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "TAXES GO TO 270, CESS AND SURCHARGE STAY WITH THE CENTRE"**
> - **270** $\rightarrow$ Distributes **Taxes + GST** between Union and States
> - **Cess & Surcharge** $\rightarrow$ Kept out of the divisible pool
