# 📌 Coffee Cultivation in India & Plantation Agriculture

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Karnataka**.
- **India's Top Coffee Producer**: **Karnataka** is by far the largest producer of coffee in India, contributing over **$70\%$** of total domestic production.
- **Top 3 Producing States**:
  1. **Karnataka** ($\sim 70\%$)
  2. **Kerala** ($\sim 20\%$)
  3. **Tamil Nadu** ($\sim 5\text{--}8\%$)
- **Core Cultivation Belt**: Grown in the Western Ghats region comprising **Kodagu (Coorg), Chikkamagaluru, and Hassan** districts in Karnataka.
- **Historic Origin**: First brought to India in the 17th century by the Sufi saint **Baba Budan**, who smuggled seven raw coffee beans from Mocha (Yemen) and planted them in the **Baba Budan Hills** of Chikkamagaluru.
- **Headquarters of the Coffee Board of India**: **Bengaluru, Karnataka** (Statutory body under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Geographical Requirements for Coffee Cultivation

*Optimal climatic and environmental conditions:*

- **Climate**: Hot and humid tropical/subtropical climate.
- **Temperature**: $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $28^\circ\text{C}$ (Frost and direct intense sunlight are harmful; grown under two-tier shade trees).
- **Rainfall**: $150\text{ to }250\text{ cm}$ per year.
- **Soil**: Well-drained, rich, friable, loamy soil containing high humus and iron content (sloping hill terrain prevents water stagnation).
- **Pre-Monsoon Showers**: *"Blossom Showers"* or *"Cherry Blossom showers"* in March–April are critical for flowering.

---

### 2. Major Coffee Varieties Grown in India

*Comparison between the two major cultivated species:*

- **1. Coffea Arabica (Arabica)**:
  - Higher market value, milder aromatic flavor, lower caffeine content ($\sim 1.5\%$).
  - Grown at higher elevations ($1000\text{--}1500\text{ m}$).
  - India's Arabica is globally celebrated for its shade-grown quality (e.g., *Coorg Arabica*, *Chikmagalur Arabica*, *Araku Valley Arabica*).
- **2. Coffea Canephora (Robusta)**:
  - Stronger, bolder taste, higher caffeine content ($\sim 2.7\%$), higher yield, and higher resistance to pests (like leaf rust).
  - Grown at lower altitudes ($500\text{--}1000\text{ m}$).
  - Dominates Kerala's production (e.g., *Wayanad Robusta*).

---

### 3. Analysis of States in the Options

- **Karnataka**: Dominates Indian coffee ($>70\%$); major regions are Coorg, Chikkamagaluru, and Hassan.
- **Kerala**: Second largest producer ($\approx 20\%$); primary hub is **Wayanad**, followed by Idukki and Palakkad.
- **Tamil Nadu**: Third largest producer ($\approx 5\text{--}8\%$); grown in the **Nilgiris, Yercaud (Shevaroy Hills), and Kodaikanal (Palani Hills)**.
- **Assam**: The undisputed leader in **Tea cultivation** in India ($>50\%$ of India's tea), along with West Bengal (Darjeeling/Dooars).
