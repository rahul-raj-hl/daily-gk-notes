# 📌 Corrosion of Metals - Tarnishing of Silver & Chemical Principles

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **A is correct and R1 is the correct explanation of A**.
- **Assertion (A) is TRUE**: Silver ($Ag$) articles lose their shine and turn black after being exposed to air for a prolonged period.
- **Reason (R1) is TRUE & EXPLAINS A**: The black coating formed on silver is **Silver Sulfide ($Ag_2S$)**, formed when silver reacts with trace amounts of **Hydrogen Sulfide ($H_2S$)** present in the atmosphere.
- **Reason (R2) is FALSE**: Silver carbonate is not the compound responsible for the characteristic black patina/tarnishing of silver.
- **Chemical Equation for Tarnishing**:
  $$4Ag + 2H_2S + O_2 \longrightarrow 2Ag_2S\text{ (black)} + 2H_2O$$

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Tarnishing of Silver Explained

- **Low Reactivity of Silver**: Silver ($Ag$) is placed near the bottom of the reactivity series, meaning it does not react readily with atmospheric oxygen ($O_2$) or moisture ($H_2O$) under normal conditions.
- **Action of Sulfur Compounds**: Industrial pollutants and natural organic decomposition release trace levels of **Hydrogen Sulfide ($H_2S$)** into the air.
- **Formation of Silver Sulfide ($Ag_2S$)**: Silver reacts with airborne $H_2S$ gas in the presence of oxygen, depositing a thin, insoluble, black-colored layer of **Silver Sulfide** on its surface.

---

### 2. Corrosion in Other Common Metals

*Distinct colors and chemical compounds formed during metal corrosion:*

- **1. Rusting of Iron ($Fe$)**:
  - Requires **both Oxygen and Moisture**.
  - Produces **Hydrated Ferric Oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$)**, which is **reddish-brown** and flaky.
- **2. Corrosion of Copper ($Cu$)**:
  - Copper reacts slowly with moist carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) in the air.
  - Forms a protective **green coating** of **Basic Copper Carbonate** $[CuCO_3 \cdot Cu(OH)_2]$.
- **3. Passivation of Aluminium ($Al$)**:
  - Forms a stable, uniform, and self-protective layer of **Aluminium Oxide ($Al_2O_3$)** that shields the underlying metal from further corrosion (*Anodising* exploits this).

## 🛡️ Methods for Preventing Corrosion

1. **Galvanization**: Coating iron or steel with a thin protective layer of molten **Zinc ($Zn$)**. Zinc acts as a sacrificial anode because it oxidizes preferentially.
2. **Electroplating**: Depositing a thin layer of a non-corroding metal (e.g., Chromium, Nickel, Gold, Silver) using electrolysis.
3. **Alloying**: Mixing iron with elements like Chromium and Nickel to create **Stainless Steel** ($Fe + Cr + Ni + C$), which resists rust completely.
4. **Anodising**: Electrolytically creating a thick, protective oxide layer on aluminium surfaces.
5. **Cathodic Protection**: Connecting the metal structure to a more reactive metal (e.g., Magnesium or Zinc blocks) to prevent oxidation.
