# 📌 Musical Forms in Indian Classical Music - Bandish & Kriti

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Bandish**.
- **Definition of Bandish**: A fixed, pre-composed melodic and rhythmic composition in **Hindustani classical music** set to a specific **Raga** (melody) and **Taal** (rhythmic cycle).
- **Core Structural Parts of a Bandish**:
  1. **Sthayi**: The opening/base theme sung primarily in the lower and middle octaves (*Mandra* and *Madhya Saptak*).
  2. **Antara**: The second section focusing on the upper middle and higher octaves (*Taar Saptak*).
  *(In Dhrupad, it expands into four parts: Sthayi, Antara, Sanchari, and Abhog).*
- **Carnatic Counterpart**: The primary structured composition in **Carnatic music** is called a **Kriti** (or *Kirtana*), composed of **Pallavi, Anupallavi, and Charanam**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Structure & Types of Bandish in Hindustani Music

- **Types by Tempo (*Laya*)**:
  - **Vilambit Bandish / Bada Khayal**: Set to a slow tempo (e.g., *Vilambit Ektaal*, *Tilwada*, *Jhoomra*), allowing slow, meditative melodic improvisation (*Alaap* and *Bol-Alaap*).
  - **Drut Bandish / Chhota Khayal**: Set to a medium-to-fast tempo (e.g., *Teentaal*, *Ektaal*, *Roopak*), characterized by fast melodic embellishments (*Taans*, *Bol-Taans*, and *Sargam*).
- **Languages Used**: Composed predominantly in **Braj Bhasha, Hindi, Awadhi, Urdu, Maithili, or Sanskrit**.
- **Gharana Legacy**: Bandishes serve as the core inherited repertoire passed down through the Guru-Shishya parampara across gharanas (such as Gwalior, Agra, Kirana, Jaipur-Atrauli, and Patiala).

---

### 2. Analysis of Other Musical Terms in the Options

- **Kriti (Carnatic Music)**:
  - The most important structural musical format in South Indian Carnatic classical music.
  - Comprises three distinct sections:
    1. **Pallavi**: The thematic opening line/refrain.
    2. **Anupallavi**: The second section exploring higher notes.
    3. **Charanam**: The concluding stanza combining melodies from both previous parts.
  - Perfected by the **Trinity of Carnatic Music**: **Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri**.
- **Gamaka (Grace / Ornamentation)**:
  - Any graceful melodic ornamentation, glide, shake, oscillation, or pitch deflection applied to a musical note (*Svara*) to bring out the raga's expressive character.
- **Rasa (Aesthetic Emotion)**:
  - The emotional essence or sentiment evoked in the listener by an artistic performance (defined in Bharata Muni's *Natya Shastra* through the **Navarasas** like *Shringara*, *Bhakti*, *Veera*, and *Shanta*).
