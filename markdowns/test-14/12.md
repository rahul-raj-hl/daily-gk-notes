# 📌 Fundamental Rights vs. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **FRs are enforceable; DPSPs are not**.
- **Justiciability & Enforceability**:
  - **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)**: **Justiciable and legally enforceable** by the Supreme Court (Article 32) and High Courts (Article 226).
  - **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51)**: **Non-justiciable and unenforceable** by any court (explicitly stated under **Article 37**).
- **Core Distinction of Democracy**:
  - Fundamental Rights establish **Political Democracy** and place negative limitations on State power.
  - DPSPs establish **Social and Economic Democracy** (a Welfare State) and impose positive obligations on the State.
- **Harmonious Construction**: In the landmark ***Minerva Mills case (1980)***, the Supreme Court ruled that the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of balance and harmony between Part III and Part IV.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comprehensive Comparison: Fundamental Rights vs. DPSPs

*Key differences across constitutional characteristics:*

| Parameter | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional Articles** | **Articles 12 to 35** | **Articles 36 to 51** |
| **Source / Borrowed From**| **Constitution of the USA (Bill of Rights)** | **Irish Constitution (1937)** |
| **Nature of Obligation** | Mostly **Negative** (prohibits the State from taking certain actions) | **Positive** (requires the State to implement welfare measures) |
| **Enforceability / Courts**| **Justiciable** (Remedy guaranteed under Art 32 & 226) | **Non-justiciable** (Article 37 declares them unenforceable in courts) |
| **Type of Democracy** | Establishes **Political Democracy** | Establishes **Socio-Economic Democracy** (Welfare State) |
| **Individual vs. Community**| Promotes **Individual Liberty & Rights** | Promotes **Community Welfare & Common Good** |
| **Legal Sanction** | Backed by **Legal Sanctions & Remedies** | Backed by **Moral, Political & Public Opinion Sanctions** |
| **Suspension** | Can be suspended during National Emergency (except Arts 20 & 21) | Cannot be suspended (they are permanent guiding principles) |

---

### 2. Evolution of Conflict: Part III vs. Part IV

*Landmark Supreme Court rulings establishing the constitutional relationship:*

1. **State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)**:
   - Held that Fundamental Rights are sacrosanct and prevail over DPSPs in case of conflict; DPSPs must run as subsidiary to Part III.
2. **Golaknath Case (1967)**:
   - Parliament cannot take away or abridge any of the Fundamental Rights to implement DPSPs.
3. **25th Constitutional Amendment Act (1971)**:
   - Inserted **Article 31C**, providing that laws giving effect to **Article 39(b)** (distribution of material resources) and **Article 39(c)** (prevention of wealth concentration) cannot be declared void on grounds of violating Articles 14 or 19.
4. **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**:
   - Upheld the validity of the first part of Article 31C and established the **Basic Structure Doctrine**.
5. **Minerva Mills Case (1980)**:
   - Struck down the extension of Article 31C to all DPSPs.
   - Held that giving absolute primacy to one over the other destroys the harmony of the Constitution; Part III and Part IV are like two wheels of a chariot.

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "FRs FIGHT IN COURT, DPSPs DIRECT THE STATE"**
> - **FRs** $\rightarrow$ Enforceable by **Court Writs (Art 32 / 226)**
> - **DPSPs** $\rightarrow$ Guiding **Directions** for policy makers (Art 37)

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

Which of the following is correct regarding Fundamental Rights and DPSPs?

- [ ] Both are enforceable in courts
- [ ] DPSPs prevail over Fundamental Rights
- [x] FRs are enforceable; DPSPs are not
- [ ] DPSPs are binding while FRs are optional

> Fundamental Rights can be enforced through writ petitions in the Supreme Court and High Courts, whereas Directive Principles are non-enforceable policy guidelines.

---

### PYQ 2

Which Article of the Constitution of India explicitly declares that Directive Principles of State Policy are non-justiciable in nature?

- [ ] Article 36
- [x] Article 37
- [ ] Article 38
- [ ] Article 39

> Article 37 states that the provisions contained in Part IV shall not be enforceable by any court, but are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country.

---

### PYQ 3

The Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution were adopted from the constitution of which country?

- [ ] United States of America
- [ ] United Kingdom
- [x] Ireland
- [ ] Canada

> The framers of the Indian Constitution borrowed the concept of Directive Principles of State Policy from the Irish Constitution of 1937 (which had borrowed it from Spain).
