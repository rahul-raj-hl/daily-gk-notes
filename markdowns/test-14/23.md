# 📌 Capillary Action: Surface Tension & Fluid Mechanics

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Capillary action** (Capillarity).
- **Definition**: The spontaneous **rise or fall (depression)** of a liquid inside a narrow-bore tube (capillary tube) when dipped into the fluid.
- **Physical Cause**: Driven by the combined effect of **Surface Tension** and the relative competition between **Adhesive forces** (liquid-to-solid attraction) and **Cohesive forces** (liquid-to-liquid attraction).
- **Capillary Rise (Ascent)**: Occurs when **Adhesion > Cohesion** (the liquid "wets" the solid, e.g., water in a glass tube), forming a **concave meniscus** and acute contact angle ($\theta < 90^\circ$).
- **Capillary Depression (Descent/Fall)**: Occurs when **Cohesion > Adhesion** (the liquid does not wet the solid, e.g., mercury in a glass tube), forming a **convex meniscus** and obtuse contact angle ($\theta > 90^\circ$).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Intermolecular Forces Behind Capillarity

*Key forces dictating fluid movement in narrow channels:*

- **Cohesive Forces**: Attractive intermolecular forces operating between molecules of the **same substance** (e.g., water-water molecular attraction).
- **Adhesive Forces**: Attractive intermolecular forces operating between molecules of **different substances** (e.g., water molecules attracted to silica molecules in glass).

---

### 2. Capillary Rise vs. Capillary Fall

*Behavioral comparison of wetting and non-wetting fluids:*

| Characteristic | Capillary Rise (e.g., Water in Glass) | Capillary Fall / Depression (e.g., Mercury in Glass) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Force Dominance** | **Adhesion $>$ Cohesion** | **Cohesion $>$ Adhesion** |
| **Surface Wetting** | Liquid **wets** the solid wall | Liquid **does not wet** the solid wall |
| **Meniscus Shape** | **Concave** (curved upward at edges) | **Convex** (curved downward at edges) |
| **Contact Angle ($\theta$)**| **Acute** ($\theta < 90^\circ$, $\approx 0^\circ$ for pure water) | **Obtuse** ($\theta > 90^\circ$, $\approx 138^\circ$ for mercury) |
| **Pressure Difference** | Pressure above meniscus $>$ Pressure below | Pressure below meniscus $>$ Pressure above |
| **Movement in Tube** | Liquid **rises** above container level | Liquid **falls / depresses** below container level |

---

### 3. Jurin's Law (Height of Liquid Column)

The equilibrium height $h$ to which a liquid rises or falls in a vertical capillary tube of radius $r$ is governed by **Jurin's Law**:

$$h = \frac{2 T \cos\theta}{\rho g r}$$

Where:
- $T$ = Surface tension of the liquid
- $\theta$ = Angle of contact between liquid and tube wall
- $\rho$ = Density of the liquid
- $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity
- $r$ = Internal radius of the capillary tube

> **Key Rule**: The height of the liquid column is **inversely proportional** to the radius of the tube ($h \propto \frac{1}{r}$). A narrower tube results in a higher rise (or deeper fall).

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Physical Property | Definition | SI Unit | Real-World Manifestation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capillarity** | Spontaneous rise/fall in a narrow tube | Meter ($\text{m}$) | Oil rising in lamp wick |
| **Surface Tension** | Elastic force per unit length on a liquid surface | $\text{N/m}$ or $\text{J/m}^2$ | Spherical shape of raindrops, floating needle |
| **Viscosity** | Internal frictional resistance to flow in a fluid | $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{Poise}$ | Honey flows slower than water |
| **Density** | Mass per unit volume of a substance | $\text{kg/m}^3$ | Ice floating on water |

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

According to Jurin's Law, if the radius of a capillary tube is doubled, the height to which water rises in it will:

- [ ] Double
- [ ] Quadruple
- [x] Reduce to half
- [ ] Remain unchanged

> The height of capillary rise is inversely proportional to the tube's radius ($h \propto \frac{1}{r}$); doubling the radius reduces the height by half.

### Q2

The contact angle ($\theta$) between pure water and a clean glass surface is:

- [x] Acute ($\approx 0^\circ$)
- [ ] Obtuse ($> 90^\circ$)
- [ ] Exactly $90^\circ$
- [ ] $180^\circ$

> For liquids that completely wet the solid surface (like pure water on clean glass), the angle of contact is acute ($\approx 0^\circ$).

### Q3

Why do farmers in dry regions break up the top layer of soil by plowing after light rains?

- [ ] To aerate the soil
- [ ] To allow weeds to decompose
- [x] To break soil capillaries and prevent underground water loss
- [ ] To increase soil alkalinity

> Plowing destroys the fine continuous soil pores (capillaries), preventing sub-soil moisture from evaporating into the air.

Which physical property of liquids is primarily responsible for rain droplets acquiring a perfectly spherical shape?

- [ ] Viscosity
- [x] Surface Tension
