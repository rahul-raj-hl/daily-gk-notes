# 📌 Gas Laws (Boyle's, Charles', Gay-Lussac's & Graham's Law)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Boyle's Law**: At constant temperature ($T$), the volume ($V$) of a gas is inversely proportional to its pressure ($P$).
- **Charles' Law**: At constant pressure ($P$), the volume ($V$) of a gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature ($T$).
- **Gay-Lussac's Law**: At constant volume ($V$), the pressure ($P$) of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature ($T$ in Kelvin).
- **Graham's Law**: The rate of diffusion/effusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its density (or molar mass).
- **Ideal Gas Equation**: Combines basic gas laws into $PV = nRT$.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. The Fundamental Gas Laws

*Behavior of ideal gases under changing temperature, pressure, and volume.*

- **Boyle's Law (Pressure-Volume Relationship)**:
  - Proposed by Robert Boyle.
  - Constant factor: Temperature ($T$) and mass of gas.
  - Formula: $P \propto \frac{1}{V}$ or $PV = \text{Constant}$ ($P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$).
  - Meaning: If pressure increases, volume decreases (e.g., compressing a syringe).

- **Charles' Law (Volume-Temperature Relationship)**:
  - Proposed by Jacques Charles.
  - Constant factor: Pressure ($P$) and mass of gas.
  - Formula: $V \propto T$ or $\frac{V}{T} = \text{Constant}$ ($\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$).
  - Meaning: Heating a gas makes it expand (e.g., hot air balloon rising).

- **Gay-Lussac's Law (Pressure-Temperature Relationship)**:
  - Constant factor: Volume ($V$) and mass of gas.
  - Formula: $P \propto T$ or $\frac{P}{T} = \text{Constant}$ ($\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$).
  - Meaning: Heating gas in a closed rigid container increases its inner pressure (e.g., pressure cooker).

- **Graham's Law of Diffusion**:
  - Deals with gas mixing (diffusion) or passing through a tiny hole (effusion).
  - Formula: $\text{Rate of Diffusion } (r) \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Density } (d)}}$ or $r \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Molar Mass } (M)}}$.
  - Meaning: Lighter gases diffuse much faster than heavier gases (e.g., Hydrogen diffuses faster than Oxygen).

## 🧠 Exam Booster

| Gas Law | Constant Property | Mathematical Relation | Practical Everyday Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boyle's Law** | **Temperature ($T$)** | $P \propto \frac{1}{V}$ ($PV = k$) | Pushing bicycle pump handle / Syringe |
| **Charles' Law** | **Pressure ($P$)** | $V \propto T$ ($\frac{V}{T} = k$) | Expansion of Hot Air Balloon |
| **Gay-Lussac's Law** | **Volume ($V$)** | $P \propto T$ ($\frac{P}{T} = k$) | Working of Pressure Cooker |
| **Avogadro's Law** | **Temp ($T$) & Pressure ($P$)** | $V \propto n$ (moles) | Inflating a football with air |
| **Graham's Law** | **Temp ($T$) & Pressure ($P$)** | $r \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$ | Cooking smell spreading in air |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Law Name | What Remains Constant? | Relation Between Variables |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boyle's Law** | **Temperature** is Constant | Pressure $\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ Volume $\downarrow$ (Inverse) |
| **Charles' Law** | **Pressure** is Constant | Volume $\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ Temperature $\uparrow$ (Direct) |
| **Gay-Lussac's Law** | **Volume** is Constant | Pressure $\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ Temperature $\uparrow$ (Direct) |
