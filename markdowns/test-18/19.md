# 📌 Rock Types & Fossil Preservation: Sedimentary, Igneous & Metamorphic

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Sedimentary rocks**.
- **Why Sedimentary Rocks Contain Fossils**: Formed by the deposition, accumulation, and gradual compaction (**lithification**) of mineral sediments and organic matter over time; the low temperature and pressure environment preserves dead plant, animal, and microorganism remains.
- **Why Igneous Rocks Lack Fossils**: Formed from the cooling and solidification of molten **magma** (subsurface) or **lava** (surface) at extremely high temperatures ($700^\circ\text{C}$–$1300^\circ\text{C}$), which burns and incinerates organic matter.
- **Why Metamorphic Rocks Lack Fossils**: Formed from preexisting rocks subjected to intense **heat, tectonic pressure, and chemical fluids**, which deform, crush, recrystallize, and obliterate any preexisting fossil traces.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Analysis of the Three Major Rock Types

*Key characteristics, formation processes, and fossil preservation potential:*

| Parameter | **Sedimentary Rocks** | **Igneous Rocks** | **Metamorphic Rocks** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin / Mode of Formation** | Weathering, erosion, deposition, and **lithification** of sediments | Cooling and crystallization of molten **magma or lava** | Recrystallization of existing rocks under **extreme heat and pressure** |
| **Fossil Content** | **Rich in fossils** (Primary source of paleontological evidence) | **Extremely rare / Absent** (Molten state incinerates fossils) | **Extremely rare / Destroyed** (Recrystallization crushes fossils) |
| **Layering / Stratification** | **Distinct strata / Bedding planes** (*Stratified rocks*) | **Non-stratified**, crystalline or glassy texture | **Foliated (banded/platy)** or non-foliated structure |
| **Sub-Classifications** | • Mechanically formed<br>• Organically formed<br>• Chemically formed | • **Intrusive (Plutonic)**: Slow cooling underground<br>• **Extrusive (Volcanic)**: Rapid cooling on surface | • Thermal (Contact) metamorphism<br>• Regional (Dynamic) metamorphism |
| **Common Rock Examples** | Sandstone, Limestone, Shale, Conglomerate, Coal, Gypsum | Granite, Basalt, Gabbro, Pumice, Obsidian, Diorite | Marble (from Limestone), Quartzite (from Sandstone), Slate, Gneiss, Schist |

---

### 2. Sub-Types and Examples of Sedimentary Rocks

*Classification based on mode of formation:*

- **Mechanically Formed (Clastic)**:
  - Formed by mechanical weathering, transport, and compaction of mineral fragments.
  - *Examples*: **Sandstone, Shale, Conglomerate, Siltstone, Loess, Breccia**.
- **Organically Formed (Biogenic)**:
  - Formed from the consolidation of animal shells, skeletons, plant detritus, and marine organisms.
  - *Examples*: **Coal, Lignite, Chalk, Peat, Coral Limestone**.
- **Chemically Formed (Evaporites/Precipitates)**:
  - Formed by the precipitation of minerals from mineral-rich water bodies due to evaporation.
  - *Examples*: **Rock Salt (Halite), Gypsum, Potash, Limestone (stalactites/stalagmites)**.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Rock Type / Phenomenon | Actual Characteristic | Common Trap / Misconception |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fossil Preservation** | **Sedimentary Rocks only** | Confusing metamorphic rock layers with sedimentary strata |
| **Coal and Petroleum Deposits** | **Found in Sedimentary basins** | Assuming fossil fuels occur in igneous formations |
| **Extrusive vs. Intrusive Igneous** | • **Extrusive**: Basalt (fine-grained)<br>• **Intrusive**: Granite (coarse-grained) | Confusing Granite (deep cooling) with Basalt (surface lava) |
| **Marble Origin** | Metamorphosed **Limestone (Sedimentary)** | Assuming Marble is an organically formed primary rock |

## 🎯 Memory Trick

> **Mnemonic: "SEDIMENTS SAVE SKELETONS, MAGMA MELTS THEM, METAMORPHIC MASHES THEM"**
> - **Sediments Save Skeletons** $\rightarrow$ Sedimentary rocks preserve fossils
> - **Magma Melts** $\rightarrow$ Igneous heat destroys organic traces
> - **Metamorphic Mashes** $\rightarrow$ Heat & pressure deform fossil records
