# 📌 61st Constitutional Amendment Act: Voting Age Reduction

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **1988** (61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988).
- **Core Provision**: Reduced the minimum voting age for elections to the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) and **State Legislative Assemblies (Vidhan Sabhas)** from **21 years to 18 years**.
- **Amended Constitutional Article**: **Article 326** (Universal Adult Suffrage).
- **Key Timeline**:
  - Passed by Lok Sabha: **15 December 1988**
  - Passed by Rajya Sabha: **20 December 1988**
  - Presidential Assent (President R. Venkataraman): **28 March 1989**
  - Came into force: **28 March 1989**
- **Prime Minister**: Enacted under the premiership of **Rajiv Gandhi**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Constitutional Provisions Governing Elections (Part XV)

*Key articles defining the electoral machinery of India:*

| Article | Subject Matter | Key Constitutional Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Article 324** | **Superintendence, direction, and control of elections** | Vests complete operational authority of conducting elections in the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**. |
| **Article 325** | **Non-discrimination in electoral rolls** | No person is ineligible for inclusion in electoral rolls on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, or sex**; single general electoral roll for every territorial constituency. |
| **Article 326** | **Universal Adult Suffrage** | Elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies are based on adult suffrage; altered by the **61st CAA 1988** (21 $\to$ 18 years). |
| **Article 327** | **Power of Parliament to make laws on elections** | Empowered Parliament to enact the *Representation of the People Acts (RPA 1950 & 1951)*. |
| **Article 328** | **Power of State Legislatures regarding elections** | Power of state legislatures to make provisions for state assembly elections (where Parliament hasn't legislated). |
| **Article 329** | **Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters** | Courts cannot question the delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats. |

---

### 2. Impact on the Election Commission of India (ECI)

- **Shift to a Multi-Member Body**:
  - The reduction of the voting age added millions of young voters to the electoral rolls, drastically increasing the administrative burden on the Election Commission.
  - To handle this surge in work, the President appointed two additional Election Commissioners on **16 October 1989**, briefly making the Commission a **multi-member body** for the first time.
  - Although reversed briefly in 1990, the multi-member setup (1 Chief Election Commissioner + 2 Election Commissioners) was permanently established via the *Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Conditions of Service) Amendment Act, 1993*.

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Question Phrase | Correct Year / Fact | Common Trap / Pitfall |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Year of the 61st Amendment Act** | **1988** | Selecting 1989 (1989 is when it *came into effect/force*) |
| **Article dealing with voting age** | **Article 326** | Confusing with Article 324 (ECI powers) or Article 325 (Electoral rolls) |
| **Minimum Age: Voting vs. Contesting Lok Sabha** | • **Voting**: **18 years**<br>• **Lok Sabha**: **25 years** | Confusing the age required to *vote* with the age to *contest* |
| **Minimum Age: Rajya Sabha / Legislative Council** | **30 years** | Confusing with Lok Sabha / Vidhan Sabha (25 years) |
| **Minimum Age: President / Vice-President / Governor** | **35 years** | Confusing with legislative bodies |
