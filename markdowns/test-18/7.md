# 📌 Sher Shah Suri: The Forerunner of Akbar's Administration

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Sher Shah Suri** (reigned 1540–1545 CE).
- **The Forerunner of Akbar**: Sher Shah Suri is celebrated as the **"Forerunner of Akbar"** (*Akbar ka Poorvagami*) because his innovations in centralized administration, provincial restructuring, land revenue measurement, coinage, and highway infrastructure served as the foundational model that Emperor Akbar and his finance minister Raja Todar Mal later codified into the Mughal system.
- **Dynasty & Rise**: Founder of the **Sur Empire (Second Afghan Empire)** after defeating Mughal Emperor Humayun at the **Battle of Chausa (1539)** and **Battle of Kannauj / Bilgram (1540)**.
- **Enduring Contributions**: Introduction of the silver **Rupiya**, creation of the **Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam)** with roadside *sarais*, and execution of the **Patta** & **Qabuliyat** revenue agreements.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Analysis: Sur Administration vs. Akbar's Governance

*How Sher Shah Suri's administrative machinery directly shaped Mughal institutions:*

| Governance Sphere | Sher Shah Suri's System (1540–1545) | Akbar's Adoption & Refinement (1556–1605) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Provincial & Local Units** | Subdivided the empire into **Sarkars** (districts) and **Parganas** (sub-districts) headed by specialized civil and military officials. | Retained Sarkars and Parganas, and grouped them into larger provincial units called **Subahs** governed by a *Subahdar*. |
| **Land Revenue & Assessment** | • Measured land using the standard **Sikandari Gaz** ($32\text{ digits}$).<br>• Classified land by fertility (Good, Middling, Bad).<br>• Fixed State demand at **$1/3^{\text{rd}}$ of average produce** using a central crop rate schedule (**Rai**).<br>• Issued **Patta** (deed) and obtained **Qabuliyat** (acceptance). | **Raja Todar Mal** (who initially served under Sher Shah) expanded this into the **Ain-i-Dahsala / Zabti System**, retaining the $1/3^{\text{rd}}$ state share and land survey standards. |
| **Monetary System & Coinage** | • Abolished debased and mixed coinage.<br>• Issued standardized tri-metallic coins: pure silver **Rupiya** ($178\text{ grains}$), copper **Dam** ($380\text{ grains}$), and gold **Mohur**. | Adopted Sher Shah's silver Rupiya and copper Dam as the standard currency basis of the entire Mughal Empire. |
| **Military & Army Control** | Revived and strictly enforced Alauddin Khalji's practices of **Dagh** (branding of horses) and **Chehra / Huliya** (descriptive descriptive rolls of soldiers). | Embedded Dagh and Chehra into the core grading and inspection mechanism of the **Mansabdari System**. |
| **Infrastructure & Communications** | • Built the **Sadak-e-Azam** (Grand Trunk Road) connecting Sonargaon (Bengal) to Peshawar/Attock.<br>• Built over **1,700 Sarais** with separate quarters for Hindus and Muslims, equipped with horse relays (*Dak Chowkis*). | Maintained the royal road network and *sarais* for imperial post, espionage networks (*Barids*), and rapid military movement. |

### 2. Central & District Administrative Framework of the Sur Empire

- **District Level (Sarkar)**:
  - **Shiqdar-i-Shiqdaran**: Chief military officer responsible for law, order, and criminal justice across the district.
  - **Munsif-i-Munsifan**: Chief judicial officer responsible for civil disputes and overseeing land revenue collection.
- **Sub-District Level (Pargana)**:
  - **Shiqdar**: Executive head and police chief of the sub-district.
  - **Munsif / Amin**: Officer in charge of land measurement and revenue assessment.
  - **Fotahdar**: Sub-district treasurer.
  - **Karkuns**: Clerks keeping revenue accounts in Persian and local languages.
- **Village Level (Gram)**:
  - Retained traditional village autonomy led by the **Muqaddam** (village headman) and **Patwari** (village accountant/record keeper).
