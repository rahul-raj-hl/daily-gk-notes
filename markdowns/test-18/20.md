# 📌 Mahatma Gandhi in South Africa: The Pietermaritzburg Turning Point

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **Pietermaritzburg** railway station.
- **The Historic Incident**: On **7 June 1893**, while traveling from Durban to Pretoria on a valid first-class ticket, M.K. Gandhi was forcibly evicted from the first-class compartment at **Pietermaritzburg railway station** after a European passenger objected to the presence of a "colored" man.
- **Catalyst for Satyagraha**: This defining incident of racial prejudice (*Apartheid / Asiatic restrictions*) transformed Gandhi from a shy legal counsel into a mass civil rights activist.
- **Major Institutional Milestones in South Africa**:
  - Founded the **Natal Indian Congress (NIC)** in **1894** to unite the Indian community.
  - Established the **Phoenix Settlement / Farm** near Durban in **1904** (inspired by John Ruskin's *Unto This Last*).
  - Founded **Tolstoy Farm** near Johannesburg in **1910** with the support of Hermann Kallenbach.
  - Launched the weekly newspaper ***Indian Opinion*** in **1903**.

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Chronology of Gandhi's South African Struggle (1893–1914)

*Key chronological milestones during Gandhi's 21 years in South Africa:*

| Year | Event / Milestone | Historical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **May 1893** | Arrival in South Africa | Arrived in Durban to represent merchant **Dada Abdullah** in a commercial legal suit. |
| **7 June 1893** | **Pietermaritzburg Incident** | Thrown out of the first-class train compartment; spent the night in the freezing waiting room, resolving to fight racial oppression. |
| **1894** | **Natal Indian Congress (NIC)** | Formed to organize peaceful resistance against the disenfranchisement of Indians in Natal. |
| **1899** | **Indian Ambulance Corps** | Organized Indian volunteers to assist wounded soldiers during the **Second Boer War** (awarded the *Kaiser-i-Hind / Boer War Medal*). |
| **1903** | Launch of ***Indian Opinion*** | Multi-lingual newspaper (English, Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil) to educate and mobilize the Indian diaspora. |
| **1904** | Foundation of **Phoenix Farm** | Established community living focused on manual labor, self-reliance, and non-violent living near Durban. |
| **1906** | **First Satyagraha Campaign** | Launched the first passive resistance against the **Asiatic Law Amendment Ordinance (Black Act)** requiring compulsory registration/fingerprinting in Transvaal. |
| **1908** | First Imprisonment | Jailed at Johannesburg for refusing to carry registration certificates (*Pass Laws*). |
| **1910** | Foundation of **Tolstoy Farm** | Set up near Johannesburg with architect Hermann Kallenbach to house Satyagrahi families during passive resistance. |
| **1913** | **Great March in South Africa** | Led over 2,000 miners and indentured laborers across the Transvaal border protesting against the £3 poll tax and invalidation of non-Christian marriages. |
| **1914** | **Gandhi-Smuts Agreement** | General Jan Smuts agreed to abolish the £3 poll tax, validate Indian marriages, and treat Indians with greater administrative leniency. |
| **9 Jan 1915** | **Return to India** | Returned permanently to India at Apollo Bunder, Bombay (commemorated as **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**). |

### 2. Philosophical Evolution & Literary Influences

*Key authors and concepts that shaped Gandhi's worldview in South Africa:*

- **John Ruskin (*Unto This Last*)**: Taught that the good of the individual is contained in the good of all, and that the life of a laborer/craftsman is the only life worth living (led directly to the founding of **Phoenix Settlement** in 1904).
- **Leo Tolstoy (*The Kingdom of God is Within You*)**: Deepened his conviction in non-violence, love as a law of life, and passive resistance to tyranny.
- **Henry David Thoreau (*Civil Disobedience*)**: Reinforced the moral duty to refuse cooperation with unjust laws and state-mandated oppression.
