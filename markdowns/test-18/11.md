# 📌 The Tripartite Struggle over Kannauj (8th–10th Century)

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Correct Answer**: **The Cholas** (Did NOT participate).
- **The Three Contenders**: The struggle lasted nearly two centuries (8th to 10th century CE) between:
  1. **The Gurjara-Pratiharas** (Western India / Rajasthan & Gujarat).
  2. **The Palas** (Eastern India / Bengal & Bihar).
  3. **The Rashtrakutas** (Deccan / Maharashtra & Karnataka).
- **Strategic Prize (Kannauj / Mahodaya)**: Following Harshavardhana's reign, **Kannauj** became the supreme symbol of imperial sovereignty in Northern India, controlling fertile Indo-Gangetic trade routes and the rich agricultural plains.
- **Why the Cholas were not involved**: The Imperial Cholas were based in the **Kaveri delta of Tamil Nadu (South India)**, focusing their expansion across Peninsular India, Sri Lanka, and maritime Southeast Asia (Srivijaya Empire).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Analysis of the Three Contenders

| Dynasty | Base Region & Core Territory | Capital City | Key Rulers Involved | Strategic Motivation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gurjara-Pratiharas** | Western India (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Malwa) | Bhinmal / Avanti $\to$ Kannauj | • **Vatsaraja**<br>• **Nagabhata II**<br>• **Mihir Bhoja** | Control the northern plains; prevent Pala westward expansion. |
| **Palas** | Eastern India (Bengal, Bihar) | Gauda / Pataliputra / Munger | • **Gopala** (Founder)<br>• **Dharmapala**<br>• **Devapala** | Secure riverine trade along the Ganga; establish hegemony as *Uttarapathasvamin*. |
| **Rashtrakutas** | Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, Karnataka) | Manyakheta (Malkhed) | • **Dhruva Dharavarsha**<br>• **Govinda III**<br>• **Indra III** | Project Deccan military supremacy north of the Narmada; collect tribute and prestige. |

---

### 2. Major Phases & Ultimate Outcome

- **Initial Clashes (Late 8th Century)**:
  - Pratihara ruler **Vatsaraja** defeated Pala king **Dharmapala**.
  - Rashtrakuta king **Dhruva** marched north, defeating both Vatsaraja and Dharmapala, but returned south due to domestic succession politics.
- **Pala Ascendancy under Dharmapala**:
  - Dharmapala held a grand *durbar* at Kannauj and placed his nominee **Chakrayudha** on the throne, earning the title ***Uttarapathasvamin*** (Lord of Northern India).
- **Pratihara Consolidation & Final Victory**:
  - **Nagabhata II** defeated Chakrayudha and Dharmapala, officially making **Kannauj the imperial capital of the Gurjara-Pratiharas** (c. 815 CE).
  - Despite later successful raids by Rashtrakuta king **Indra III** (who sacked Kannauj in 916 CE), the **Gurjara-Pratiharas remained the ultimate long-term masters** of the city until their eventual fragmentation into Rajput clans (Chandelas, Paramaras, Gahadavalas, Chauhans).

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Dynasty / Event | Role in History | Common Trap / Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Cholas** | **South Indian & Maritime Power** | Confusing their northern expedition (Rajendra I) with the Tripartite Struggle |
| **The Rashtrakutas** | **Deccan Contender in Tripartite Struggle** | Mistakenly assuming only northern powers participated |
| **Ganga Expedition of Rajendra I** | **11th Century CE (c. 1023 CE)** | Occurred long after the Tripartite Struggle ended |
| **Capital of Pratiharas** | **Kannauj (post-Nagabhata II)** | Confusing it with Ujjain or Manyakheta |
