# 📌 Work-Energy Theorem

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Concept**: The **Work-Energy Theorem** states that the net work done by all forces acting on a body equals the total change in its kinetic energy.
- **Fundamental Formula**:
  
  $$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i = \frac{1}{2}m v^2 - \frac{1}{2}m u^2$$

- **Work Types & Energy Response**:
  - **Positive Work ($W > 0$)**: Kinetic energy increases ($v > u$).
  - **Negative Work ($W < 0$)**: Kinetic energy decreases ($v < u$).
  - **Zero Work ($W = 0$)**: Kinetic energy remains constant ($v = u$).

---

## 📖 Main Concept & Mathematical Derivation

### 1. Proof for Constant Force (Algebraic Method)

From the third equation of motion:

$$v^2 - u^2 = 2as \implies a = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2s}$$

Multiplying both sides by mass ($m$):

$$ma = m\left(\frac{v^2 - u^2}{2s}\right)$$

Since Force $F = ma$:

$$F = \frac{m(v^2 - u^2)}{2s} \implies F \cdot s = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$$

Since Work $W = F \cdot s$:

$$W = \Delta K$$

---

### 2. Proof for Variable Force (Calculus Method)

Kinetic energy is defined as:

$$K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Differentiating $K$ with respect to time ($t$):

$$\frac{dK}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}mv^2\right) = \frac{1}{2}m \cdot 2v \cdot \frac{dv}{dt} = m \cdot \frac{dv}{dt} \cdot v$$

By Newton's Second Law ($F = ma = m \frac{dv}{dt}$):

$$\frac{dK}{dt} = F \cdot v$$

Since velocity $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$:

$$\frac{dK}{dt} = F \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} \implies dK = F \, dx$$

Integrating from initial state $(x_i, K_i)$ to final state $(x_f, K_f)$:

$$\int_{K_i}^{K_f} dK = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F \, dx$$

$$K_f - K_i = W \implies \Delta K = W$$

## 🧠 Exam Booster

| Metric / Parameter | Value / Formula | Key Exam Clue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Applicability Scope** | Valid in all inertial frames | Applies to conservative, non-conservative, internal, and external forces alike |
| **Relation with Linear Momentum ($p$)** | $K = \frac{p^2}{2m}$ | If momentum doubles ($2p$), kinetic energy quadruples ($4K$) |
| **Work Done by Centripetal Force** | **Zero** ($W = 0$) | Force is perpendicular to displacement ($\theta = 90^\circ$), so speed/KE remains constant |
| **SI Unit of Work & Kinetic Energy** | **Joule ($\text{J}$)** or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$ | $1\text{ Joule} = 10^7\text{ ergs}$ |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Parameter | Work Done ($W$) | Momentum Change ($\Delta p$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Theorem** | **Work-Energy Theorem** ($W = \Delta K$) | **Impulse-Momentum Theorem** ($I = \Delta p$) |
| **Core Equation** | $W = \int F \, dx$ | $\Delta p = \int F \, dt$ |
| **Integrated Over** | Position / Displacement ($x$) | Time ($t$) |

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

According to the Work-Energy Theorem, the net work done by the forces acting on a body is equal to the change in its:

- [ ] Potential energy
- [x] Kinetic energy
- [ ] Linear momentum
- [ ] Acceleration

> The Work-Energy Theorem explicitly states $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$.

---

### PYQ 2

If a vehicle of mass $1000\text{ kg}$ accelerates from rest ($0\text{ m/s}$) to a speed of $20\text{ m/s}$, what is the total work done on the vehicle?

- [ ] $100\text{ kJ}$
- [x] $200\text{ kJ}$
- [ ] $400\text{ kJ}$
- [ ] $800\text{ kJ}$

> $W = \Delta K = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2) = \frac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times (20^2 - 0) = 500 \times 400 = 200,000\text{ J} = 200\text{ kJ}$.

---

### PYQ 3

A constant stopping force brings a moving car to rest. The work done by the stopping force is:

- [ ] Positive
- [x] Negative
- [ ] Zero
- [ ] Infinite

> Since the final velocity $v = 0$, $K_f = 0$. $\Delta K = 0 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2 = -\frac{1}{2}mu^2$. Negative work reduces kinetic energy.

---

### PYQ 4

If the linear momentum of a moving body is doubled while its mass remains constant, its kinetic energy will:

- [ ] Double
- [ ] Triple
- [x] Become 4 times
- [ ] Remain the same

> Since $K = \frac{p^2}{2m}$, if $p \rightarrow 2p$, then $K' = \frac{(2p)^2}{2m} = 4 \left(\frac{p^2}{2m}\right) = 4K$.

---

### PYQ 5

A satellite revolves around the Earth in a circular orbit. What is the work done by the gravitational force on the satellite in one complete revolution?

- [ ] Maximum positive work
- [ ] Minimum negative work
- [x] Zero
- [ ] Depends on the mass of the satellite

> The gravitational force acts as a centripetal force, which is perpendicular to the orbital displacement ($\theta = 90^\circ$). $W = F \cdot s \cdot \cos(90^\circ) = 0$.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

A force $F = (3x^2 + 2x)\text{ N}$ acts on a particle of mass $2\text{ kg}$, displacing it from $x = 0$ to $x = 2\text{ m}$. What is the change in the particle's kinetic energy?

- [ ] $8\text{ J}$
- [x] $12\text{ J}$
- [ ] $16\text{ J}$
- [ ] $20\text{ J}$

> $\Delta K = W = \int_0^2 (3x^2 + 2x) \, dx = \left[ x^3 + x^2 \right]_0^2 = (2^3 + 2^2) - 0 = 8 + 4 = 12\text{ J}$.

---

### Q2

A bullet of mass $20\text{ g}$ ($0.02\text{ kg}$) enters a wooden block with a speed of $100\text{ m/s}$ and emerges with a speed of $20\text{ m/s}$. How much work was done by the resistive force of the wood?

- [ ] $-80\text{ J}$
- [x] $-96\text{ J}$
- [ ] $-100\text{ J}$
- [ ] $-120\text{ J}$

> $W = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2) = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.02 \times (20^2 - 100^2) = 0.01 \times (400 - 10000) = 0.01 \times (-9600) = -96\text{ J}$.

---

### Q3

Which of the following forces can be included when applying the Work-Energy Theorem ($\sum W = \Delta K$)?

- [ ] Only conservative forces
- [ ] Only non-conservative forces
- [ ] Only external forces
- [x] All forces (conservative, non-conservative, internal, and external)

> The Work-Energy Theorem is universal: the net work done by **ALL** forces acting on a system equals the change in its kinetic energy.

---

### Q4

If the kinetic energy of a body increases by $300\%$, by what percentage does its linear momentum increase?

- [ ] $50\%$
- [x] $100\%$
- [ ] $150\%$
- [ ] $200\%$

> $K_f = K + 3K = 4K$. Since $p \propto \sqrt{K}$, $p_f = \sqrt{4} \, p = 2p$. An increase from $p$ to $2p$ represents a $100\%$ increase.

---

### Q5

A block of mass $m$ slides down a rough inclined plane of vertical height $h$. If it reaches the bottom with a velocity $v$, what is the work done by the friction force ($W_f$)?

- [ ] $mgh + \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
- [x] $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 - mgh$
- [ ] $mgh - \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
- [ ] $-\frac{1}{2}mv^2$

> By Work-Energy Theorem: $W_{\text{gravity}} + W_f = \Delta K \implies mgh + W_f = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - 0 \implies W_f = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - mgh$. (Since $v < \sqrt{2gh}$, $W_f$ is negative).
