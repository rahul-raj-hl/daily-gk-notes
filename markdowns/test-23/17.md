# 📌 Magnetic Classification of Materials

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Categorization**: Based on their behavior in an external magnetic field, materials are classified into three primary types: **Diamagnetic**, **Paramagnetic**, and **Ferromagnetic**.
- **Diamagnetic**: Feebly **repelled** by magnets; move from stronger to weaker parts of a non-uniform field. Magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) is **small and negative**.
- **Paramagnetic**: Feebly **attracted** by magnets; move from weaker to stronger parts of a field. Magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) is **small and positive**.
- **Ferromagnetic**: Strongly **attracted** by magnets; acquire permanent magnetization in the direction of the field. Magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) is **very large and positive**.

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Fundamental Property Comparison Table

| Property | Diamagnetic Materials | Paramagnetic Materials | Ferromagnetic Materials |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action in Magnetic Field** | Feebly repelled by a magnet | Feebly attracted by a magnet | Strongly attracted by a magnet |
| **Direction of Magnetization** | Opposite to applied field | Same direction as applied field | Same direction (very strong) |
| **Movement in Non-Uniform Field** | Stronger to weaker field regions | Weaker to stronger field regions | Weaker to stronger field regions |
| **Magnetic Susceptibility ($\chi$)** | Small & **Negative** ($-1 \le \chi < 0$) | Small & **Positive** ($\chi > 0$) | Very Large & **Positive** ($\chi \gg 100$) |
| **Relative Permeability ($\mu_r$)** | Slightly less than 1 ($\mu_r < 1$) | Slightly greater than 1 ($\mu_r > 1$) | Extremely large ($\mu_r \gg 1$) |
| **Temperature Effect** | **Independent** of temperature | Decreases with temperature rise ($\chi \propto 1/T$) | Decreases above **Curie Temperature** |
| **Atomic Origin** | Paired electrons (no net dipole) | Unpaired electrons (weak dipole) | Domain formation & alignment |
| **Standard Examples** | Bismuth, Copper, Water, Gold, Air | Aluminium, Platinum, Oxygen, Sodium | Iron, Cobalt, Nickel, Gadolinium |

---

### 2. Temperature Dependence & Curie Point

* **Curie's Law** (Paramagnetism): Magnetic susceptibility is inversely proportional to absolute temperature:
  
  $$\chi = \frac{C}{T}$$

* **Curie-Weiss Law** (Ferromagnetism): Above a critical temperature called the **Curie Temperature ($T_c$)**, ferromagnetic materials transition into a **paramagnetic** state:

  $$\chi = \frac{C}{T - T_c} \quad (T > T_c)$$

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Parameter | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic | Ferromagnetic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Susceptibility ($\chi$) Sign** | **Negative** | **Positive** | **Positive (Large)** |
| **Permeability ($\mu_r$) Value** | $0 \le \mu_r < 1$ | $1 < \mu_r < 1 + \epsilon$ | $\mu_r \gg 1000$ |

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

Which of the following types of magnetic materials has a negative magnetic susceptibility ($\chi < 0$)?

- [ ] Paramagnetic
- [x] Diamagnetic
- [ ] Ferromagnetic
- [ ] Ferrimagnetic

> Diamagnetic materials have a negative magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$), meaning they acquire magnetization in the opposite direction of the applied field.

---

### PYQ 2

When a ferromagnetic material is heated above its Curie temperature, it converts into a:

- [ ] Diamagnetic material
- [x] Paramagnetic material
- [ ] Antiferromagnetic material
- [ ] Non-magnetic conductor

> Above the Curie temperature ($T_c$), thermal agitation destroys the domain alignment of ferromagnetic materials, causing them to behave as paramagnetic substances.

---

### PYQ 3

Which of the following substances is feebly repelled when brought near a strong pole of a magnet?

- [ ] Iron
- [ ] Aluminium
- [x] Copper
- [ ] Nickel

> Copper is a diamagnetic substance, meaning it is feebly repelled by magnetic fields. Iron and Nickel are ferromagnetic, whereas Aluminium is paramagnetic.

---

### PYQ 4

The relative magnetic permeability ($\mu_r$) of a diamagnetic substance is:

- [ ] Equal to zero
- [x] Slightly less than 1
- [ ] Slightly greater than 1
- [ ] Much greater than 100

> The relative permeability ($\mu_r = 1 + \chi$) of diamagnetic substances is slightly less than 1 because their susceptibility ($\chi$) is small and negative.

---

### PYQ 5

Which among the following elements shows ferromagnetic properties at room temperature?

- [ ] Copper
- [ ] Aluminium
- [ ] Bismuth
- [x] Cobalt

> Iron, Cobalt, and Nickel are the primary elemental ferromagnetic substances at room temperature.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

Superconductors exhibit perfect diamagnetism and completely expel internal magnetic lines of force. What is this phenomenon called?

- [ ] Peltier Effect
- [x] Meissner Effect
- [ ] Seebeck Effect
- [ ] Zeeman Effect

> The Meissner effect is the complete expulsion of magnetic flux lines from the interior of a superconductor when it transitions into the superconducting state, making it a perfect diamagnet ($\chi = -1$).

---

### Q2

How does the magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) of a paramagnetic material change with an increase in absolute temperature ($T$)?

- [ ] Increases linearly ($ \chi \propto T $)
- [x] Decreases inversely ($ \chi \propto 1/T $)
- [ ] Remains constant regardless of temperature
- [ ] Increases exponentially

> According to Curie's Law ($\chi = C/T$), the magnetic susceptibility of a paramagnetic material is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature.

---

### Q3

Which of the following combinations correctly identifies a **Paramagnetic** liquid and a **Diamagnetic** metal?

- [ ] Water (liquid), Iron (metal)
- [x] Liquid Oxygen (liquid), Bismuth (metal)
- [ ] Mercury (liquid), Nickel (metal)
- [ ] Kerosene (liquid), Aluminium (metal)

> Liquid Oxygen is paramagnetic, while Bismuth is one of the strongest naturally occurring diamagnetic metals.

---

### Q4

Which of the following statements regarding diamagnetic materials is INCORRECT?

- [ ] They move from stronger to weaker parts of a non-uniform magnetic field.
- [ ] Their magnetic susceptibility is independent of temperature.
- [x] They acquire strong magnetization parallel to the external magnetic field.
- [ ] Their relative magnetic permeability is slightly less than 1.

> Diamagnetic materials acquire weak (feeble) magnetization in a direction **opposite** (antiparallel) to the applied magnetic field.

---

### Q5

A rod made of material $X$ aligns itself perpendicular to an external magnetic field when suspended freely between two strong magnetic poles. What type of material is $X$?

- [ ] Paramagnetic
- [x] Diamagnetic
- [ ] Ferromagnetic
- [ ] Ferrimagnetic

> When placed in a magnetic field, a diamagnetic rod experiences maximum repulsion along the field line and rotates to align itself perpendicular to the magnetic field.
