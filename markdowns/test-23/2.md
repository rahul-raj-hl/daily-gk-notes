# 📌 Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti): Samudragupta's Conquests & Heritage

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Primary Subject**: The Allahabad Pillar Inscription detail the **political and military achievements of Gupta Emperor Samudragupta** (reigned c. 335–375 CE).
- **Author & Court Poet**: Composed by **Harisena**, who served as the court poet (*Kaviraja*), *Mahadandanayaka* (Chief Judicial Officer), and *Sandhivigrahika* (Minister of War and Peace) in Samudragupta's court.
- **Style & Language**: Written in **Sanskrit** using the **Champu kavya** style (a combination of prose and verse) in the late **Brahmi script**.
- **Multi-Ruler Monument**: The single pillar contains inscriptions from three distinct historical periods:
  1. **Mauryan Emperor Ashoka** (3rd Century BCE Edicts & Queen's Edict of Kaurwaki).
  2. **Gupta Emperor Samudragupta** (4th Century CE *Prayag Prashasti*).
  3. **Mughal Emperor Jahangir** (17th Century CE Persian inscription by Mir Abdullah).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Comparative Breakdown: Three Inscriptions on the Allahabad Pillar

| Historical Era | Monarch / Ruler | Author / Official | Language & Script | Subject Matter / Core Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mauryan Period** (3rd Cent. BCE) | **Ashoka** | Imperial Edicts | Prakrit / Brahmi | • Schism Edict (warning against division in Sangha).<br>• **Queen's Edict** detailing charitable donations of **Queen Kaurwaki** (mother of Tivara). |
| **Gupta Period** (4th Cent. CE) | **Samudragupta** | **Harisena** | Sanskrit / Brahmi | • **Prayag Prashasti**: Eulogy detailing 5 categories of geopolitical conquests across Aryavarta, Dakshinapatha, and border kingdoms. |
| **Mughal Period** (17th Cent. CE) | **Jahangir** | Mir Abdullah (Kalamdan) | Persian / Nastaliq | • Details Jahangir's accession to the Mughal throne; ordered by Jahangir when he was Prince Salim. |

---

### 2. Geopolitical Categories of Samudragupta's Conquests in Prayag Prashasti

| Category / Region | Policy Adopted by Samudragupta | Geographical / Political Extent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rulers of Aryavarta (North)** | **Prasabhadharana** (Violent uprooting and direct annexation) | 9 rulers of Northern India (e.g., Nagasena, Ganapatinaga) were completely defeated and their territories annexed to the Gupta Empire. |
| **Rulers of Dakshinapatha (South)** | **Grahana-Mokshanugraha** (Capture, release, and restoration) | 12 Southern kings (e.g., King Vishnugopa of Kanchi) were defeated, captured, released, and reinstated as tribute-paying vassals. |
| **Atavika Rajyas (Forest Kingdoms)** | **Paricharakikrita** (Reduced to servitude) | Forest rulers in the Vindhyan region were subdued and compelled to serve the emperor. |
| **Pratyanta Rajyas (Border States)** | **Sarva-karadana & Agyakarana** (Tribute payment & obedience) | Frontier states (Assam/Kamarupa, Nepal, Samatata) paid tribute and obeyed royal decrees. |
| **Foreign / Distant Monarchs** | **Atmanivedana & Kanyopayana** (Self-surrender & matrimonial alliances) | Shakas, Kushanas, and Sinhala ruler (**King Meghavarna** of Sri Lanka) sought alliance. |

## ⚠️ Frequently Confused

| Historical Document / Ruler | Associated Feature / Context | Common Exam Trap / Confusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prayag Prashasti vs. Mehrauli Iron Pillar** | • **Prayag Prashasti**: Samudragupta (Allahabad Pillar).<br>• **Mehrauli Iron Pillar**: Chandragupta II 'Vikramaditya' (Delhi). | Attributing the Mehrauli Iron Pillar to Samudragupta |
| **Harisena vs. Banabhatta** | • **Harisena**: Court poet of **Samudragupta** (Prayag Prashasti).<br>• **Banabhatta**: Court poet of **Harshavardhana** (*Harshacharita*). | Confusing the court poets of the Guptas and Vardhanas |
| **Dakshinapatha Policy vs. Aryavarta Policy** | • **Aryavarta**: Direct annexation (*Prasabhadharana*).<br>• **Dakshinapatha**: Defeat, release, and vassalage (*Grahana-Mokshanugraha*). | Assuming Samudragupta directly annexed Southern Indian kingdoms |

The 'Champu' style of writing used in the Prayag Prashasti refers to a literary composition made of:

- [ ] Pure Prakrit verse
