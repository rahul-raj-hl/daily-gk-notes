# 📌 Sedimentary Rocks: Classification & Formation Mechanisms

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Classification by Formation**: Sedimentary rocks form through **lithification** (compaction + cementation) and are categorized into three main types:
  - **Mechanically Formed (Clastic)**: Sandstone, Conglomerate, Limestone, Shale, Loess.
  - **Organically Formed**: **Geyserite**, Chalk, Limestone, Coal (Peat, Lignite, Bituminous, Anthracite).
  - **Chemically Formed**: Limestone, Halite (Rock Salt), Potash, Gypsum, Chert.
- **Key Categories**:
  - **Arenaceous Rocks**: Sand-rich, coarse-grained, porous, and hard; act as petroleum/groundwater reservoirs (e.g., Sandstone).
  - **Argillaceous Rocks**: Clay-rich, fine-grained, soft, and mostly impermeable (e.g., Shale, Claystone).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Classification of Sedimentary Rocks

| Category | Primary Agent / Source | Key Examples | Key Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanically Formed** *(Clastic)* | Weathering, erosion, and transport by water, wind, or ice | Sandstone, Shale, Conglomerate, Loess, Claystone | Layered/stratified; divided into Arenaceous (sandy) and Argillaceous (clayey) types. |
| **Organically Formed** | Accumulation of plant/animal remains and microbial/biogenic deposits | **Geyserite**, Chalk, Coal, Organic Limestone | Rich in carbon or silica/calcium deposited by organic processes. |
| **Chemically Formed** | Precipitation of minerals directly from water solutions | Halite (Rock Salt), Potash, Gypsum, Chert | Crystalline texture resulting from evaporation/chemical saturation. |

---

### 2. Arenaceous vs. Argillaceous Sedimentary Rocks

* **Arenaceous Rocks**:
  - Composed mostly of **sand grains** (e.g., quartz).
  - Coarser grain size, hard, high porosity, and permeability.
  - Form ideal subterranean **reservoirs for groundwater and petroleum** (e.g., Sandstone).
* **Argillaceous Rocks**:
  - Composed mostly of **fine clay particles**.
  - Soft, fine-grained, low permeability (impermeable cap rocks).
  - Act as seal/cap rocks preventing oil or water migration (e.g., Shale).
