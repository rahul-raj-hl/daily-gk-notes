# 📌 Indian Medical Service (IMS) under British Rule

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Incorrect Statement Identified**: **"Indians were never admitted to IMS"** is FALSE.
- **First Indian Officer in IMS**: **Surjo Kumar Goodeve Chuckerbutty** (*Surjo Coomar Goodeve Chuckerbutty*) became the first Indian native to qualify and join the Indian Medical Service on **24 January 1855**, followed by **Rajendra Chandra Chandra** in January 1858.
- **Origin**: Established initially as a medical department in the **Bengal Presidency in 1764** primarily to care for British East India Company troops and servants.
- **Recruitment**: Recruited health professionals through open **competitive examinations** (held in London).
- **Unification**: The separate Presidency medical services of Bengal, Madras, and Bombay were amalgamated into a single unified **Indian Medical Service (IMS)** after 1857 (officially unified under the Crown).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Evolution and Historical Timeline of the Indian Medical Service (IMS)

| Year / Period | Key Event / Milestone | Operational Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1764** | Formation of Bengal Medical Service | Set up by the British East India Company to serve Company troops and servants in the Bengal Presidency. |
| **1855** | Entry of First Indian into IMS | **Dr. Surjo Kumar Goodeve Chuckerbutty** passed the competitive examination in London and joined the IMS on **24 January 1855**. |
| **1858** | Entry of Second Indian | **Dr. Rajendra Chandra Chandra** joined the service on **27 January 1858**. |
| **Post-1857** | Amalgamation of Presidency Services | Medical services of Bengal, Madras, and Bombay Presidencies were consolidated under the British Crown. |
| **1920s–1930s** | Lee Commission Recommendations (1924) | Pushed for greater "Indianization" of civil and medical services, fixing quotas for native Indian recruitment. |

---

### 2. Characteristics of the Indian Medical Service (IMS)

- **Dual Role (Military & Civil)**: Primarily served as a **military medical corps** for the British Indian Army, but its officers were also deputed to civil hospitals, medical colleges, public health departments, and prison administration.
- **Competitive Examination System**: Selection was based on a rigorous open competitive exam held in Britain, requiring candidates to possess recognized British medical qualifications.
- **Hospitals Served**: Provided healthcare infrastructure to both British military personnel and native Indian civilians across government and civil hospitals.
