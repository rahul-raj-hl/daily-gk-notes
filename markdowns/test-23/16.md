# 📌 Speed of Sound & Environmental Factors

## ⚡ Quick Summary

- **Core Question Answer**: When the pressure of a gas changes while temperature remains constant, the **speed of sound remains unchanged**.
- **Pressure Effect**: Speed of sound is **independent of pressure** at constant temperature because any change in pressure brings about a proportional change in density, keeping the ratio $\frac{P}{\rho}$ constant.
- **Temperature Effect**: Speed of sound increases with an increase in temperature ($\approx +0.61\text{ m/s}$ per $1^\circ\text{C}$ rise in air).
- **Humidity Effect**: Speed of sound is **higher in humid air** than in dry air because humid air (containing water vapor $H_2O$) is less dense than dry air (containing $N_2$ and $O_2$).

---

## 📖 Main Concept

### 1. Speed of Sound Equation & Pressure Independence

The speed of sound ($v$) in an ideal gas is given by Newton-Laplace formula:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}$$

Where:
* $\gamma$ = Adiabatic index / ratio of specific heats ($C_p / C_v$)
* $P$ = Pressure of the gas
* $\rho$ = Density of the gas

According to the Ideal Gas Law ($P = \rho \cdot \frac{R T}{M}$), substituting $P/\rho$ yields:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}}$$

**Conclusion**: At a constant temperature ($T$), if pressure ($P$) increases, density ($\rho$) increases proportionally such that $P/\rho$ remains constant. Hence, **pressure has NO effect on the speed of sound**.

---

### 2. Key Factors Affecting the Speed of Sound

| Factor | Effect on Speed of Sound | Physical Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pressure ($P$)** | **No Effect (Remains Unchanged)** | Ratio $P/\rho$ stays constant at fixed temperature. |
| **Temperature ($T$)** | **Increases** ($v \propto \sqrt{T}$) | Higher thermal energy increases particle kinetic motion ($\Delta v \approx 0.61\text{ m/s per } 1^\circ\text{C}$). |
| **Humidity** | **Increases in humid air** | Molar mass of water vapor ($18\text{ g/mol}$) is lower than air ($29\text{ g/mol}$), making humid air less dense. |
| **Density ($\rho$)** | **Decreases** ($v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}$) | In gases, denser mediums resist sound propagation velocity at constant $P$. |
| **Medium State** | $v_{\text{Solids}} > v_{\text{Liquids}} > v_{\text{Gases}}$ | Higher elasticity ($E$) and rigid molecular packing in solids. |

## 📚 Related Previous Year Questions

### PYQ 1

If the pressure of a gas is doubled at a constant temperature, what will be the effect on the speed of sound in that gas?

- [ ] It becomes double
- [ ] It becomes half
- [x] It remains unchanged
- [ ] It quadruples

> At constant temperature, the ratio of pressure to density remains constant ($P/\rho = \text{Constant}$). Hence, changing pressure has no impact on sound velocity.

---

### PYQ 2

Why does sound travel faster in moist air than in dry air?

- [ ] Moist air has higher pressure than dry air
- [x] Moisture decreases the density of air
- [ ] Moisture increases the elasticity of air
- [ ] Dry air has lower pressure than moist air

> Water vapor replaces heavier nitrogen ($N_2$) and oxygen ($O_2$) molecules, reducing the overall density of air. Since $v \propto 1/\sqrt{\rho}$, sound travels faster in moist air.

---

### PYQ 3

By approximately how much does the speed of sound in air increase for every $1^\circ\text{C}$ rise in temperature?

- [ ] $0.31\text{ m/s}$
- [x] $0.61\text{ m/s}$
- [ ] $1.2\text{ m/s}$
- [ ] $3.3\text{ m/s}$

> The speed of sound in air increases by approximately $0.61\text{ m/s}$ for every $1^\circ\text{C}$ temperature increase ($v_t \approx v_0 + 0.61t$).

---

### PYQ 4

In which of the following media does sound travel with the highest velocity?

- [ ] Air at $20^\circ\text{C}$
- [ ] Water at $20^\circ\text{C}$
- [x] Iron/Steel rod
- [ ] Vacuum

> Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave requiring a medium. It travels fastest in solids due to high elastic modulus, followed by liquids, and slowest in gases.

---

### PYQ 5

Which of the following properties of a sound wave does NOT affect its speed through a given uniform medium?

- [ ] Temperature of medium
- [ ] Humidity of medium
- [x] Frequency of sound wave
- [ ] State of matter of medium

> The speed of sound depends strictly on the physical parameters of the medium (density, elasticity, temperature), not on wave parameters like frequency or amplitude.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1

What happens to the speed of sound when sound waves pass from air into water?

- [ ] Decreases because water is denser
- [x] Increases because water has much higher bulk elasticity
- [ ] Remains the same because frequency stays constant
- [ ] Drops to zero

> Although water is denser than air, its bulk modulus (elasticity) is far greater, causing sound to travel roughly $4\text{ to }5$ times faster in water ($\approx 1480\text{ m/s}$) than in air ($\approx 343\text{ m/s}$).

---

### Q2

If the absolute temperature of an ideal gas is quadrupled (multiplied by 4), how does the speed of sound in that gas change?

- [ ] It doubles
- [x] It doubles ($v \propto \sqrt{T}$)
- [ ] It quadruples
- [ ] It remains unchanged

> Speed of sound is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature ($v \propto \sqrt{T}$). If $T$ becomes $4T$, the velocity becomes $\sqrt{4} = 2$ times the original value.

---

### Q3

Which of the following statements regarding sound propagation is INCORRECT?

- [ ] Sound waves cannot travel through a vacuum.
- [ ] Sound travels faster in solids than in gases.
- [x] Increasing atmospheric pressure increases the speed of sound in air.
- [ ] Sound travels faster on a humid rainy day than on a dry summer day at the same temperature.

> Statement 3 is incorrect because speed of sound is completely independent of pressure changes at constant temperature.

---

### Q4

Calculate the approximate speed of sound in air at a temperature of $30^\circ\text{C}$, assuming speed at $0^\circ\text{C}$ is $332\text{ m/s}$.

- [ ] $332.0\text{ m/s}$
- [ ] $340.3\text{ m/s}$
- [x] $350.3\text{ m/s}$
- [ ] $362.5\text{ m/s}$

> Using the formula $v_t = v_0 + (0.61 \times t)$: $v_{30} = 332 + (0.61 \times 30) = 332 + 18.3 = 350.3\text{ m/s}$.

### Q5

During a thunderous storm, a person sees lightning flash first and hears the thunderclap a few seconds later. This phenomenon occurs because:

- [ ] Thunder is produced after lightning occurs
- [ ] Human ears take longer to process acoustics than eyes process light
- [x] Light travels infinitely faster ($\approx 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$) than sound ($\approx 343\text{ m/s}$) in air
- [ ] Sound waves get reflected by clouds causing delay

> Light travels at $3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$ whereas sound travels at around $340\text{ m/s}$ in air. The light from lightning reaches the observer almost instantaneously compared to thunder.
